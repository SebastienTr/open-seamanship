> **In an emergency in French waters**
>
> - **At sea:** send a DSC distress alert if you have one, then a
>   [Mayday on VHF channel 16](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
>   The CROSS answers.
> - **From the shore, by phone:** dial **196** (free, 24 hours a day).
>   **112** also works.
>
> Source: [Service-Public.fr, F33954](../library/fr-service-public-fiches.html),
> checked 26 October 2026.

## Who answers: the CROSS

In France, sea rescue is coordinated by the **CROSS** (*centres régionaux
opérationnels de surveillance et de sauvetage*). They are the French
Maritime Rescue Coordination Centres (MRCC) under the international SAR
convention. They keep a permanent radio and telephone watch, receive
alerts from radios, distress beacons and satellite systems, and direct
search and rescue. They are run by the Ministry of the Sea and work under
the maritime prefect; the watchkeepers are French Navy sailors
([Wikipedia FR, 'Centre régional opérationnel de surveillance et de sauvetage', intro and 'Sauver'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

The CROSS do not own the lifeboats and helicopters. They can call on any
suitable means: Navy, customs, Gendarmerie, air force and civil-defence
ships and aircraft, SNSM lifeboats, local fire brigades and beach rescue
posts, and any ship near the casualty. For medical help they work with the
maritime medical advice centre in Toulouse (CCMM)
([Wikipedia FR, 'Les moyens d'intervention'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

You do not need to know which centre covers you: channel 16 and 196
reach the right one.

## How to reach them

**VHF channel 16** is the first choice at sea. Service-Public.fr says it
plainly: from the shore call 196 or 112; at sea, prefer channel 16
([Service-Public.fr, F33954](../library/fr-service-public-fiches.html)).
A radio call is heard by every boat in range, including the one that may
reach you first, and it does not depend on a phone network.

The CROSS listen on channel 16, on 2182 kHz (MF), and for **DSC** alerts
on channel 70 near the coast (GMDSS sea area A1) and on 2187.5 kHz
further out (area A2)
([Wikipedia FR, 'Fréquences attribuées'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
See [GMDSS and DSC](../run-the-boat/gmdss-and-dsc.html) and
[Using a VHF radio](../run-the-boat/vhf-radio-procedure.html).

A distress alert needs no CROSS number: the DSC button sends it to every
station. This page does not list the CROSS MMSI numbers, because no open
official list was found to check them against; if you want them in your
radio, take them from an official list of coast stations.

**196** is a free short number, in service since November 2014, that
puts any fixed or mobile phone straight through to a CROSS, day and night.
The CROSS can ask phone operators to locate the caller's mobile
([Wikipedia FR, '196 : le numéro pour alerter les CROSS'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
Use it if you see someone in trouble from the coast, or as a back-up
when the VHF is out of reach. Keep the phone in a waterproof pouch.

## What the rules ask of you

Division 240, the French rules for pleasure boats up to 24 m, says
([Division 240](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)):

- Any boat with a fixed or handheld VHF must, when possible, keep a
  permanent listening watch on channel 16 at sea, at the usual steering
  position (art. 240-2.07).
- From 6 miles from shelter, a **fixed VHF** is required (art. 240-2.05).
  From 60 miles, add an **EPIRB** and a waterproof **handheld VHF**; a
  satellite phone is recommended to reach a maritime medical centre or a
  rescue centre at any time (art. 240-2.06).
- A DSC radio must be programmed with the boat's own **MMSI**, and a
  fixed DSC set must send the boat's position in its distress alert
  (art. 240-2.20).
- Anyone sailing alone is advised to wear a lifejacket with a handheld
  VHF attached to it (art. 240-2.03).

Full equipment lists: [France: required safety equipment](france-safety-equipment.html).

## The SNSM: volunteer lifeboats

The **SNSM** (*Société nationale de sauvetage en mer*) is a charity,
recognised as being of public benefit. It was formed in 1967 by merging
the Société centrale de sauvetage des naufragés (founded 1865) and the
Hospitaliers sauveteurs bretons (founded 1873). Its volunteer crews
rescue people at sea free of charge, around mainland France and overseas.
Its lifeboats, based at about 218 stations, go out at the request and
under the authority of the CROSS. Most of its money comes from public
donations and legacies
([Wikipedia FR, 'Société nationale de sauvetage en mer', intro and 'Histoire'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

You never call the SNSM directly in an emergency: call the CROSS, which
sends the lifeboat.

## After the call

- Answer the CROSS's questions: position, number of people, what is
  wrong, what help you need. Stay on the channel they give you.
- Put on lifejackets, prepare [distress signals](../emergency/distress-signals.html)
  and keep the radio on.
- For illness or injury, the CROSS can connect you to the CCMM doctors
  in Toulouse: see [Getting medical advice by radio](../health/getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
- The CROSS also broadcast weather and navigation warnings: see
  [France: marine weather bulletins](france-weather-bulletins.html).
