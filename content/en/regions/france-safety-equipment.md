Division 240 is the part of the French ship-safety regulation (the
*arrêté du 23 novembre 1987*) that sets the conditions of use and the
safety equipment for all pleasure craft up to 24 m (79 ft) hull length,
for personal use or training, at sea. It applies on top of the build rules
(CE marking, see [EU: recreational craft rules](eu-recreational-craft.html))
([Division 240, art. 240-1.01](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).
The latest amendment is the arrêté of 21 May 2026.

## The key idea: distance from a shelter

The kit depends on how far you go from a **shelter** (*abri*): a place on
the coast where the boat and crew can get to safety, by anchoring,
landing or berthing, and leave again without help, given the weather and
sea of the moment and the boat itself
([art. 240-1.02 IV.2](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)). A harbour
that you cannot enter in the present wind and sea is not a shelter today.

| Distance from shelter | Kit level | Article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 2 miles | Basic (*basique*) | 240-2.03 |
| 2 to under 6 miles | Coastal (*côtier*) | 240-2.04 |
| 6 to under 60 miles | Semi-offshore (*semi-hauturier*) | 240-2.05 |
| 60 miles and more | Offshore (*hauturier*) | 240-2.06 |

Each level includes everything in the level before.
Tenders may not go beyond 300 m from shelter; beach craft, small
human-powered craft, boards and personal watercraft have their own limits
in the same table ([art. 240-2.01 §8](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).

## Basic kit: under 2 miles

([Art. 240-2.03](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html))

- For each person, a buoyancy aid or lifejacket of at least **level 50**,
  easy to reach, or a wetsuit or drysuit that protects the torso, floats
  and keeps the wearer warm.
- A waterproof light with at least 6 hours' battery: one torch for the
  boat, or one light per person worn or fixed to their buoyancy aid.
- One or more portable fire extinguishers, as set by the owner's manual
  (or annex 240-A.3).
- A manual bailer (bucket, scoop or hand pump) on boats that are not
  self-draining or that have a cabin.
- A towing point and a towline.
- An anchor line suited to the boat and the area (small boats under 250 kg
  and personal watercraft may be exempted).
- A way to know the day's tide times and coefficients for the area (not
  needed in the Mediterranean).
- Outside territorial waters, fly the national flag.

## Coastal kit: 2 to 6 miles

([Art. 240-2.04](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)) Basic kit, plus:

- A **lifebuoy** (horseshoe or ring) with at least 142 N of buoyancy and a
  light that lasts 6 hours and is visible for 0.5 mile, marked with the
  boat's name and registration ([art. 240-2.17](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).
- A **level 100** buoyancy aid or lifejacket per person. A swimmer who is
  actually wearing a level 50 aid, or a suitable wetsuit or drysuit, does
  not need one.
- **3 red hand flares**.
- A fixed or temporarily fixed **steering compass**, class A or B,
  compensated, lit, readable from the helm, needing no power except for
  its light.
- Up-to-date official **charts** of the area (paper or electronic).
- The **collision regulations** (COLREGs) or a summary, and a document
  describing the **buoyage** of the area.

## Semi-offshore kit: 6 to 60 miles

([Art. 240-2.05](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)) Coastal kit, plus:

- A **level 150** lifejacket for every person on board.
- A **fixed VHF** radio.
- A **liferaft** (or rafts) for everyone on board, of an accepted type
  ([art. 240-2.18](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).
- Gear to fix a position, plot and follow a course; an up-to-date **list
  of lights**; a **logbook**.
- A way to receive **marine weather forecasts** on board.
- A **harness and tether**: one on board motorboats, one per person on
  sailing boats, with jackstays or clip-on points.
- The **first-aid kit** listed in art. 240-2.19: elastic adhesive bandage,
  sterile gauze, waterproof plasters, haemostatic dressing, tape, 10 pairs
  of gloves, hand gel, a survival blanket and chlorhexidine antiseptic.
- A waterproof **searchlight** good enough to find a person in the water
  at night.

## Offshore kit: 60 miles and more

([Art. 240-2.06](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)) Semi-offshore
kit, plus an **EPIRB**, a **waterproof handheld VHF**, and a liferaft of
an offshore type (ISO 9650 type I, class II, or an approved type). A
satellite phone is recommended.

## Rules for everyone

([Art. 240-2.01 and 240-1.03](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html))

- Never exceed the maximum number of people or the recommended load on
  the builder's plate. Babies under 1 year do not count.
- Keep all safety gear in good order, in date and ready. Do not stow it in
  the engine space.
- **Children up to 30 kg** need a **level 100** buoyancy aid at any
  distance from shelter.
- **Kill cord:** on outboard boats with a helm or remote control, and on
  personal watercraft, the kill cord must be clipped to the driver's
  wrist, leg or buoyancy aid whenever the engine runs. Never lengthen it.
  Stop the engine or go to neutral before the driver moves about. Carry a
  second cord where everyone knows it, so the crew can restart and go back
  for the driver.
- The **skipper** (*chef de bord*) checks that the voyage suits the boat,
  that all safety gear is on board, in good order and suited to the
  people, and that it is used when needed.
- With a VHF on board, keep watch on channel 16 when possible
  ([art. 240-2.07](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).
