> **In an emergency in UK waters**
>
> - **At sea:** send a **DSC distress alert**, then a
>   [Mayday on VHF channel 16](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
> - **On the coast, or with only a phone:** dial **999** and ask for the
>   **Coastguard**.
>

## Who answers: HM Coastguard

**His Majesty's Coastguard** (HMCG) is part of the Maritime and Coastguard
Agency (MCA). It starts and coordinates all maritime search and rescue in
the UK Search and Rescue Region, and rescues on cliffs and the shoreline.
It is a civilian service, founded in 1822. It runs the search and rescue
helicopters, and its Joint Rescue Coordination Centre at Fareham
coordinates aircraft. Lifeboats come from the **RNLI** and from
independent lifeboat stations, all under Coastguard coordination
([Wikipedia EN, 'HM Coastguard'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

The Coastguard listens on channel 16 and for DSC alerts. You do not need to
know which centre covers you.

## Calling for help by radio

The MCA's leaflet for small boats sets out the DSC procedure
([MCA, GMDSS VHF DSC procedures for small boat users](../library/mca-gmdss-small-boat-leaflet.html)):

1. **Distress** (grave and imminent danger): press and hold the DSC
   distress button until the alert is sent.
2. When the alert is acknowledged, or after about 15 seconds, give the
   **Mayday call and message on channel 16**: "Mayday, mayday, mayday,
   this is [boat name ×3], [call sign, MMSI]", then "Mayday, [name, call
   sign, MMSI], position, nature of distress, help needed, number of
   people on board, other information, over."
3. **Urgency** (concern for a person or the boat, not yet grave and
   imminent): send a DSC all-ships urgency call naming a working channel,
   then "Pan pan" ×3 on channel 16, and give your message on the working
   channel.
4. **If you hear a DSC distress alert:** stop transmitting, listen on
   channel 16. If the Coastguard has not acknowledged it after about
   5 minutes and you can help, acknowledge by voice and say what help you
   can give. Relay the message to the coast by any means.

## The RNLI

The **Royal National Lifeboat Institution** is a charity founded in 1824
and based in Poole, Dorset. It runs lifeboats around the coasts of the
UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man, and on some inland
waters. It is funded mostly by legacies and donations, and most of its
crews are unpaid volunteers. In 2023 it had 238 lifeboat stations and 432
rescue craft, with about 9,000 lifeboat volunteers. Its **all-weather
lifeboats** are large, self-righting boats with enclosed wheelhouses; its
**inshore lifeboats** are inflatables and RIBs for shallower water close to
the shore. It also runs beach lifeguards
([Wikipedia EN, 'Royal National Lifeboat Institution'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

The RNLI saves lives, not property. It may tow a boat to safety when that
is right, but it does not salvage ships or cargo and does not charge a
salvage fee ([Wikipedia EN, 'Royal National Lifeboat Institution', 'Rescues and lives saved'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
You do not call the RNLI directly: the Coastguard sends the lifeboat.

## Before you go

HM Coastguard's own advice, given when the SafeTrx tracking app closed on
31 December 2025
([GOV.UK news, 4 December 2025](../library/govuk-safetrx-closing-2025.html)):

- Carry a reliable means of calling for help: a VHF/DSC radio, a personal
  locator beacon, or a mobile phone in a waterproof pouch.
- **Tell someone ashore** where you are going and when you expect to be
  back. If you are late, they can call 999 and ask for the Coastguard.
- Always wear a lifejacket on the water, and check your equipment.

**For medical advice at sea**, contact HM Coastguard first, on VHF DSC,
MF DSC or VHF channel 16 (a "Pan pan" call if urgent). The Coastguard
takes the basic details, then puts you through to a doctor of the UK
telemedical advice service and, if needed, helps arrange evacuation by
helicopter or lifeboat. By phone, MGN 623 gives +44 344 382 0026 and
+44 208 312 7386
([MCA, MGN 623 Amendment 1, paras 1.4 and 1.5](../library/mca-mgn-623-tmas.html)).
See [Getting medical advice by radio](../health/getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
For forecasts and warnings, see [UK: weather and safety broadcasts](uk-safety-information.html).
