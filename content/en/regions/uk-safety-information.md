**Maritime Safety Information** (MSI) means navigational and weather
warnings, weather forecasts and other urgent safety messages for ships.
In the UK the **Maritime and Coastguard Agency** (MCA) runs the service.
The **Met Office** writes the forecasts, the **UK Hydrographic Office**
(UKHO) issues the navigational warnings, and **HM Coastguard** broadcasts
them by NAVTEX, VHF and MF radio. Satellite services carry them further
offshore ([MCA, MSI Leaflet, Introduction and section 1](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)).
You are expected to check MSI before and during a passage; broadcasts can
be delayed by search and rescue work
([GOV.UK, 'Maritime safety: weather and navigation'](../library/govuk-maritime-safety-weather.html)).

## What is broadcast

Weather products from the Met Office ([MSI Leaflet, section 3](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)):

| Product | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Inshore Waters Forecast | Coastal waters, by inshore area; new every 6 hours from 0500 UTC, broadcast every 3 hours |
| Strong Wind Warning | Force 6 (22 knots) or more expected in an inshore area, not in the last forecast; valid until the next inshore forecast; shown as "SWW" |
| Gale Warning | Force 8 (34 knots) or more; valid 24 hours unless cancelled |
| Storm Warning | Force 10 (48 knots) or more |
| Shipping Forecast | 31 sea areas around the UK, twice a day, with a 24-hour outlook |
| High Seas Forecast | 13 deep-water areas of the North Atlantic |
| Extended outlook | 3 to 5 days, by NAVTEX |
| Fishing Fleet Forecast | 1 October to 31 March, four areas |

Navigational warnings for UK coastal waters are called **WZ** warnings.
If you see a new danger (a wreck, a light out, a drifting object), report
it through the nearest Coastguard centre
([MSI Leaflet, section 2](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)).

## VHF broadcasts

- HM Coastguard first **announces on channel 16**, then reads the
  broadcast on **channel 62, 63 or 64** (sometimes 10), depending on the
  aerial nearest you. Switch to the channel given.
- VHF covers about **30 miles** offshore.
- **Routine A**, every 12 hours: the full broadcast with gale warnings,
  the new inshore forecast, the new Shipping Forecast and all navigational
  warnings in force.
- **Routine B**, every 3 hours between the A broadcasts: gale warnings,
  the inshore forecast and new navigational warnings.
- Gale, storm and navigation warnings are also broadcast **on receipt**,
  announced by a DSC alert.

([MSI Leaflet, sections 8 to 10](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html))

Routine A times by Coastguard centre (local time), January 2026:

| Centre | Routine A (local time) |
| --- | --- |
| Shetland, Stornoway, Dover, Falmouth | 07:10 and 19:10 |
| Aberdeen, Holyhead, Solent | 07:30 and 19:30 |
| Humber, Milford Haven | 07:50 and 19:50 |
| Belfast | 08:10 and 20:10 |

Routine B follows every 3 hours at the same minutes past the hour (for
example Falmouth: 01:10, 04:10, 10:10, 13:10, 16:10, 22:10). The 13:xx
Routine B carries the new inshore forecast
([MSI Leaflet, Table 5](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)).

## MF broadcasts

MF reaches about **150 miles**. Broadcasts are announced on **2182 kHz**
and read twice a day (local time): Shetland 1770 kHz, Stornoway 1743 kHz
and Falmouth 1880 kHz at 07:10 and 19:10; Aberdeen 2226 kHz at 07:30 and
19:30; Humber 1925 kHz at 07:50 and 19:50; Belfast 1883 kHz at 08:10 and
20:10. They may be repeated 6 hours later if a new gale or storm warning
is issued ([MSI Leaflet, Table 4](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)).

## NAVTEX

NAVTEX prints or displays messages automatically. UK stations cover about
**300 miles** ([MSI Leaflet, sections 5 to 7](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)):

| Station | 518 kHz (international, English) | 490 kHz (national: inshore forecasts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cullercoats | G | U |
| Niton | E | I |
| Portpatrick | O | C |

On 518 kHz, gale warnings and other routine messages go out every
4 hours; Niton, for example, at 0040, 0440, 0840, 1240, 1640 and 2040
UTC, with the Shipping Forecast outlook at 0840 and 2040 UTC. Irish and
Belgian stations (Malin Head, Valentia, Oostende) cover some UK areas too.
Select the station letters for where you are and where you are going. See
[GMDSS and DSC](../run-the-boat/gmdss-and-dsc.html).

## BBC Radio 4

The BBC reads gale warnings, the Shipping Forecast, coastal station
reports and the inshore forecast at **00:48** and **05:34** every day,
and gale warnings with the Shipping Forecast at **17:54** at weekends
(local time), on FM Radio 4 and BBC Sounds. The dedicated long-wave
broadcast ended on 31 March 2024
([MSI Leaflet, section 4, Table 1](../library/mca-msi-leaflet.html)).

## Using it on board

1. Before you sail, note the Routine A and B times and the channel of the
   aerial nearest your route.
2. Keep the VHF on channel 16 and listen for the announcement.
3. Write the forecast down as it is read; use the
   [Beaufort scale](../tables/beaufort-scale.html) and
   [Reading the weather at sea](../run-the-boat/reading-the-weather.html).
4. A strong wind or gale warning is your cue to change the plan early:
   see [Heavy weather](../run-the-boat/heavy-weather.html).
