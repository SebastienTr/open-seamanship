Brittany is the western tip of France, between the Channel and the Bay of
Biscay. It is one of the finest cruising and fishing coasts in Europe and
one of the most demanding. The tides are big, the tidal streams fast, and
the rocks many. The Iroise sea, off Brest, is described as one of the
most dangerous sea areas in Europe, with a long history of wrecks
([Wikipedia FR, 'Mer d'Iroise'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). The old
Breton saying runs: *"Qui voit Molène voit sa peine, qui voit Ouessant
voit son sang, qui voit Sein voit sa fin"*: who sees Molène sees his
sorrow, who sees Ouessant sees his blood, who sees Sein sees his end.

## Tides

The tide here is **semi-diurnal**: two high waters and two low waters a
day. French tables give a **coefficient** for each tide, from 20 (very
small neaps) to 120 (the largest springs); 70 is average
([Wikipedia FR, 'Marée'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

- In the **Iroise**, the range is about 3 m (10 ft) at coefficient 45
  and about 7 m (23 ft) at coefficient 120
  ([Wikipedia FR, 'Mer d'Iroise', 'Océanographie'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
- In the **Bay of Mont-Saint-Michel**, on the border with Normandy, the
  range is the largest in Europe: about 10 m (33 ft) on average, about
  12 m (39 ft) at mean springs and up to 15 m (49 ft) at exceptional springs
  ([Wikipedia FR, 'Baie du Mont-Saint-Michel'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

French rules require you to carry the day's tide times and coefficients
([Division 240, art. 240-2.03](../library/fr-legi-division-240.html)).
Plan every passage on the tide: harbour entrances dry, rocks cover and
uncover, and the streams decide when you can pass. See
[Tides and tidal streams](../run-the-boat/tides-and-tidal-streams.html) and
[Rule of twelfths](../tables/rule-of-twelfths.html).

## The three famous passages

### Raz de Sein

A strait about 8 km (4.3 miles) wide between the Pointe du Raz and the
Île de Sein, at the south of the Iroise. West of Sein, the reefs of the
Chaussée de Sein block the way for more than 20 miles, so the Raz is the
shortest route along the coast. At spring tides of coefficient 100, the
stream runs at about **7 knots** on the flood (towards the north-east) and
**6 knots** on the ebb (towards the south-west). Even a moderate breeze
against the stream raises a very rough sea. Sailing guides advise passing
**at slack water**. Large ships are no longer allowed through. Lights in
the Raz include La Vieille and Tévennec
([Wikipedia FR, 'Raz de Sein'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

### Passage du Fromveur

Between the Molène archipelago and Ouessant (Ushant). Its Breton name is
said to mean "great current". The stream reaches nearly **7 knots** at
mid-tide at springs across the passage, and up to **9 knots** in places.
With wind against tide the sea becomes extremely dangerous. It is marked
by two famous offshore lighthouses, La Jument and Kéréon. A tidal turbine
has been installed in the passage since 2015
([Wikipedia FR, 'Passage du Fromveur'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

### Chenal du Four

The inshore channel between the Pointe Saint-Mathieu and the island of
Béniguet, running north to Le Four lighthouse. It is the usual yacht
route between the Channel and the Biscay coast
([Wikipedia EN, 'Chenal du Four'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). It is
marked by the Saint-Mathieu and Kermorvan lighthouses, and navigation in
it is regulated by an order of the Atlantic maritime prefecture. The
Chenal de la Helle runs parallel, 3 km to the west
([Wikipedia FR, 'Chenal du Four'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
Like the Raz, it is a passage to time with the tide.

**For all three:** plan to arrive near slack water or with a fair tide,
never wind against tide, in good visibility, with a working engine and an
escape plan. See [Planning a passage](../run-the-boat/passage-planning.html)
and [Engine failure near rocks](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html).

## Big ships

Off Ouessant runs a compulsory **traffic separation scheme** for large
ships, set up after many wrecks including the *Amoco Cadiz*. It is watched
by CROSS Corsen, and an emergency towing vessel is stationed at Ouessant
([Wikipedia FR, 'Mer d'Iroise', 'Sauvetage en mer'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
Keep clear of the lanes and keep a good lookout; see
[Rules of the road](../run-the-boat/rules-of-the-road.html).

## Lighthouses

Brittany has more than three quarters of all French lighthouses, and the
Iroise has the densest lights and marks of the French coast
([Wikipedia FR, 'Mer d'Iroise'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). Some are
famous for how they were built:

- **Ar-Men**, on a rock at the western end of the Chaussée de Sein, about
  10 km west of Sein, took from 1867 to 1881 to build. Workers from Sein
  could land only when weather and tide allowed: in the 1869 season that
  meant about 42 hours of actual work. Automated in 1990.
- **La Jument** (1904 to 1911) marks the entrance to the Fromveur.
- **Kéréon** (1907 to 1916) stands in the Fromveur itself; keepers lived
  there until 2004.
- **Le Créac'h** (1863), on Ouessant, is the landfall light that marks the
  entrance to the Channel and is among the most powerful in Europe.

## Rescue

- **North and west coasts**, from Mont-Saint-Michel to Penmarc'h:
  **CROSS Corsen**. **South coast**, from Penmarc'h: **CROSS Étel**
  ([Wikipedia FR, 'Centre régional opérationnel de surveillance et de sauvetage'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
- Call on **VHF channel 16**, or **196** by phone. See
  [France: CROSS, SNSM and calling for help](france-search-and-rescue.html).
- The **SNSM** has volunteer lifeboat stations all round the Iroise: Sein,
  Douarnenez, Camaret, Le Conquet, Portsall, Molène, Ouessant and Audierne,
  among others, each with an all-weather lifeboat of 14.8 to 17.6 m
  (49 to 58 ft) ([Wikipedia FR, 'Mer d'Iroise', 'Sauvetage en mer'](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
