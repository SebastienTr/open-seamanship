The **Recreational Craft Directive** (RCD), Directive 2013/53/EU, sets the
safety and environmental rules that boats, jet skis and their engines
must meet before they are **sold or first used** in the European Union.
It replaced Directive 94/25/EC on **18 January 2016**
([Directive 2013/53/EU, Art. 54 and 56](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
The first directive dates from 1994; a 2003 amendment added personal
watercraft and engine emission limits
([Wikipedia EN, 'Recreational Craft Directive'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

It is a **build and market** rule. It does not tell you what to carry or
how to sail: each country keeps its own navigation rules, as long as they
do not require changes to a conforming boat
([Art. 5](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)). For France, see
[France: required safety equipment (Division 240)](france-safety-equipment.html).

## What it covers

- **Recreational craft** of **2.5 to 24 m** (8 to 79 ft) hull length,
  for sport and leisure, whatever the propulsion.
- **Personal watercraft** (jet skis) under 4 m.
- Propulsion engines, some components, and boats or engines after a
  **major conversion** (for example a change of propulsion, or an engine
  modified to give more than 15 % more power).

([Art. 2 and 3](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html))

**Not covered** for design and construction: racing boats labelled as
such, canoes and kayaks, gondolas and pedalos, surfboards and
sailboards, original historic craft designed before 1950 and their
replicas, commercial passenger boats, submersibles, hovercraft,
hydrofoils, amphibious vehicles, and boats **built for your own use**,
provided you do not sell them within **5 years** of first use
([Art. 2(2)](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).

## Design categories

Every boat gets a **design category**: the wind and sea it is designed to
handle ([Annex I, Part A, 1](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).

| Category | Wind (Beaufort) | Significant wave height |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | over force 8 | over 4 m (13 ft) |
| B | up to force 8 | up to 4 m (13 ft) |
| C | up to force 6 | up to 2 m (6.6 ft) |
| D | up to force 4 | up to 0.3 m (1 ft), occasional waves of 0.5 m (1.6 ft) |

Category A does **not** cover abnormal conditions such as storms,
hurricanes or rogue waves. More in
[Design categories A to D](../know-your-boat/design-categories.html)
and [Beaufort scale](../tables/beaufort-scale.html).

## Marks and papers on a conforming boat

- **Hull identification number**: country code of the builder, builder's
  code, serial number, month and year of building, and model year
  ([Annex I, Part A, 2.1](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
- **Builder's plate**, fixed permanently and separate from the hull
  number, showing the builder, the **CE marking**, the **design
  category**, the **maximum recommended load** and the **number of
  people** the boat was designed for
  ([Annex I, Part A, 2.2; Art. 18](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
  Do not load beyond it.
- **Owner's manual**, in a language the user can understand, with all
  the information needed for safe use. It must state the maximum engine
  power ([Art. 7(7); Annex I, Part A, 2.5 and 4](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
- Engines carry their own identification and CE marking.

## Safety features the boat must have

Some essential requirements that matter to a crew
([Annex I, Part A](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)):

- Designed to reduce the risk of **falling overboard**, with a **means of
  reboarding** that a person in the water can reach or deploy alone (2.3).
- Boats under 6 m that can swamp must have **flotation** to stay afloat
  when swamped; habitable multihulls that can capsize must float upside
  down and have an escape route (3.3, 3.8).
- **Shut-off valves** on through-hulls below the waterline (3.4).
- **Liferaft stowage** for categories A and B, and for C and D boats over
  6 m (3.7).
- Strong points for anchoring, mooring and **towing** (3.9).
- Outboards that cannot start in gear (with small exceptions), and an
  **emergency stop (kill cord)** on tiller outboards (5.1.4, 5.1.6).
- Petrol tanks separate from living spaces; ventilated battery spaces;
  **gas cylinders in a locker** that drains overboard; fire-fighting
  equipment or marked places for it (5.2, 5.3, 5.5, 5.6).
- Navigation lights that comply with the COLREGs when fitted (5.7).
- Any toilet connected to a **holding tank** or treatment system (5.8).

## Buying, building or importing

- A boat placed on the EU market must conform and carry the CE marking.
  Boats that met the old directive and were on the market before
  18 January 2017 may still be sold ([Art. 55](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
- If you **import a boat privately** from outside the EU and the builder
  has not certified it, you must have it assessed by a notified body
  (**post-construction assessment**) before using it
  ([Art. 19(2) and 23](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
- The same applies if you sell a **self-built boat** within 5 years, or
  after a **major conversion**
  ([Art. 19(3) and (4)](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).
