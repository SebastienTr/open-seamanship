Each flag stands for a letter. Flown alone, a letter flag has an urgent or
common meaning, set out in the International Code of Signals. The same
letters can be sent by light, sound or radio
([Pub. 102, p. 21](../library/nga-pub102.html)). A pleasure boat rarely flies
flags to signal, but you may see them on fishing vessels, ships, pilot boats
and divers' boats, and you can fly V, W, N over C or O yourself in an
emergency.

## Single-letter flags

Colours as in the plate of Pub. 102. "Hoist" is the side next to the mast or
halyard; "fly" is the free side.

| Flag | Looks like | Meaning when flown alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A Alfa | White (hoist) and blue, swallow-tailed | I have a diver down; keep well clear at slow speed. |
| B Bravo | Red, swallow-tailed | I am taking in, discharging or carrying dangerous goods.* |
| C Charlie | Horizontal stripes blue, white, red, white, blue | Yes (affirmative).* |
| D Delta | Yellow, blue, yellow horizontal stripes (blue widest) | Keep clear of me; I am manoeuvring with difficulty.* |
| E Echo | Blue over red | I am altering my course to starboard.* |
| F Foxtrot | White with a red diamond | I am disabled; communicate with me. |
| G Golf | Six vertical stripes, yellow and blue | I require a pilot. From fishing vessels close together on the grounds: I am hauling nets.* |
| H Hotel | White (hoist) and red, halved vertically | I have a pilot on board.* |
| I India | Yellow with a black disc | I am altering my course to port.* |
| J Juliett | Blue, white, blue horizontal stripes | I am on fire and have dangerous cargo on board: keep well clear of me; or: I am leaking dangerous cargo. |
| K Kilo | Yellow (hoist) and blue, halved vertically | I wish to communicate with you. |
| L Lima | Yellow and black quarters | You should stop your vessel instantly. |
| M Mike | Blue with a white diagonal cross | My vessel is stopped and making no way through the water. |
| N November | Blue and white chequers | No (negative). Visual or sound only; on the radio say "NO". |
| O Oscar | Red and yellow, divided diagonally | Man overboard. |
| P Papa | Blue with a white square in the centre | In harbour: all persons report on board, the vessel is about to sail. At sea, from a fishing vessel: my nets have come fast on an obstruction. |
| Q Quebec | All yellow | My vessel is "healthy" and I request free pratique (health clearance on arrival). |
| R Romeo | Red with a yellow upright cross | No single-letter meaning. |
| S Sierra | White with a blue square in the centre | I am operating astern propulsion.* |
| T Tango | Vertical stripes red, white, blue | Keep clear of me; I am engaged in pair trawling.* |
| U Uniform | Red and white quarters | You are running into danger. |
| V Victor | White with a red diagonal cross | I require assistance. |
| W Whiskey | Blue border, white band, red centre | I require medical assistance. |
| X X-ray | White with a blue upright cross | Stop carrying out your intentions and watch for my signals. |
| Y Yankee | Yellow and red diagonal stripes | I am dragging my anchor. |
| Z Zulu | Four triangles: yellow (top), black (hoist), red (bottom), blue (fly) | I require a tug. From fishing vessels close together on the grounds: I am shooting nets.* |

Meanings: [Pub. 102, p. 21](../library/nga-pub102.html), in shortened
wording. Flag patterns: the Pub. 102 colour plate and
[International maritime signal flags, wikipedia-en](../library/wikipedia-en.html).
\* When made by sound, these may only be used as the collision regulations
allow; see [Sound signals](../run-the-boat/sound-signals.html).

## Signals for an emergency

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| N over C (November above Charlie) | I am in distress (one of the official distress signals) |
| V | I require assistance (not distress) |
| W | I require medical assistance |
| O | Man overboard |
| Square flag with a ball (or anything like a ball) above or below it | Distress |

Distress signals: [Pub. 102, ch. 4, section 1](../library/nga-pub102.html).
For all distress signals, see [Distress signals](../emergency/distress-signals.html)
and [Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html).

## Letters with numbers

A single letter followed by numbers has a fixed meaning
([Pub. 102, p. 22](../library/nga-pub102.html)):

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| A + 3 figures | Azimuth or bearing |
| C + 3 figures | Course |
| D + 2, 4 or 6 figures | Date |
| G + 4 or 5 figures | Longitude (last 2 figures are minutes) |
| K + 1 figure | I wish to communicate with you by... (method) |
| L + 4 figures | Latitude (first 2 figures are degrees, last 2 minutes) |
| R + figures | Distance in nautical miles |
| S + figures | Speed in knots |
| T + 4 figures | Local time (hours and minutes) |
| V + figures | Speed in km/h |
| Z + 4 figures | UTC (GMT), hours and minutes |

## Other flags in the set

The full set has 26 letter flags, 10 numeral pennants, 3 substitutes and the
answering pennant (red and white vertical stripes). A substitute repeats a flag
already in the same hoist, so one set can spell any group. The answering
pennant also serves as a decimal point
([Pub. 102, ch. 1, section 5 and colour plate](../library/nga-pub102.html)).

Two-letter and three-letter signals (for example "AC: I am abandoning my
vessel") are in the full Code; see the library copy of
[Pub. 102](../library/nga-pub102.html).
