The Beaufort scale turns wind speed into a force number from 0 (calm) to 12
(hurricane force). Admiral Francis Beaufort devised it in 1806 and it was
adopted in 1838 ([Bowditch vol. 1, section 4111](../library/bowditch.html)).
Wind speeds are in knots; see [Unit conversions](unit-conversions.html).

A Beaufort force describes the **mean wind** (averaged over 10 minutes by
convention), not the gusts. Gusts can be much stronger than the force
suggests ([Échelle de Beaufort, wikipedia-fr](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

## The scale

In France, conditions from force 7 are treated as serious, especially for
coastal craft, and the CROSS broadcast a special weather bulletin (BMS) on VHF
([Échelle de Beaufort, wikipedia-fr](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). See
[Heavy weather](../run-the-boat/heavy-weather.html) and
[France: marine weather bulletins](../regions/france-weather-bulletins.html).

Aerographer's Mate Table 3-3. Wave heights: Bowditch section 4121. km/h:

"Storm" and "hurricane" here describe wind strength only. A force 12 wind
does not mean a tropical cyclone.

## Estimating the wind from the sea

With no anemometer, look at the sea, not the swell. Judge the wind-driven
waves: their size, the whitecaps, foam and spray. Then pick the line in the
table that fits best ([Bowditch vol. 1, section 4111](../library/bowditch.html);
[Aerographer's Mate, ch. 3](../library/navedtra-aerographers-mate-1976.html)).

You will **underestimate** the wind when:

- the wind is offshore and you are within sight of land (short fetch);
- the wind has only just sprung up or increased;
- heavy rain is flattening the sea;
- there is ice nearby;
- the wind blows the same way as a strong tide or current.

You will **overestimate** the wind when:

- the wind is dying (the sea is still left over from stronger wind);
- waves are running into shallow water;
- the wind blows against the tide or current (steep, short seas);
- wind waves break on top of a swell.

How long the sea takes to build (Bowditch Table 4111, open sea, unlimited
fetch): a force 5 wind builds the sea to 90 % of its full height in about 12
hours; a force 7 needs about 21 hours and a force 11 about 32 hours. Close to a
coast the sea may never reach full height: with an offshore force 7 wind and
22 NM of fetch, waves stay at about half their full height.

On a dark night, judge the wind by its feel and by its effect on the rigging
and flags. Remember that on a moving boat you feel the apparent wind, not the
true wind.
