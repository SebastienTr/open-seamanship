## What they are

Every recreational boat sold in the European Union since the 1990s must be
built to a set of essential requirements and carry a CE mark. The builder
assigns it a **design category**, A, B, C or D. In the words of the Directive,
these categories "specify ranges of wind force and of significant wave height"
([Directive 2013/53/EU, recital 33](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).

**Significant wave height** is the average height of the highest third of the
waves. Individual waves can be much higher.

## The four categories

The Directive's table ([Annex I, Part A, section 1](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)):

| Category | Wind force (Beaufort) | Significant wave height (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | exceeding 8 | exceeding 4 |
| B | up to, and including, 8 | up to, and including, 4 |
| C | up to, and including, 6 | up to, and including, 2 |
| D | up to, and including, 4 | up to, and including, 0,3 |

And its explanatory notes, quoted:

- **A**: "A recreational craft given design category A is considered to be
  designed for winds that may exceed wind force 8 (Beaufort scale) and
  significant wave height of 4 m and above but excluding abnormal conditions
  such as storm, violent storm, hurricane, tornado and extreme sea conditions
  or rogue waves."
- **B**: "A recreational craft given design category B is considered to be
  designed for a wind force up to, and including, 8 and significant wave
  height up to, and including, 4 m."
- **C**: "A watercraft given design category C is considered to be designed
  for a wind force up to, and including, 6 and significant wave height up to,
  and including, 2 m."
- **D**: "A watercraft given design category D is considered to be designed
  for a wind force up to, and including, 4 and significant wave height up to,
  and including, 0,3 m, with occasional waves of 0,5 m maximum height."

The notes end: "Watercraft in each design category must be designed and
constructed to withstand the parameters in respect of stability, buoyancy, and
other relevant essential requirements listed in this Annex and to have good
handling characteristics."

For scale, in imperial units: 4 m is about 13 ft, 2 m about 6.5 ft, 0.3 m about
1 ft and 0.5 m about 1.6 ft. Force 4 is a moderate breeze, force 6 a strong
breeze and force 8 a gale; see [Beaufort scale](../tables/beaufort-scale.html).

## What the category covers

The category is a **design** limit. It is what the boat was built and tested to
survive: stability and freeboard, buoyancy, and structure suited to those
conditions and to the builder's maximum recommended load
([Annex I, 3.2 and 3.3](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)). For example:

- habitable multihulls that could capsize must stay afloat upside down;
- boats under 6 m that can be swamped in their category must stay afloat
  when swamped (both from [Annex I, 3.3](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)).

## What it does not tell you

- **It is not a licence to go there.** Whether you may take the boat to a given
  distance from shore is a matter of national rules, and of the skipper's
  judgement. See [EU: recreational craft rules](../regions/eu-recreational-craft.html)
  and [France: required safety equipment](../regions/france-safety-equipment.html).
- **It assumes the boat is as built and loaded within limits.** Extra weight
  high up, a heavy dinghy on the davits, open hatches or water in the bilge
  all reduce the safety margin. See
  [Why boats float and stay upright](stability-basics.html).
- **It says nothing about the crew or the gear.** A category A boat with a
  tired crew and a worn rig is not safe in a gale.
- **Category A is not "any weather".** It explicitly excludes storms,
  hurricanes, extreme seas and rogue waves.
- **Conditions can exceed the forecast.** If you plan to the edge of the
  category, a stronger gust or a wind-against-tide sea takes you beyond it.
  Leave a margin. See [Planning a passage](../run-the-boat/passage-planning.html).

An FAO guide for small fishing vessels takes the same view of category C:
suitable for seas up to about 2 m and steady winds of force 6 or less, and the
right level for most small boats fishing in coastal waters
([FAO 2025, p. 14](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).

## Finding your boat's category

Look for the **builder's plate**, fixed separately from the hull number,
usually near the helm or in the cockpit. The Directive requires it to show at
least ([Annex I, 2.2](../library/eu-rcd-2013-53.html)):

1. the manufacturer's name or trade mark and contact address;
2. the CE marking;
3. the design category;
4. the manufacturer's maximum recommended load, not counting full fixed tanks;
5. the number of persons the boat was designed for.

Do not exceed the load or the number of persons on the plate. The owner's
manual that comes with a CE boat gives more detail on the category and the
load.
