## Situation

The boat will sit for weeks or months, especially where it can freeze. Water
left in an engine can freeze, split a block or a heat exchanger and crack
pumps. Salt left inside keeps corroding. Fuel and batteries go stale.

## Do now

**Inboard diesel**

1. **Run the engine** until warm, then stop it. Change the oil and oil filter
   while the oil is warm, so the acids and dirt from the season are not left
   in the engine all winter.
2. **Fresh-water circuit**: make sure it holds the right strength of
   antifreeze for the coldest weather expected. If it holds plain water,
   drain it fully. The Navy rule: drain all fresh water when freezing is
   possible "unless an antifreeze solution is being used"
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 250](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
3. **Raw-water circuit**: close the seacock first
   ([Engineman 3, p. 501](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)). Then
   either drain every low point (pump, heat exchanger, oil cooler, exhaust
   muffler), or draw fresh water and then antifreeze through it, following
   your engine's manual. Salt left inside keeps working.
4. **Fuel**: drain water from the filter bowls; renew the filters now or in
   spring.
5. **Battery**: charge it fully. Then either keep it on a proper charger with a
   float stage, or disconnect it and recharge it every few weeks. A battery
   left discharged deteriorates, and a cold battery has much less cranking
   power ([TM 9-8000, p. 12-6](../library/tm-9-8000-automotive-1985.html);
   [NEETS Module 1, p. 2-30](../library/navedtra-neets.html)). See
   [Batteries](batteries.html).
6. **Seacocks**: close them all if the boat stays afloat; exercise each one so
   it does not seize. Label any that you closed so nobody starts the engine
   with the raw-water cock shut.
7. **Write it down**: put a note on the engine controls listing what you
   drained, removed or closed.

**Outboard motor** ([TM 5-278, para. 9, p. 29](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html))

1. Run it in fresh water (a tank or flushing attachment) if it has been in
   salt water.
2. Drain the fuel from the tank, line and carburettor, or as your motor's
   manual says for an injected motor.
3. Drain water from the gear case and cooling passages, rocking the motor
   upright so all the water runs out. Refill the gear case with fresh lubricant.
4. Take out the spark plugs, put a little clean oil into each cylinder, turn
   the motor over slowly by hand to spread it, and refit the plugs.
5. Store the motor upright, as it sits on the transom. Never lay it with the
   powerhead lower than the leg, or water can run into the cylinders
   ([TM 5-278, para. 5c, p. 16](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).

## Why

Water expands when it freezes, enough to split cast iron. Salt and stale water
inside an engine corrode it from the inside. Antifreeze both stops freezing
and, in proper coolant, carries corrosion inhibitors
([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 175-176](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
Used oil carries acids from combustion, so fresh oil is better company for the
engine over the winter. A boat ashore and under cover dries out and does not
soak up water ([Knight, *Sailing*, ch. IX](../library/knight-sailing.html)).

## Check that it worked

- No water left where it can freeze: drain plugs gave water, then nothing.
- The antifreeze strength is right (check with a coolant tester).
- The note on the controls matches what you did.
- The battery holds its voltage after a few days off charge.

## If that fails, and in spring

Bringing the engine back to work is a "start after a long idle period"
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 247](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)):

1. Read your note. Refit anything removed; close drain cocks; open the
   seacocks.
2. Fill and vent the cooling system; check the coolant level.
3. Check the oil level.
4. Check the fuel tank and filter bowls for water. Bleed the fuel system if
   you opened it. See [Bleeding air from a diesel](../fix-it/bleeding-a-diesel.html).
5. Start, and check at once for oil pressure and water at the exhaust. If no
   water comes, stop and see [Engine overheating](../fix-it/engine-overheating.html).
6. Look under the engine for new leaks after the first run.

If a block or heat exchanger has frozen and split, you will usually see
coolant leaking or water in the oil. Do not run the engine; get it inspected.
