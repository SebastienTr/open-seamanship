## Situation

Someone on board is badly injured, unconscious, not breathing normally,
bleeding heavily, or suddenly very ill (chest pain, fits, severe pain).

## Do now

1. **Make it safe** for you first, then for them: moving gear, fire,
    fumes, the edge of the deck. Slow down or heave to
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-3](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. **Heavy bleeding:** press hard on the wound with a pad and keep
    pressing ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-31](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. **Not breathing, or only gasping:** start CPR, and have someone fetch
    the defibrillator (AED) if there is one. See
    [First aid: what to do first](../health/first-aid-priorities.html).
4. **Unconscious but breathing:** turn them on their side. Nothing by
    mouth ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-1](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. **Call for help by radio** (with a helper, one person does this at
    once while you give first aid):
    - Life in danger now: **[Mayday](mayday-distress-call.html)**.
    - Otherwise **Pan-Pan** on channel 16: *Pan-Pan* ×3, *all stations*
      ×3, *this is* boat name ×3, call sign, MMSI, position,
      **"I require medical advice"**, *over*. The rescue centre will put you
      through to a doctor, free of charge
      ([Pub. 117, 500A](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).
6. **Have ready** for the doctor: boat name, position, where you are
    going, what medicines you carry; the patient's age, pulse, breathing
    rate, temperature, what happened and when, allergies, and anything you
    have already given ([Pub. 117, p. 5-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).
7. **Keep them warm, lying down, and never alone.** Write down times,
    what you see and what you do.
8. **Do what the doctor says**, including turning for port or preparing
    for a helicopter.

## Why

Without breathing, the brain is damaged in 4 to 6 minutes; bleeding from
an artery can kill even faster
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-1](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
After that, a doctor on the radio is your best help: there is no ambulance
at sea.

## Check that it worked

Bleeding controlled, breathing checked, and you are talking to a doctor or
the rescue centre.

## If that fails

- No radio contact: see [Mayday](mayday-distress-call.html), *If that
  fails*.
- Helicopter coming: clear a space on deck, secure loose gear, keep the
  speed and heading they ask for. Do not grab the hook unless the
  helicopter crew asks; then let it touch the deck first (static shock).
  Never tie it to the boat
  ([Pub. 117, 400I](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).
