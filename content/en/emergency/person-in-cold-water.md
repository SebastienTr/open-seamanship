## Situation

Someone is in cold water. Below about 22 °C (72 °F) the body cannot make
heat as fast as the water takes it away
([FM 55-501, 10-27, 10-39](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
This card is for the person in the water and for the rescuers.

## Do now

**If you are in the water:**

1. **The first minutes: keep your mouth out of the water.** You will gasp
    and breathe fast, for up to a few minutes. Lie back in your lifejacket, do not try to swim, and
    wait for your breathing to settle
    ([Wikipedia, 'Cold shock response'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
    [MCA leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)).
2. **Do not swim** to the boat or the shore; it drains heat and strength
    ([MCA, p. 75](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
3. **Keep heat in:** clothes on, cuffs and collar tight, knees up to your
    chest, arms tight to your sides. With others, huddle close
    ([FM 55-501, 10-47, 10-48](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
4. **Back to the waves** to keep spray off your face
    ([MCA, p. 75](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
5. **Be seen and heard:** lifejacket light, whistle, PLB, wave one arm
    ([MCA, p. 75](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
6. **Get onto anything that floats**
    ([FM 55-501, 10-49](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

**If you are rescuing:**

7. Follow [Man overboard](man-overboard.html). Lift them out **lying
    flat**, never by the arms or legs
    ([Wikipedia, 'Man overboard'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
    [MCA, p. 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
8. **Handle gently.** No rubbing, no alcohol, no hot shower
    ([MCA, pp. 75 to 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
9. **Check breathing (and pulse) for up to 1 minute**: in a cold person
    both can be very slow. If plainly not breathing, do not wait out the
    minute: give **5 rescue breaths**, then CPR, 30 compressions to 2
    breaths. **Any pulse at all:** no chest compressions; if not breathing,
    rescue breaths only. Same steps as [Near drowning](../health/near-drowning.html)
    and [Hypothermia](../health/hypothermia.html). Cold people can look dead
    and still be saved ([FM 55-501, 10-27, 10-35](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
10. **Breathing:** on their side, wrapped up, out of the wind, never left
    alone ([MCA, p. 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
    Then [Hypothermia](../health/hypothermia.html).

## Why

Water takes heat from the body about 25 times faster than air
([FM 55-501, 10-49](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)). The
first danger is not cold but **cold shock**: the gasp can pull water into
the lungs ([Wikipedia](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
[FM 55-501, 10-46](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## Check that it worked

In the water: breathing calm, face clear, holding still. On board: lying
down, breathing, wrapped, watched.

## If that fails

Get radio medical advice:
[Getting medical advice by radio](../health/getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
