## Situation

You smell gas (a strong smell is added to LPG so that leaks can be
noticed: [HSE](../library/hse-about-lpg.html)), the gas alarm sounds, or a cooker or heater flame has gone out with the tap on.

## Do now

1. **Put out every flame**: cooker, heater, cigarettes, candles
    ([MGN 312, para 5](../library/mca-mgn-312-lpg.html)).
2. **Turn the gas off at the bottle** (cylinder valve), and every
    appliance tap ([MGN 312, para 5](../library/mca-mgn-312-lpg.html)).
3. **Make no sparks.** Do not switch lights, pumps, fans, radios or the
    engine on **or** off. Do not start the engine
    ([FM 55-501, 11-14](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
4. **Get everyone out** of the cabin onto the open deck.
5. **Air the boat:** open every hatch, door and locker. The gas lies
    **low**, in the bilge and under the floor, so air it from the bottom
    ([HSE](../library/hse-about-lpg.html);
    [FM 4-01.502, p. 4-20](../library/fm-4-01-502-watercraft-safety.html)).
    Bail out the bilge air with a bucket, or pump it with a **hand** pump.
6. **Wait.** Keep everyone out until you can no longer smell gas anywhere,
    including down in the bilge.
7. **Do not look for the leak with a flame**, ever
    ([MGN 312, para 5](../library/mca-mgn-312-lpg.html)).

## Why

LPG (propane and butane) is heavier than air. It sinks and collects in the
lowest part of the boat ([HSE](../library/hse-about-lpg.html)) and can
travel to a spark far from the leak, then flash back
([FM 55-501, 11-14](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)). A hull
holds it like a bowl.

## Check that it worked

No smell anywhere, including the bilge; the gas alarm, if fitted, is quiet.

## If that fails

- Smell still there: keep the gas off, keep airing, call the coastguard
  if you need help.
- Gas burning at a leak: do not put the flame out unless you can shut the
  gas off; unburnt gas can explode
  ([FM 4-01.502, 3-9](../library/fm-4-01-502-watercraft-safety.html)).
  See [Fire on board](fire-on-board.html).
- Headache, dizziness, sleepiness: get to fresh air; see
  [Carbon monoxide poisoning](../health/carbon-monoxide.html)
  ([MCA, p. 27](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- Petrol vapour is also heavier than air and behaves the same way: same
  steps ([FM 55-501, 11-14](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
