## Situation

You hear a new noise (knock, bang, click, rattle, squeal) or feel a new
vibration. Each engine has its own normal sound at each speed and load, so a
change in pitch, tone or vibration is the warning
([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 270-271 and 282](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).

## Do now

1. **Throttle back to idle and take the drive out of gear.** Does the noise or
   vibration stop?
   - *Stops when out of gear:* the cause is in the propeller, shaft or gearbox.
     Go to step 2.
   - *Still there in neutral:* the cause is in the engine or something it
     drives. Go to step 3.
2. **Propeller and shaft.**
   - Rope, net, weed or a plastic bag on the propeller causes sudden heavy
     vibration and loss of speed
     ([TM 55-1030, paras 40-41](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)).
     See [Rope or net round the propeller](fouled-propeller.html).
   - A bent or damaged propeller blade causes vibration and wears the bearings
     and gears ([TM 5-278, para 15 h](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
   - With the engine stopped, look at the shaft coupling and the stern gland.
     See [Leaking stern gland or shaft seal](leaking-stern-gland.html).
3. **Engine in neutral: what kind of noise?**
   - **Heavy pounding or hammering**, louder under load: a mechanical knock
     from a loose, worn or broken part. **Stop the engine.** These usually
     need major repair ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 281](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
     If oil pressure is also low, suspect worn bearings
     ([Engineman 2, p. 464](../library/navpers-engineman-2-1957.html)).
   - **Sharp knocking** from the cylinders (a fuel knock, or detonation):
     **stop the engine** and find out why
     ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 281](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
     Check for water or air in the fuel first
     ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 266](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
   - **Light, fast metallic clicking** from the top of the engine: valve gear
     (valve clearance, a broken valve spring, a sticking valve)
     ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 281](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
     A clicking from the timing-gear end may be a broken gear tooth: stop the
     engine ([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 274-275](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
   - **Rattling:** a loose part, a guard, a bracket or the exhaust; or a failed
     bearing ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 282](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
   - **Squeal or rumble from the front:** a slipping belt, or a worn water pump
     or alternator bearing
     ([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 14](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html)).
   - **Puffing or hissing:** a leaking exhaust manifold or gasket, a loose
     injector or glow plug, or a cylinder head gasket
     ([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 15](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html)).
     Exhaust leaks inside the boat are dangerous: ventilate and fix them.
4. **Find the spot.** With great care near moving parts, press one end of a
   long screwdriver or a metal bar on the engine and the other to your ear. It
   carries the sound and helps you find where it is loudest
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 282](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
5. **Check engine mounts and bolts.** An engine loose on its mounts vibrates
   ([TM 55-1030, para 40](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)). Look for
   broken rubber, loose nuts, or an engine that moves a lot when you put it in
   gear.

## Why

Knocks come from two families. A mechanical knock is metal hitting metal
because something is loose or worn. A combustion knock comes from fuel or oil
burning too early or all at once, with very high pressure; the Navy says to
stop the engine at once to prevent damage
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 281](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)). The
Army table for a small diesel lists injection timing, a faulty injector and
low compression as causes of knocking
([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 13](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html)).
A cold engine also knocks more, because cold cooling water slows ignition
([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 271-272](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).

Every engine and shaft also has speeds where it vibrates strongly (critical
speeds). If the vibration comes only in one narrow band of revs, avoid that
band and pass through it quickly
([Engineman 3, pp. 468-469](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The noise or vibration is gone across the whole range of revs, in and out of
  gear.
- Oil pressure and temperature are normal.

## If that fails

The next causes in the Army tables are valve clearance, a damaged rocker arm,
worn tappets or bent pushrods, a loose flywheel, and carbon in the combustion
chambers ([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 14](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html);
[TM 9-2815-252-24, malfunctions 13-14](../library/tm-9-2815-252-24-dn2m.html)).
These need tools, the engine manual and usually a mechanic.

If you must keep using the engine to reach safety, run it at the lowest revs
that do the job and watch oil pressure and temperature. Do not run an engine
that is pounding. Plan a [tow](../run-the-boat/towing.html) or sail in.
