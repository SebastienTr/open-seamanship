## Situation

A running outboard normally shows a small stream of water from a hole on the
engine casing, often called the tell-tale or pilot stream. It shows that
cooling water is flowing. Fishing-vessel inspectors check this "cooling water
indicator" ([FAO inspection guidelines, p. 41](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).
At low speed the water may come as a spray; at higher speed it is a firmer
stream ([TM 55-1030, para 8](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)).

The stream has stopped, gone weak, or comes out as steam. Or the overheat
alarm has sounded.

## Do now

1. **Stop the engine** as soon as it is safe. The rubber impeller that pumps
   the water is ruined quickly if it runs dry
   ([Wikipedia, "Outboard motor"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)), and the
   engine overheats. If you need the engine a few more minutes to get clear of
   danger, run it at idle only.
2. **Is the water intake under water?** If the engine was tilted up, the boat
   is lifting in waves, or the motor is mounted high, the intake can suck
   air. Lower the engine fully and try again.
3. **Look at the water intake grilles** on the lower unit. Clear weed, a
   plastic bag, mud or sand. A clogged intake screen stops circulation
   ([TM 55-1030, para 30, item 15](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)).
   Tilt the engine up to reach them, engine stopped.
4. **Check the tell-tale hole itself.** It is small and blocks easily with
   salt, sand or insects. Push a thin wire or nylon line into it, then restart
   briefly at idle. If water now flows freely, it was only the tell-tale.
5. **If you ran in shallow water,** sand and mud may have been drawn into the
   pump and the water passages. Sand-filled tubes and a clogged inlet are on
   the 1944 overheating list ([TM 5-278, p. 72](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).

## Why

Most outboards, old and new, cool the engine with outside water, lifted by a
rubber impeller pump in the lower unit
([Wikipedia, "Outboard motor"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Water goes up
through the cylinder jackets and out with the exhaust. Anything that stops it
(a blocked intake, a worn or broken impeller, a blocked or broken pipe)
overheats the engine
([TM 5-278, p. 72](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)). Sand is
the impeller's enemy: abrasive water wears pumps fast
([Engineman 3, pp. 528-529](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).

## Check that it worked

- A steady tell-tale stream within a few seconds of starting, at idle and at
  speed.
- No overheat alarm, no steam, no hot smell.

## If that fails

- **The impeller is worn or broken.** If the intake and tell-tale are clear
  and there is still no water, the impeller is the likely cause. On most
  outboards it sits in the lower unit and changing it means lowering the
  gearcase. That is a job for calm water or the shore, with the right impeller
  kit and the manual. See [Changing a raw-water pump impeller](changing-a-raw-water-impeller.html)
  for the general idea.
- **Overheated already.** If the engine ran hot for a while, it may have
  scored a piston or cylinder ([TM 5-278, pp. 9-10](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
  Check it starts and runs smoothly before you rely on it.
- **Salt deposits and corrosion.** After use in salt water, flush the cooling
  system with fresh water ([TM 5-278, para 9 a](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).


Use oars or a paddle, sail, or ask for a tow while you sort it out. Background:
[How an outboard works](../know-your-boat/how-an-outboard-works.html).
