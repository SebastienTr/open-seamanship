## Situation

There is little or no water from the exhaust, the engine is overheating, and
the seacock and strainer are clear (see [Engine overheating](engine-overheating.html)).
Or it is time for routine replacement. Most small marine diesels and most
outboards use a pump with a rubber impeller: soft vanes that bend against the
pump body to push water. It primes itself, but it is ruined quickly if it
runs without water ([Wikipedia, "Flexible impeller" and "Outboard motor"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

You need: a new impeller and cover gasket (or O-ring) for your pump, a
screwdriver or spanner for the cover screws, a torch, and pliers or an
impeller puller. For outboards, see [Outboard: no cooling-water stream](outboard-no-cooling-water.html).

## Do now

1. **Stop the engine and make sure it cannot be started.** Switch off the
   battery isolator ([TM 9-2815-252-24, p. 3-11](../library/tm-9-2815-252-24-dn2m.html)).
2. **Close the raw-water seacock.** Before you open any part of the sea-water
   side, close the sea connections, or you can flood the boat
   ([Engineman 3, p. 501](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
3. **Put a rag and a tray under the pump.** Some water will come out.
4. **Remove the cover.** Undo the cover screws evenly. Keep the screws in a
   cup: they are small and the bilge is deep. Peel off the old gasket.
5. **Note how the old impeller sits.** Look at which way its vanes are bent
   before you pull it out. Take a photo if you can.
6. **Pull the impeller out.** Grip it with pliers by the hub (not the vanes),
   or use two screwdrivers either side, gently, or a puller. Some impellers
   are held by a screw, a pin, a key or a circlip on the shaft.
7. **Count the vanes.** Are any missing, torn or cracked? **Every missing piece
   must be found.**
8. **Clean the pump body.** Wipe out sand and grit. Look for a deep groove
   worn in the body or the cover: a worn pump pumps less.
9. **Fit the new impeller.** Bend the vanes the same way as the old ones and
   push it onto the shaft, turning it in the pump's normal direction of
   rotation, so the vanes fold the right way. Line up any key, pin or flat.
10. **Fit a new gasket and the cover.** Tighten the screws evenly, a little at
    a time. Do not over-tighten into soft metal.
11. **Open the seacock.** Do not forget this step. Check the cover for leaks.
12. **Start the engine and watch the exhaust.** Water should appear within a
    few seconds. If not, stop at once: a dry impeller is destroyed quickly.

## Why

Most impellers fail from running dry (a closed seacock, a blocked strainer,
the boat lifted out with the engine running) or from age and grit. The Navy
rules for sea-water pumps are the same: do not let the pump run dry, and keep
sand and grit out with a good strainer, because solids caught between the
impeller and the casing break or score it
([Engineman 3, pp. 524 and 528-529](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).

Broken vanes do not vanish. They are carried downstream with the water, into
the pipes, the heat exchanger or the oil cooler, where they block the flow.
Foreign material clogging a cooler greatly cuts its capacity
([Engineman 3, pp. 503-504](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).

## Check that it worked

- A strong, steady stream of water from the exhaust (or the tell-tale on an
  outboard) within seconds of starting.
- Normal engine temperature under load.
- No drips from the pump cover or seal.

## If that fails

- **Pieces of the old impeller missing.** Look in the pump outlet hose first,
  then at the next fitting downstream, often the heat-exchanger inlet end
  cover. Remove the end cover (seacock closed) and pick the pieces out.
- **Still no water.** Check for an air leak on the suction side (loose hose
  clip, cracked strainer lid) that stops the pump priming; check the hose from
  the seacock is not collapsed; check the pump drive belt or coupling.
- **Water leaking from the small hole under the pump body.** The shaft seal is
  worn. Sea water reaching the pump bearings ruins them
  ([Engineman 3, p. 526](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
  Plan a pump overhaul.

**Carry spares.** A spare impeller and gasket are some of the most useful
engine spares on board. Fishing-vessel inspections check the cooling-water
pump and the spare parts carried
([FAO small fishing vessel inspection guidelines, pp. 41-42](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).
