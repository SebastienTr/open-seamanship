## Situation

There is more water than usual in the bilge aft, and it comes from where the
propeller shaft passes through the hull. The seal there is either a
traditional **stuffing box** (a nut or flanged gland squeezing rings of
greased packing round the shaft) or a modern **shaft seal** (a rubber
bellows with a lip or face seal)
([Wikipedia, 'Stuffing box'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Do now

1. **Measure how bad it is.** Pump the bilge dry and note how long it takes to
   fill again. If water is rising faster than you can pump, go to
   [Taking on water](../emergency/taking-on-water.html) now.
2. **Stop the shaft.** Take the engine out of gear. A stopped shaft leaks
   less and lets you work safely. Never put hands near a turning shaft or
   coupling.
3. **Look closely** with a torch. Is the water coming from the gland itself,
   from the hose that joins the gland to the stern tube, from the hose
   clips, or from somewhere else (a cockpit drain, the exhaust, the rudder
   tube)?
4. **Stuffing box dripping too fast:** tighten the gland nuts a little at a
   time, evenly on both sides so the gland stays square (a cocked gland
   leaks). Stop when the drip slows to a slight trickle with the shaft
   turning. **Do not jam it tight**
   ([Machinist's Mate 3, ch. 9](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).
5. **No more adjustment left** (the gland is right up against the box): the
   packing is worn and needs another ring. See "Adding a ring" below.
6. **Hose or clips leaking:** tighten the clips. A split hose here is
   serious; reduce the leak with tape and lashings and get to port.
7. **Modern shaft seal leaking:** check that the bellows is not split, the
   clips are tight, and the seal has not slid along the shaft. If it leaks
   badly and you cannot fix it, wrap it tightly with rags and grease or soap
   and lash it as a stop-gap
   ([FM 55-501, para. 17-4](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)),
   then run the engine only when needed.
8. **Shaft has slid aft** (gap at the coupling, shaft pulled out of the
   seal): stop the engine, do not engage gear, and stop the water first.

### Adding a ring while afloat

The gland can be drawn back along the shaft about 25 mm (1 in) to add one
ring of new packing while the boat is in the water
([Machinist's Mate 3, ch. 5](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).
Expect water to come in while you do it, so have the pump ready and work
fast.

1. Use packing of the right size and type (flax, PTFE or graphite;
   [Wikipedia](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
2. Wrap it once round the shaft and cut it with the ends **square**, leaving
   a small gap for the ring to grow.
3. Push the ring into the box. If you fit more than one, **stagger the
   joints** round the shaft.
4. Refit the gland, draw it up with a spanner to seat the packing, then back
   it off to finger tight. Tighten a little more over the next hours as it
   settles
   ([Machinist's Mate 3, pp. 349-350](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).

## Why

Packing seals by being squeezed against the turning shaft. It needs a little
water to cool and lubricate it. The Navy practice was to tighten the gland to
stop leakage in port, and to slacken it under way just enough for the shaft
to turn easily with a slight trickle of water
([Machinist's Mate 3, p. 155](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).
Too tight, and the packing burns and scores the shaft, which makes the next
leak worse. Old, hard packing, a scored shaft, or a gland pulled up crooked
will leak however tight you make it
([Machinist's Mate 3, p. 349](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).

A clean engine space and a working bilge alarm let you see a leak early
([MCA guide, PDF p. 30](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Check that it worked

- After 15 minutes running, the gland is warm but you can keep your hand on
  it. Hot means too tight: slacken a little.
- Slight drip under way, little or none at rest.
- The bilge stays at its usual level over several hours.

## If that fails

- **Leak you cannot control:** close any seacock you can, use all pumps, and
  head for port or call for help. See
  [Taking on water](../emergency/taking-on-water.html) and
  [Bilge pump not working](bilge-pump-not-working.html).
- **Vibration at the gland:** the shaft or a bearing may be damaged, often
  after a rope round the propeller. See
  [Rope or net round the propeller](fouled-propeller.html) and
  [Knocking, vibration or unusual noise](engine-knocking-or-vibration.html).
- Plan a proper repack or seal replacement at the next haul-out. See
  [Through-hulls and seacocks](../know-your-boat/through-hulls-and-seacocks.html).
