## Situation

You ran over a rope, net, pot line or plastic sheet. The engine stalled,
slowed down, or the boat started to shake and the propeller has little or no
push. This page is about clearing it. If you are drifting towards rocks or a
lee shore, go first to
[Engine failure near rocks or a lee shore](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html).

## Do now

1. **Put the gear in neutral and stop the engine.** A wrap pulls tighter every
   time the shaft turns. It can stall the engine, bend the shaft or tear the
   stern gland.
2. **Check where you are drifting.** Anchor, sail, or call for a tow if you
   have no time to work on it. See [Anchoring](../run-the-boat/anchoring.html)
   and [Towing and being towed](../run-the-boat/towing.html).
3. **Tell the crew what you are going to do** and who does what, before
   anyone leans over the stern
   ([MCA, 'Retrieving gear'](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
4. **Look over the stern.** Find the ends of the rope. Is it still fixed to a
   pot, net or buoy? A line fixed to gear on the seabed can hold the stern
   down or pull a small boat over. Ease the strain or cut the line free from
   the gear first, keeping clear of the bight.
5. **Try from deck first.**
   - With the engine stopped and the key out, pull on the free end while
     someone turns the shaft slowly by hand the opposite way to the wrap.
   - Cut the rope with a sharp knife or saw-edged blade lashed to a boathook.
     Cut close to the propeller and pull pieces away as they come free.
   - Wear a harness clipped on whenever you lean outboard.
6. **Go in the water only as a last resort.** All of these must be true:
   - the engine is stopped, in neutral, and the key is removed or the
     battery isolated so nobody can start it;
   - the boat is safely anchored, moored or in sheltered water, and not
     pitching heavily (a stern dropping onto your head can knock you out);
   - the swimmer wears a lifejacket or buoyancy and a line to the boat, with
     a person on deck holding it and watching all the time;
   - there is a way back on board (ladder, bight of rope, step);
   - the swimmer has thermal protection in cold water.
   **Never go in alone.**
7. **Enter the water slowly.** Let the cold shock pass (the gasping usually
   lasts less than 5 minutes) before putting your face in. Make short dives and come up to
   rest.

## Why

A rope round the shaft or blades works like a brake and a winch at the same
time. It can stop the engine, and it can drag fishing gear or a mooring
towards the hull. Every turn of the shaft makes it tighter and harder to cut,
so stopping the engine early is the single most useful thing you can do.

Cold water is the main danger to the person clearing it. Sudden immersion
causes a gasp and fast breathing that you cannot control. In water colder
than about 15 °C (59 °F), the time you can hold your breath falls from about
a minute to only a few seconds
([Wikipedia, 'Cold shock response'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). A swimmer
under a moving hull with a knife, out of sight of the crew, has very little
margin. This is why the MCA guide asks for a harness when working over the
side and for someone to know what you are doing when you work alone
([MCA guide, pp. 29-31](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

Synthetic ropes give no warning before they break and recoil hard. Keep your
body out of line with a rope under strain
([MCA guide, PDF p. 48](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The shaft turns freely by hand, with the engine stopped, through several
  full turns.
- Start the engine in neutral, then engage ahead gently at low speed. There
  should be normal push and no new vibration.
- Look at the stern gland: no new leak. A wrap can push the shaft or damage
  the seal. See [Leaking stern gland](leaking-stern-gland.html).

## If that fails

- **Cannot clear it:** sail if you can, or anchor and ask for a tow. A
  fouled boat is not in distress unless it is in danger; use a Pan-Pan or
  Mayday only as the situation needs
  ([Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html)).
- **New vibration or knocking after clearing:** a blade or the shaft may be
  bent. Run at low speed only. See
  [Knocking, vibration or unusual noise](engine-knocking-or-vibration.html).
- **Engine overheats after the wrap:** the same rope or a plastic bag may be
  blocking the cooling-water inlet. See
  [Engine overheating](engine-overheating.html).
- **Swimmer in trouble:** pull them back with the line at once. See
  [Person in cold water](../emergency/person-in-cold-water.html) and
  [Hypothermia](../health/hypothermia.html).
- **After a dismasting or with lines in the water:** check that no rope is
  hanging over the side before you start the engine
  ([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
