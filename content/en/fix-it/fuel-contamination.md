## Situation

The engine misses, loses power or stops, and the filter bowl shows water, or
the fuel is dark with black or brown slime or "sludge". Or a filter that was
new a few hours ago is blocked again. Water and dirt are the main cause of
fuel pump and injector trouble
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 266](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).

Water in diesel lets microbes grow where fuel and water meet, making slime
that blocks filters. This is often called "diesel bug"
([Wikipedia, "Microbial contamination of diesel fuel"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Do now

1. **Get the boat safe** if the engine has stopped. See
   [Engine failure near danger](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html).
2. **Drain the water separator.** Open the drain at the bottom of the bowl into
   a clear jar. Water and dirt sink. Drain until clean fuel runs. Some filters
   are built to block completely when water reaches them
   ([Construction Mechanic 3 & 2, pp. 119-120](../library/navedtra-construction-mechanic-3-2-1978.html)).
3. **Look at what came out.**
   - *Clear water, then clean fuel:* drain and go on to step 5.
   - *Black or brown slime, or a lot of dirt:* the tank is contaminated. Change
     the filters (step 4) and plan to clean the tank.
4. **Change the filter elements.** A clogged filter or water separator is an
   early check in the Army table for an engine that will not start
   ([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 3](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html)).
   Change both primary and engine filter if you have spares. Fit new seals. Fill the new filters with clean fuel if you
   can.
5. **Bleed the system.** See [Bleeding air from a diesel fuel system](bleeding-a-diesel.html).
6. **Run gently and watch the bowl.** Sludge left in the tank can block the
   new filter again, especially when the boat is rolling. Keep spare filters
   and a spanner at hand.

**No spare filter?** In an emergency you can wash a blocked element and get
some more running from it. Plug both ends first (corks, not paper) so dirt is
not washed to the clean side. Scrub the outside in clean diesel, and refit
([Engineman 3, pp. 497-498](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
A washed filter is better than none: never run the engine with unfiltered
fuel ([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 267-268](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).

**Bad fuel from one source?** If you can switch to a second tank or a clean
can of fuel, do it, and keep the bad fuel for testing.

## Why

Water ruins the injection system. It corrodes the injection pump, where the
clearances are tiny, and it erodes injector nozzles so they no longer spray
properly. That causes poor combustion and knocking
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 266](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
Dirt held up by a sticky injector valve lets fuel through unburnt and gives
black smoke ([Construction Mechanic 3 & 2, p. 119](../library/navedtra-construction-mechanic-3-2-1978.html)).
Fuel that does not meet its specification also gives hard cold starting, loss
of power, smoky exhaust, rough running and deposits
([Fundamentals of Diesel Engines, p. 10](../library/navpers-16178-fundamentals-of-diesel-engines.html)).

Water gets in by condensation in a part-empty tank, through deck fillers and
vents, and with bad fuel
([Construction Mechanic 3 & 2, p. 120](../library/navedtra-construction-mechanic-3-2-1978.html);
[Wikipedia, "Microbial contamination of diesel fuel"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The filter bowl stays clear after an hour or more of running, including in a
  seaway.
- The engine runs evenly at full power.
- Fuel drawn from the lowest point of the tank is clear and bright, with no
  water layer.

## If that fails

- **Filters keep blocking.** The tank needs cleaning: pump out the bottom of
  the tank, open the inspection hatch if there is one, and wipe out the slime
  and sludge. On ships, fuel is sampled from the tank bottom regularly and the
  water and sediment drained off
  ([Engineman 3, p. 469](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
- **Engine still runs badly with clean fuel.** Water may already have damaged
  the injectors or the injection pump
  ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 266](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
  This is a workshop job.

**Prevention.** Drain the water separator often. Keep the tank full when the
boat is laid up, to leave less air for condensation. Check the deck filler
seal. Before a long trip on an engine that has stood idle, take a sample from
the tank bottom ([Engineman 3, p. 453](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
See [Laying up an engine for winter](../know-your-boat/winterising-an-engine.html)
and [The diesel fuel system](../know-your-boat/diesel-fuel-system.html).
