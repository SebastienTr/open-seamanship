## Situation

The exhaust is smoking more than usual. A healthy diesel under load has an
exhaust with "little or no colour"
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 282](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
Note the colour, when it happens (cold start, idle, full load) and whether it
is getting worse.

If you see **steam with little or no water** from a wet exhaust, that is a
cooling problem: go to [Engine overheating](engine-overheating.html) now.

## Do now

Work on the colour you see.

### Black or dark grey: too much fuel for the air

1. **Ease the throttle.** Black smoke at full load often means the engine is
   overloaded ([Engineman 3, p. 459](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
   Look for rope or weed on the propeller ([Rope or net round the propeller](fouled-propeller.html))
   and a fouled hull.
2. **Air filter and intake.** Check the air filter and that the engine space
   gets enough air. A blocked air intake is step 1 in the Army table
   ([TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunction 11](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html);
   [Engineman 3 & 2, p. 271](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
3. **Exhaust restriction.** A blocked silencer or mixing elbow also
   starves the engine of air
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 272-273](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
4. **Injectors.** A dirty or dribbling injector lets fuel through unburnt and
   makes black smoke
   ([Construction Mechanic 3 & 2, p. 119](../library/navedtra-construction-mechanic-3-2-1978.html)).
   This, and injection timing, are the next steps in both Army tables: a job
   for a mechanic.

### Blue or blue-grey: burning lubricating oil

1. **Oil level.** Too much oil in the sump can make an oil mist that is drawn
   into the air intake on some engines
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 279](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
   Check the dipstick; drain down to the mark if overfilled.
2. **Air intake.** A blocked air intake is step 1 for blue smoke in both Army
   tables ([TM 9-2815-252-24, malfunction 12](../library/tm-9-2815-252-24-dn2m.html)).
3. **Light-load running.** Long hours at very low load leave carbon, stick
   rings and make the engine smoke when load is applied
   ([Engineman 3, pp. 458-459](../library/navpers-engineman-1957.html)).
   A good run under load sometimes clears it.
4. **Wear.** Worn valve guides or seals, or worn rings, let oil into the
   cylinders. Worn rings also give hard starting, loss of power and fumes
   (blowby) from the crankcase breather
   ([Construction Mechanic 3 & 2, p. 119](../library/navedtra-construction-mechanic-3-2-1978.html);
   [TM 9-2815-254-24, malfunctions 8 and 12](../library/tm-9-2815-254-24-isuzu-c240.html)).
   Keep track of how much oil you add.

### White or grey-white: fuel not burning, or water

1. **Cold start.** Some white smoke from a cold diesel that clears as it
   warms is common. Cold cooling water slows ignition and makes rough running
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, pp. 271-272](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
2. **Thermostat.** If the engine never reaches its normal temperature and
   keeps smoking white, the thermostat may be stuck open
   ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 272](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
3. **Water in the fuel.** Check the filter bowl
   ([Water or bug in the diesel](fuel-contamination.html)).
4. **Coolant or sea water in the cylinders.** White smoke that smells sweet
   or steamy, a falling coolant level, or milky oil point to a head gasket or
   a leaking exhaust elbow letting water in
   ([Wikipedia, "Hydrolock"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Stop and get
   help: water in the cylinders can lock the engine.

## Why

Smoke is unburnt or partly burnt fuel or oil. The darker the smoke, the more
unburnt fuel ([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 282](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).
Black smoke means the cylinder got more fuel than its air could burn. Blue
means lubricating oil is reaching the cylinder. Late injection also gives a
smoky exhaust, high exhaust temperatures and loss of power
([Engineman 3 & 2, p. 268](../library/navedtra-engineman-3-2-1979.html)).

## Check that it worked

- Under normal load the exhaust is almost clear once the engine is warm.
- Oil and coolant levels stay steady between checks.

## If that fails

If the smoke persists after the simple checks, the remaining causes are
injectors, injection timing, the injection pump, valve guides and seals, and
worn rings ([TM 9-2815-252-24, malfunctions 11 and 12](../library/tm-9-2815-252-24-dn2m.html)).
These need tools and a mechanic. Heavy smoke with knocking: see
[Knocking, vibration or unusual noise](engine-knocking-or-vibration.html).
