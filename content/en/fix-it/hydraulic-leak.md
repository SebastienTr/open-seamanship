## Situation

There is oil on the deck or in the bilge, a hose is spraying or weeping, a
hauler, winch or crane has gone slow or weak, or hydraulic steering needs
more turns of the wheel than usual. Hydraulic oil under pressure is a hazard
in itself, not only a mess.

## Do now

1. **Stop the machine and make the load safe.** Land any load on deck, put the
   hauler or winch brake on, and make sure gear in the water cannot pull back
   out. A hauler that loses pressure with pots or a net on the line can let
   the gear run, or let a fouled line pull the boat over
   ([MCA guide, PDF pp. 48 and 55](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
2. **Shut off the hydraulic pump** (stop the engine or disengage the pump
   drive) and let the pressure fall before you go near the leak.
3. **Never feel for a leak with your hand.** A jet from a pinhole leak can
   pass through the skin at pressures as low as about 7 bar (100 psi). The
   wound looks tiny but the oil destroys the tissue inside; delay in surgery
   can lead to losing a finger or worse
   ([Wikipedia, 'High pressure injection injury'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
   Look for it with a piece of card or wood held near the hose, and wear
   gloves and eye protection.
4. **Keep oil away from heat and flame.** Mineral hydraulic oil burns, and a
   fine spray onto a hot exhaust can ignite
   ([Fluid Power, ch. 3](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)). Wipe
   up oil on deck: it is very slippery.
5. **Find the leak.** Clean the area with a rag, then run the system briefly at
   low pressure and watch from a safe distance. Common places are hose ends,
   fittings, ram seals and the pump shaft seal.
6. **Fitting leaking:** with the pressure off, tighten the nut a sixth of a
   turn (one flat of the nut), holding the fitting body with a second
   spanner. If it still leaks after a total of a third of a turn, take it
   apart and look for a cracked flare, damaged thread or dirt. **Never
   tighten a fitting while there is pressure in the line**
   ([Fluid Power, ch. 5](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)).
7. **Hose leaking or split:** replace it with a hose of the same pressure
   rating and fittings. If you have no spare, isolate that circuit if your
   system has valves to do so, and use the rest.
8. **Top up the reservoir** to the mark with the right oil
   ([Fluid Power, ch. 7](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)). Do not
   mix types unless you know they are compatible. Keep dirt out: wipe the
   filler before you open it.

## Hydraulic steering

Low fluid or air in hydraulic steering shows as a wheel that turns many times
with little rudder movement. Top up at the helm pump or reservoir, then bleed
out the air as the system's instructions describe
([Fluid Power, ch. 7](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)). If you lose
steering, fit the emergency tiller; see
[Steering failure](steering-failure.html). On many systems the helm unit
works as a hand pump, so the wheel may still steer slowly if the power pump
fails ([FM 55-501, para. 17-1](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## Why

Hydraulic systems carry power as oil under pressure. A small leak at a
fitting usually comes from a loose nut, a damaged flare or dirt on the seal.
Overtightening damages the flare and makes the leak worse, which is why the
manual limits each adjustment to a sixth of a turn. Low oil in the reservoir
lets air into the pump, which makes the system slow, noisy and weak
([Fluid Power, chs 5 and 7](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)).
Before opening any part of the system, relieve the pressure
([Fluid Power, ch. 7](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)).

## Check that it worked

- Run the system at low load first, then working load: no drips at the repair
  after 10 minutes.
- Oil level stays at the mark after use.
- The hauler, winch or steering moves at its normal speed without
  juddering or whining.

## If that fails

- **Cannot stop the leak:** shut down that system and bring gear in by hand
  or with another winch. Do not keep topping up and running: the oil goes
  into the bilge and overboard.
- **Oil spray near the engine and smell of burning:** stop the engine and see
  [Fire on board](../emergency/fire-on-board.html).
- **Skin pierced by an oil jet,** even if it looks like a pinprick: this
  needs a surgeon urgently; delay can cost the finger or hand. Get radio medical advice and head for port.
  See [Getting medical advice by radio](../health/getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
- **Oil in the bilge:** do not pump it overboard; collect it with rags or
  absorbent pads. See [Bilge pump not working](bilge-pump-not-working.html).
