## Situation

You heard a bang, a wire is hanging loose, the mast is bending, or the mast is
already down. Most dismastings start with one failed part of the standing
rigging: a wire, a terminal, a rigging screw or a chainplate
([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Fast action in the
first seconds can save the mast.

## Do now

1. **Shout a warning and get everyone clear of the mast** and of anything that
   could fall. A falling mast can kill
   ([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Everyone on
   deck wears a lifejacket and clips on.
2. **Take the load off the broken side, at once:**
   - **Windward shroud broken:** tack (or gybe if you must, under control)
     so the broken side is to leeward and unloaded.
   - **Forestay broken:** bear away and run downwind, so the wind pushes the
     mast forward against the backstay.
   - **Backstay broken:** head up towards the wind, ease or furl the
     headsail at once (it pulls the mast forward), and sheet the mainsail in
     hard so the mainsheet and leech hold the mast back. Then take a spare
     halyard aft to the stern as a temporary backstay.
3. **Reduce or drop the sails** as soon as the load is off. Do not let them
   flog: a shaking sail jerks the mast and can split
   ([Knight, pp. 375-376](../library/knight-modern-seamanship-1903.html)).
   Start the engine to hold your heading, **but only after checking that no
   rope or wire is in the water** (step 7).
4. **Rig a temporary stay.** Take a spare halyard (or two) to the
   chainplate, toe rail or a strong deck fitting on the damaged side, and
   tighten it with a winch. Use more than one if you can. Then lash or tape
   the broken part so it cannot swing.
5. **Mast down:**
   - Check that nobody is hurt or overboard. See
     [Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html) and
     [First aid: what to do first](../health/first-aid-priorities.html).
   - Look for damage to the hull where the mast or wire hit it.
   - Decide quickly: can you get the mast and sails back on deck and lash
     them, or is the wreckage bashing the hull? Broken spars against a thin
     hull can sink the boat
     ([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
6. **If you must cut it away:** use bolt cutters, a hacksaw, or knock out
   the clevis pins. Keep hands, legs and heads
   out of the bight of every wire under load; wires and synthetic ropes
   recoil violently when they part
   ([MCA guide, PDF p. 48](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
   Tie a line to anything you want to recover before you cut.
7. **Before starting the engine,** walk round the deck and check that no
   line, sheet or wire is trailing in the water. A rope round the propeller
   now leaves you with no sail and no engine
   ([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). See
   [Rope or net round the propeller](fouled-propeller.html).
8. **Tell the coastguard.** If you are in danger, or anyone is hurt, send a
   Mayday; otherwise a Pan-Pan or a call to say you are making for port. See
   [Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).

## Why

The mast stays up only because the shrouds and stays pull on it from every
side. When one part breaks, the sail's force is suddenly not balanced and the
mast bends towards the unsupported side, often within seconds. Turning the
boat so the wind pushes the mast towards the parts that are still sound buys
you time. A sudden shock load, for example from an accidental gybe, is a
common way for worn rigging to fail
([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

After a dismasting the hull is usually still sound, and the boat is still the
best liferaft you have. Do not abandon it unless it is actually sinking
([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). See
[Abandon ship](../emergency/abandon-ship.html) only if it is.

## Check that it worked

- The mast stands straight, sighting up along its track, with no new bend.
- The temporary stay stays tight after 15 minutes of motoring or sailing
  gently.
- No new water in the bilge from where the mast or wreckage struck.

## If that fails

- Motor to port under minimum sail, or none, on the tack that keeps the
  repaired side unloaded.
- **Jury rig:** set a small sail on what is left of the mast, or on a
  spinnaker pole or boom lashed upright and stayed with halyards and lines
  ([Wikipedia, 'Jury rigging'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). See
  [Sails and rigging](../know-your-boat/sails-and-rigging.html) and
  [Ropes and knots](../know-your-boat/ropes-and-knots.html).
- **Taking on water** from the impact: see
  [Taking on water](../emergency/taking-on-water.html).
- Ask for a tow: see [Towing and being towed](../run-the-boat/towing.html).
- Afterwards, have all the rigging checked. Stainless wire can look sound
  outside while inner strands have broken or corroded
  ([Wikipedia, 'Dismasting'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
  [Verrill, ch. VI](../library/verrill-sailboat.html)).
