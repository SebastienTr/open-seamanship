## Situation

You pull the cord or press the start button and the outboard turns over but
will not start, or starts and dies at once. If an electric-start outboard does
not turn at all, check the battery and connections as for an inboard
([Engine won't turn over](engine-wont-crank.html)) and check it is in neutral.

If you are drifting towards danger, anchor first. See
[Engine failure near danger](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html).

A petrol engine needs three things: the right mixture of fuel and air, good
compression, and a strong spark at the right time
([TM 5-278, p. 40](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)). On
outboards, fuel and ignition troubles are the most likely
([TM 55-1030, para 32](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)).

## Do now

1. **Kill cord (safety lanyard).** Is the clip on the stop switch? Many
   outboards will not start without it
   ([Wikipedia, "Outboard motor"](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
2. **Gear lever in neutral**, and the stop button not stuck in.
3. **Fuel tank.** Fuel in it? Tank vent screw on the cap open? With the vent
   closed, fuel stops flowing
   ([TM 5-278, para 14 b and figure 35](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
   Fuel tap open?
4. **Fuel line.** Is the hose connector pushed fully onto the engine and the
   tank? Squeeze the primer bulb (if fitted) until it is firm.
5. **Choke or primer.** A cold engine needs choke or priming; a warm one
   usually does not ([TM 55-1030, para 12](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html)).
6. **Throttle** at the "start" mark.
7. **Pull firmly and steadily**, not with a jerk
   ([TM 5-278, figure 42](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
   Try several times.
8. **If you smell strong petrol, it is flooded.** A mixture that is too rich
   will not fire. Stop choking and priming. The old manuals clear a flooded
   engine by shutting off the fuel at the carburettor and cranking until it
   fires and runs the excess out, then resetting
   ([TM 55-1030, para 12](../library/tm-55-1030-evinrude-1945.html);
   [TM 5-278, para 15 e](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
   On a modern outboard, use the method in its manual (often choke off and
   throttle open while cranking).
9. **Spark plugs.** Take one out. Black, wet or oily? Clean and dry it, or fit
   a new one. Plugs foul from long slow running and too much oil in the fuel
   ([TM 5-278, p. 9](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
   Check the plug cap is pushed firmly on.

## Why

The 1944 trouble chart for outboards lists, for "motor fails to start":
under *ignition*, wrong, fouled or cracked plugs and a wrong gap, loose or
broken wires; under *fuel*, wrongly mixed fuel, a blocked fuel line or
screen, water in the fuel, a stuck float, a blocked jet, and a flooded
carburettor or crankcase
([TM 5-278, section V, pp. 70-72](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
The cheap causes are all on that list. Modern outboards add a kill-cord switch
and, often, an electric fuel pump and computer control, but still need fuel,
air, spark and compression.

## Check that it worked

- The engine starts within a few pulls and runs on with the choke off once
  warm.
- A stream of cooling water comes from the tell-tale outlet. If not, stop it:
  see [Outboard: no cooling-water stream](outboard-no-cooling-water.html).

## If that fails

- **Water or dirt in the fuel.** Look at the fuel filter (on the engine, or in
  the hose) and the bottom of the tank. Drain and clean, or use fresh fuel
  ([TM 5-278, section V](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
- **Wrong two-stroke mix.** Too much or too little oil, or unmixed petrol, makes
  starting hard and fouls plugs
  ([TM 5-278, section V](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
  Use the ratio on your engine.
- **No spark.** With a spark tester, check for a spark at each plug lead.
  No spark points to loose or broken wires, a shorted stop (kill) circuit, or
  a failed ignition unit
  ([TM 5-278, section V](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
  Test away from petrol vapour.
- **Engine dropped overboard.** Do not try to start it until the water is out;
  water left inside for a few hours damages it
  ([TM 5-278, para 15 j](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).
- **Still nothing.** Low compression (worn rings, scored cylinder) needs a
  workshop ([TM 5-278, section V](../library/tm-5-278-outboard-motors-1944.html)).

Use the oars or paddle, or call for a tow. Background:
[How an outboard works](../know-your-boat/how-an-outboard-works.html).
