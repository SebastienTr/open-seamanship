## Situation

The wheel or tiller moves but the rudder does not follow, the wheel spins
freely, or it is jammed hard over. Common causes are a broken cable or wire,
a loose or broken quadrant or tiller arm, low fluid or air in hydraulic
steering, a seized rudder bearing, or a rope or net jamming the rudder
([FM 55-501, ch. 17](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## Do now

1. **Slow down or stop.** Take the engine out of gear if the rudder is jammed
   hard over, or the boat will circle. Keep a lookout.
2. **Check your position and drift.** If you are close to danger, anchor. See
   [Engine failure near rocks or a lee shore](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html)
   and [Anchoring](../run-the-boat/anchoring.html).
3. **Fit the emergency tiller.** Most wheel-steered boats have one that fits
   on top of the rudder stock, under a deck plate aft. On hydraulic systems
   you may need to open a bypass valve or disconnect the ram so the tiller
   can move the rudder
   ([FM 55-501, para. 17-1](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
4. **Steer carefully with it.** A tiller works the opposite way to a wheel:
   to turn to starboard, push the tiller to port. Hold it firmly, especially
   when going astern; the water can throw the rudder over and the tiller can
   sweep a person off the deck
   ([FM 55-501, para. 17-2](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
5. **No tiller, or rudder lost:**
   - **Twin engines:** keep one engine at a steady speed and steer by
     changing the speed of the other
     ([Knight, p. 212](../library/knight-modern-seamanship-1903.html)).
   - **Sailing boat:** balance the boat with the sails. More mainsail and
     less headsail turns her towards the wind; more headsail and less
     mainsail turns her away. Moving crew weight forward or aft also changes
     the balance
     ([Verrill, ch. IV](../library/verrill-sailboat.html);
     [Admiralty Manual, pp. 237-238](../library/admiralty-manual-seamanship-1915.html)).
   - **Drag:** tow a bucket, drogue, or a coil of warp astern. Hauled to one
     quarter, it pulls the stern that way. A drogue also keeps the stern to
     a following sea ([Admiralty Manual, p. 233](../library/admiralty-manual-seamanship-1915.html)).
   - **Sweep:** lash an oar, spinnaker pole or floorboard over the stern as
     a steering oar.
6. **Call for help** if you cannot steer and are drifting towards danger,
   or ask for a tow. See [Towing and being towed](../run-the-boat/towing.html)
   and [Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).

## Find the fault

Once you are safe, trace the system from the wheel to the rudder:

- **Hydraulic:** look at the reservoir or the top helm pump. Low fluid, or a
  wheel that turns many times with little rudder movement, points to a leak
  or air. Find and fix the leak, top up to the mark with the right fluid,
  then bleed out the air
  ([Fluid Power, ch. 7](../library/navedtra-fluid-power-1970.html)). On many
  systems the helm unit itself works as a hand pump if the power pump fails
  ([FM 55-501, para. 17-1](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)). See
  [Hydraulic leak](hydraulic-leak.html).
- **Cable or wire:** look for a broken or jumped wire, a slipped pulley, or a
  sheared clamp or pin at the quadrant. A broken wire can sometimes be
  joined with wire grips or a shackle and re-tensioned.
- **Tiller or quadrant:** check the bolts or key that lock it to the rudder
  stock. If the stock turns inside the quadrant, it has slipped.
- **Jammed rudder:** look over the stern for rope, net or weed. See
  [Rope or net round the propeller](fouled-propeller.html).

When you shift back to the main steering, put the rudder amidships first so
the wheel and rudder match
([Machinist's Mate 3, pp. 510-511](../library/navpers-machinists-mate-3-1958.html)).

## Why

Every steering system has a chain of parts between your hand and the rudder.
The emergency tiller skips that whole chain and moves the rudder stock
directly, which is why the first step is nearly always to fit it. Ships carry
a separate emergency hand pump for the same reason
([Principles of Naval Engineering, p. 523](../library/navpers-principles-naval-engineering-1970.html)).
Without a rudder, anything that pushes or drags one side of the boat more
than the other will turn it.

## Check that it worked

- Small, steady movements of the tiller or wheel give a steady change of
  heading, both ways.
- The rudder angle matches the wheel (rudder indicator or a look over the
  stern).
- Nothing rubs, clicks or slips when you put the helm hard over each way at
  low speed.

## If that fails

- Keep the boat safe first: anchor or heave to and wait for help.
- In rough weather, a drogue astern helps hold the boat end-on to the sea.
  See [Heavy weather](../run-the-boat/heavy-weather.html).
- If the rudder is damaged and the boat is taking water at the rudder tube,
  see [Taking on water](../emergency/taking-on-water.html).
