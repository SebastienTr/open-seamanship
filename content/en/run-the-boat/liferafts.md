A liferaft is what you step into when the boat can no longer keep you
alive. It keeps everyone together, out of the water and wind, and is much
easier to find than people in the sea
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 85](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
In an emergency, go to [Abandon ship](../emergency/abandon-ship.html); once
aboard, see [Surviving in a liferaft](../health/survival-in-a-liferaft.html).

## How it is built

([FM 55-501, paras 10-74 to 10-85, pp. 10-25 to 10-27](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html))

- **Buoyancy tubes** in at least two separate compartments. An approved raft
  still carries its rated number of people with half the compartments
  deflated.
- **Gas inflation** from a cylinder, fired by a sharp pull on the painter
  (the operating cord, about 30 m or 100 ft long on the rafts described).
- **Relief valves** let off extra gas: a hissing sound just after inflation is
  normal. At night the gas cools and you may need to top up with the pump.
- An **inflatable floor** that insulates you from cold water.
- A **canopy** in a bright colour, with lights that switch on by themselves
  and last at least 12 hours, and gutters to catch rain.
- **Water pockets** or a ballast chamber under the floor, which fill as the
  raft lands and make it much harder to capsize.
- Boarding ladders and lifelines inside and out.

## Stowage and float-free

The raft sits in a hard case in a cradle on open deck, "this side up", held
by a strap with a **hydrostatic release unit** (HRU). If the boat sinks
before you can launch, at about 3 to 4.5 m (10 to 15 ft) the HRU frees the
case, it floats up, the pull of the sinking boat on the painter fires the
gas, the raft inflates in about 30 seconds, and a weak link in the painter
breaks to free it ([FM 55-501, paras 10-70 to 10-73 and 10-87, pp. 10-24 and 10-29](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

For that to work ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 85](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)):

- put nothing on top of the raft and nothing in its way;
- keep the painter made fast to the boat;
- service or replace the HRU when due;
- make sure two people can carry the raft to the side if they must launch it
  by hand.

## Launching by hand

([FM 55-501, para 10-86, p. 10-27](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html);
[MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 85 and 88](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html))

1. Check the painter is made fast to a strong point on the boat and clear of
   tangles.
2. Release the strap at the HRU hook. Do not cut or remove the bands round
   the case.
3. Two or more people throw the case into the water, clear of obstructions.
4. Pull the painter out all the way, then give a sharp tug. The raft inflates.
5. Wait for the abandon-ship order before boarding; boarding too early can
   stop the raft inflating fully.
6. Board dry if you can: climb down until you are close, then step or jump
   into the doorway, never onto the roof. From the water, put a foot on the
   ladder, grab the inside lifelines and pull yourself in; do not pull on the
   canopy, it tears ([FM 55-501, paras 10-88 to 10-89, pp. 10-29 to 10-30](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
7. When everyone is in, cut the painter with the knife kept near the
   entrance. Get clear of the boat, but stay reasonably near where she was,
   and stream the drogue
   ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 88-89](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

Two people can lift a helpless person in: kneel on the tube, take them by the
lifejacket and upper arms, dip them down once and use the rebound to bring
them in backwards ([FM 55-501, para 10-90, p. 10-30](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

**If the raft inflates upside down**, pull on the righting strap and move
back as it comes over so it does not land on you
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 85](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## What is inside

Contents depend on the raft's "pack". A raft for ocean-going ships, as
described in FM 55-501, carries a buoyant rescue line, paddles, a pump,
sea anchors, bailers and sponges, first aid kit, torch, mirror and whistle,
parachute and hand flares, food and water, seasickness tablets, a repair kit
with plugs and clamps, and an instruction booklet
([FM 55-501, paras 10-91 to 10-92, pp. 10-31 to 10-33](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
Coastal and leisure rafts carry much less, so keep a grab bag ready
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 85](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)):
handheld VHF, flares, PLB or EPIRB, water, warm clothing. See
[GMDSS and DSC](gmdss-and-dsc.html).

If a helicopter comes, stay in the middle of the raft until you are lifted:
the downdraft has turned rafts over
([Pub. 117, 400I, p. 4-17](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).

## Servicing

A raft's case is sealed, so you cannot inspect inside; servicing is done
ashore at an approved station. In the US a fishing vessel's liferaft and
hydrostatic release must be serviced every year, and disposable releases
replaced by their expiry date ([46 CFR 28.140, Table 28.140](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)).
In the UK, servicing follows MCA notes MGN 548 (SOLAS rafts) and MGN 553
(other rafts) ([UK small fishing vessel code, 7.4.2](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html)).
Learn how your raft launches when you join a boat, and practise the drill
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 85](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Who must carry one (fishing boats)

- **UK**: since MSN 1871 (2017), open fishing vessels from 7 m registered
  length up to 15 m, and decked ones from 7 m, must carry liferafts with room
  for everyone on board; larger decked vessels already had to
  ([MSN 1871, summary, p. 1](../library/mca-msn-1871.html)). The required
  standard rises with distance from a safe haven: beyond 150 miles, SOLAS
  rafts with insulated floor and canopy, float-free, with enough spare
  capacity that losing one raft still leaves room for all
  ([UK small fishing vessel code, 7.4.1](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html)).
- **US**: documented fishing vessels beyond 50 miles from the coast need an
  inflatable liferaft with a SOLAS A pack; between 20 and 50 miles in cold
  waters, a SOLAS B pack; nearer the coast, lesser craft such as buoyant
  apparatus may be allowed. Boats under 10.97 m (36 ft) with three or fewer
  people within 12 miles of the coast are exempt
  ([46 CFR 28.120 and Table 28.120(a)](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)).
- **France and elsewhere**: see the national rules, and for leisure boats
  [France: required safety equipment](../regions/france-safety-equipment.html).
