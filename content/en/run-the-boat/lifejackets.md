## Why wear one

In UK fishing, falling overboard caused 48% of deaths between 2008 and 2018,
ahead of flooding (18%) and capsize (14%). The MCA says many of those who
drowned could have been saved by a personal flotation device (PFD)
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 36](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
Without buoyancy, a person in the water soon becomes too cold and too tired to
stay afloat ([EU guide, p. 53](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)).
People go in by reaching outboard, slipping, being knocked over by a rope or
dragged by gear, by capsize, or by a sea coming aboard (same pages). A PFD
only works if it is being worn when that happens. For what to do in the
water, see [Person in cold water](../emergency/person-in-cold-water.html).

## Lifejacket or working PFD?

Two kinds of device do two different jobs
([MSN 1871, p. 28](../library/mca-msn-1871.html);
[UK small fishing vessel code, 7.6](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html)):

- **The boat's lifejackets** (one per person, with light, whistle and
  reflective tape) are for abandoning ship. They are bulky, but give the most
  support while waiting for rescue.
- **A working PFD** is light enough to wear all the time on deck in case you
  fall in. It may be an inflatable lifejacket, a buoyancy aid or a buoyant
  garment.

If you have to abandon ship and there is time, take off the working PFD and
put on the boat's lifejacket ([MSN 1871, p. 28](../library/mca-msn-1871.html)).

## How much buoyancy

Buoyancy is measured in newtons (N)
([EU guide, Module VI, pp. 137-138](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)):

| Type | Buoyancy | Turns an unconscious person face up? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buoyant waistcoat or body warmer | 50-70 N | No |
| Industrial work vest | up to 100 N | Not reliably; bulky for fishing |
| Buoyant thermal suit | 50-80 N | No; wear a 275 N lifejacket with it |
| Inflatable lifejacket, offshore standard | 150 N | Yes, for an average adult |
| Inflatable lifejacket, heavy duty | 275 N | Yes, even with heavy clothing or tools |

For anyone working alone the MCA and the EU guide recommend a 150 N
automatic inflatable, separate or built into oilskins: it must be able to turn
you on your back with your mouth clear of the water even if you are
unconscious. Buoyant waistcoats and work vests are not enough
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 33](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
See [Fishing alone](single-handed-fishing.html).

## Inflatable or foam

US fishermen who tested PFDs for a month at work found
([NIOSH, PFDs That Work](../library/niosh-pfds-that-work.html)):

| Inflatable | Foam |
| --- | --- |
| Slim and light | Thicker, sometimes heavier, warmer |
| Must be worn over rain gear | Can be worn under rain gear |
| Needs regular checks and re-arming kits | Little maintenance; still floats with small punctures |
| Usually costs a little more | Usually cheaper |

Try several on while doing your normal work. On a crowded deck the PFD must
not snag on gear ([NIOSH](../library/niosh-pfds-that-work.html)). Tuck the
inflation toggle away, and wear the PFD over all other clothing
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 37 and 58](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Choosing an inflatable

From the EU guide ([pp. 137-138](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)):

- **Automatic or manual**: an automatic one inflates when you are in the
  water, even unconscious. A manual one has to be pulled.
- **Soluble or hydrostatic trigger**: a soluble pill can fire if the jacket
  stays damp; a hydrostatic one is not affected by damp but must be serviced
  by the maker.
- **Belt and crotch straps**: unless the belt is tight, the jacket rides up
  over your head. Crotch straps stop it.
- **Gas cylinder**: hard work can loosen it, and then the jacket will not
  inflate. Check it often.
- Buy at least one spare re-arming kit; in remote places the right one may be
  hard to find ([NIOSH](../library/niosh-pfds-that-work.html)).

## Checking it

([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 37](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html))

- Check the gas cylinder is not corroded and is screwed in tight.
- Look for damage to the cover and stitching, mildew, leaks and loose straps.
- Test-inflate it (the MCA suggests every other month) to check the bladder
  holds air; when repacking, close the valve cap and
  check the automatic head.
- Have it serviced as the maker recommends. Dry it in a cool, airy place,
  never in front of a heater.

## Putting it on and going in

Practise putting on your lifejacket until you can do it within 2 minutes
([FM 55-501, para 10-19, p. 10-5](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)),
and your immersion suit unaided in under 2 minutes
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 37](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
If you must jump, use a ladder or rope instead if you can, and avoid jumping
from higher than 4.5 m (15 ft). Fasten the jacket, fold your arms over it,
cover nose and mouth with one hand, look straight ahead and jump feet first,
feet together ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 88-89](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
[FM 55-501, para 10-20, p. 10-6](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
Full steps are in [Abandon ship](../emergency/abandon-ship.html).

## What the rules say

- **UK fishing vessels**: crew must wear a PFD, or a harness and lifeline that
  stops them going overboard, unless the risk of falling in has been removed
  and that is written down in a risk assessment
  ([UK small fishing vessel code, 6.4.1](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html);
  [MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 36](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  The MCA recommends wearing one on open deck at all times anyway.
- **US commercial fishing vessels**: carry an approved PFD, immersion suit or
  exposure suit of the right size for each person, stowed where it can be
  reached from both the work station and the bunk. Immersion or exposure
  suits are required in cold waters, for example on documented vessels
  seaward of the boundary line north of 32° N ([46 CFR 28.110](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)).
- **Elsewhere in Europe**: some countries require everyone on deck on boats
  under 15 m to wear a PFD. Whatever the law, wearing one is the most
  effective single safety measure ([EU guide, p. 53](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)).
  For French leisure boats see
  [France: required safety equipment](../regions/france-safety-equipment.html).
