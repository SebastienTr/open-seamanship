## Situation

A boat has lost power or steering and needs a tow, or you are the one being
towed. Towing looks simple but is dangerous: a towline under load can part
and whip back, and a boat can be pulled over or swamped. If anyone is in
danger, call the coastguard first: see
[Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html) or, for an
urgent but not life-threatening problem, the PAN PAN call in
[Using a VHF radio](vhf-radio-procedure.html).

## Do now

**If you are towing**

1. Agree the plan with the other skipper by radio or voice: which end, which
   line, what speed, what signals, which VHF channel.
2. Check the other boat first: if she is flooding or listing, deal with that
   before towing ([BCH, Ch. 4, B.1.a, p. 4-8](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
3. Use a nylon or polyester line, not natural fibre
   ([BCH, A.4, p. 2-6](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html);
   [FM 55-501, para 19-8, p. 19-1](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
   Fake it on deck so it runs clear.
4. Approach so that your stern and propeller stay clear of the other boat
   and of the line ([BCH, D.3.o, p. 6-75](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
   Pass the towline with a heaving line
   ([BCH, C.5.k, p. 2-35](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
5. Make fast with one round turn and three figure-eights, so it can be eased
   or cast off quickly under load. Never make fast so that it cannot be
   thrown off quickly ([BCH, D.2.h, p. 2-57](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html);
   [FM 55-501, para 19-34, p. 19-9](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
   Keep a knife or axe at hand ([FM 55-501, para 19-23, p. 19-6](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
6. Take up the strain slowly, then build speed gently.
7. Pay out a long towline in open water and adjust it so both boats meet the
   waves together, "in step"; a line that goes slack and snaps tight carries
   much higher loads ([FM 55-501, paras 19-43 to 19-44, p. 19-12](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
8. Keep speed below the towed boat's design speed. A displacement hull cannot
   go much faster than about 1.34 × √(waterline length in feet) knots
   ([BCH, B.7, pp. 3-8 to 3-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)):
   for a 7.3 m (24 ft) waterline that is about 6.5 knots. Slow down more in a
   seaway.
9. Post someone to watch the tow and the line at all times, and to report
   too much strain, the tow sheering about, or failing gear
   ([FM 55-501, para 19-45, p. 19-13](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
10. Before entering harbour, shorten the tow or bring the boat alongside;
    tugs take a tow alongside in harbour and astern outside
    ([FM 55-501, para 19-38, p. 19-11](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

**If you are being towed**

1. Everyone left on board wears a lifejacket
   ([BCH, p. 3-23](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
2. Make the towline fast to the strongest point forward, or to a bridle
   spread to both sides; a bridle reduces yawing and strain on fittings
   ([BCH, Appendix A, 'Bridle', p. A-3](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
   Protect it with chafe gear where it crosses the bow
   ([FM 55-501, paras 19-12 to 19-13, p. 19-2](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
3. Keep everyone as far as possible from the line and its fittings once it
   is loaded ([BCH, A.13, p. 2-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
4. In following seas, a drogue streamed astern helps stop yawing and
   broaching ([BCH, Appendix A, 'Drogue', p. A-7](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
5. Show the lights for a vessel being towed at night. Collisions have
   happened with towed craft showing no lights, including a fishing boat in
   tow after a total electrical failure
   ([BEAmer 2001, section 3](../library/beamer-abordages-peche-commerce-2001.html)).
   Carry a torch if you have no power.

## Why

Nylon stretches and absorbs shocks, but if it parts it snaps back with great
force, in any direction ([BCH, A.10.b, p. 2-8](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html);
[FM 55-501, para 19-40, p. 19-11](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
Do not double the line: a doubled line may be stronger than the fitting,
which then tears out and flies back with the line
([BCH, A.10.b to A.13, pp. 2-8 to 2-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
A long towline with a dip in it (catenary) absorbs shocks; slowing down
deepens the dip ([FM 55-501, para 19-44, p. 19-12](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

**Towing alongside** (the towing boat lashed to the side of the tow, her stern
further aft than the tow's stern) gives the best control in harbours and
narrow water, but not in waves, where the two hulls pitch differently and
lines can part ([FM 55-501, paras 19-4, 19-24 to 19-34, pp. 19-0 to 19-9](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
Use three lines: a spring from the towing boat's bow aft to the tow (it does
the pulling), a bow line, and a stern line, all hauled tight so the two move
as one.

## Check that it worked

- The towline is in step with the waves and never jerks out of the water.
- The tow follows without sheering wildly; no water is coming aboard her.
- Fittings and chafe points are holding; check them every few hours, and
  more often in heavy weather
  ([FM 55-501, para 19-47, p. 19-14](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## If that fails

- **The line starts to part or is under extreme load**: do not run. Drop
  flat on the deck ([FM 55-501, para 19-40, p. 19-11](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
  Never stand in line with a loaded towline or in a bight
  ([BCH, A.13, pp. 2-8 to 2-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html);
  [FM 55-501, para 19-23, p. 19-6](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
- **The tow is swamping or heeling**: slow down or stop. If your own boat is
  being pulled over, release or cut the line: keep a quick release, knife or
  axe ready ([MCA stability guidance, p. 16](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).
- **The towline is round a propeller**: stop that engine; see
  [Rope or net round the propeller](../fix-it/fouled-propeller.html).
- **Conditions are too bad**: anchor the disabled boat if the depth allows
  ([Anchoring](anchoring.html)) and ask the coastguard for help.
