Buoys and beacons mark channels and dangers. The world uses one system, the
**IALA Maritime Buoyage System**, with two regions that differ only in the
colour of the lateral (channel) marks
([Bowditch, section 824](../library/bowditch.html);
[Chart No. 1, Q 130](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)).

## Which region am I in?

| Region | Where | Port-hand marks (left, going in) | Starboard-hand marks (right, going in) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Europe (including France and the UK), Africa, Greenland, Australia, and Asia except the countries in Region B | **Red**, can-shaped | **Green**, cone-shaped |
| **B** | North, Central and South America, Japan, Republic of Korea, Taiwan, the Philippines | **Green**, can-shaped | **Red**, cone-shaped |

Sources: [Chart No. 1, Q 130 and p. 6](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html).
US waters follow Region B, except US possessions west of the International
Date Line and south of 10° N, which follow Region A (Chart No. 1, p. 6).

The **shapes** are the same in both regions: can to port, cone to starboard.
Only the colours swap. In Region B people say "red right returning". In
Region A, red is on your left when you come in from sea. **If you sail from
one region to the other, say the rule out loud before every channel.**

## Which way is "coming in"?

Lateral marks assume you are coming **from seaward**: entering a harbour, an
estuary or a river. Along a coast, the buoyage authority fixes the direction,
usually clockwise round a continent; where it is not obvious, the chart shows
a large open arrow ([Bowditch, section 826](../library/bowditch.html);
[Chart No. 1, Q 130.2](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)). Going out to sea,
everything is reversed: in Region A you leave the red marks to starboard.

## Lateral marks: the sides of the channel

| | Region A | Region B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Port side | Red; can, pillar or spar buoy; red can topmark (if any); red light (if any), any rhythm | Green; can, pillar or spar; green can topmark; green light |
| Starboard side | Green; cone, pillar or spar; green cone topmark (point up); green light | Red; cone, pillar or spar; red cone topmark; red light |
| Preferred channel **to starboard** (the main channel is to your right) | Red with one broad green band; can topmark; red light Fl(2+1) | Green with one broad red band; can topmark; green light Fl(2+1) |
| Preferred channel **to port** | Green with one broad red band; cone topmark; green light Fl(2+1) | Red with one broad green band; cone topmark; red light Fl(2+1) |

Red and green are kept for lateral marks only. A lateral light may have any
rhythm, because its colour says which side to leave it
([Bowditch, sections 825-826](../library/bowditch.html);
[Chart No. 1, Q 130.1](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)). In US waters,
starboard-hand (red) marks carry even numbers and port-hand (green) marks odd
numbers, counting up from seaward (Chart No. 1, p. 6).

## Cardinal marks: the safe side by compass

A cardinal mark tells you **where the safe water is**, by compass direction.
Pass **north of a north mark, east of an east mark, south of a south mark,
west of a west mark**. The marks are the same in Regions A and B
([Bowditch, section 827](../library/bowditch.html)).

| Mark | Topmark (two black cones) | Colours | Light (white) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **North** | Both points **up** | Black above yellow | Continuous quick (Q) or very quick (VQ) flashes |
| **East** | Points **apart** (base to base) | Black, yellow band in the middle | 3 flashes: VQ(3) every 5 s or Q(3) every 10 s |
| **South** | Both points **down** | Yellow above black | 6 flashes plus a long flash: VQ(6)+LFl every 10 s or Q(6)+LFl every 15 s |
| **West** | Points **together** ("wineglass") | Yellow, black band in the middle | 9 flashes: VQ(9) every 10 s or Q(9) every 15 s |

Memory aids: the cone points show where the black is on the buoy. The flash
counts follow a clock face: 3 o'clock east, 6 o'clock south, 9 o'clock west.
The long flash after the south mark's six stops it being read as three or
nine. Quick flashing is 50 to 79 flashes a minute, very quick 80 to 159
([Bowditch, section 827](../library/bowditch.html);
[Chart No. 1, Q 130.3](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)). Cardinal marks are
pillar or spar buoys; the shape has no meaning. They are common in Europe but
seldom used in US waters (Chart No. 1, Q 130.3).

## Other marks

| Mark | Meaning | Colours | Topmark | Light |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Isolated danger** | A small danger with navigable water all round it. Do not pass close: the mark is on top of the danger | Black with one or more red horizontal bands | Two black balls, one above the other | White, group of 2 flashes, Fl(2) |
| **Safe water** | Navigable water all round: landfall, mid-channel or centre-line mark | Red and white **vertical** stripes; spherical, pillar or spar | One red ball | White: isophase, occulting, one long flash every 10 s, or Morse "A" |
| **Special** | A special area or feature shown on the chart: outfalls, cables, spoil ground, military zones, racing or bathing areas, data buoys | Yellow; any shape that does not look like a lateral or safe water mark | Yellow X | Yellow, any rhythm not used by white marks |
| **New danger** | A new danger (often a wreck) not yet on charts or in notices | Yellow and blue | Yellow upright cross | See "Uncertain" |

Memory aids: two balls, two flashes (isolated danger); one ball, one long
flash (safe water). Sources: [Bowditch, sections 828-831](../library/bowditch.html);
[Chart No. 1, Q 130.4 to 130.7](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html).

A new danger may also be marked by cardinal or lateral marks, one of them
doubled until the danger is well known, with quick or very quick lights and
sometimes a radar beacon showing Morse "D" ([Bowditch, section 831](../library/bowditch.html)).

## Using marks safely

- **Buoys move.** A floating mark is moored on a chain and swings round its
  sinker. It can drag, sink, capsize or be carried away, and its light can go
  out. Never rely on a single buoy; check with bearings of fixed objects
  ashore ([Chart No. 1, p. 6](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)). A position
  from buoy bearings is an estimate, not a fix
  ([Fixing your position](position-fixing.html)).
- **Do not cut corners.** The mark shows the edge of the channel, not the edge
  of the danger.
- **At night, read the light before you act.** Time it with a watch:
  colour, number of flashes, period. Compare with the chart legend, for
  example "Q(6)+LFl 15s" ([Reading a nautical chart](reading-a-chart.html)).
- **Lighthouses, sector lights, leading lights and big navigation buoys are
  outside the system.** A shore light may happen to have the same rhythm as a
  buoy; check its position on the chart ([Bowditch, section 825](../library/bowditch.html)).
