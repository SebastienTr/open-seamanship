**If someone is caught in a winch, hauler or rope: hit the emergency stop or
take the load off now, then free them. If someone goes overboard, see
[Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html).**

A fishing deck is crowded, moving, wet and often slippery, with powerful
machinery and ropes under load. Most injuries come from a few hazards that
repeat every haul. Wear a PFD whenever you are on deck: see
[Lifejackets and buoyancy aids](lifejackets.html).

## Ropes, wires and warps under load

- **Never stand in a bight** (loop) of rope or wire, or in line with a loaded
  rope. Do not step over a rope lying on deck: lift it and walk under, or
  step on it and cross; never straddle it ([FM 55-501, p. 19-6](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html);
  [BCH, A.13, p. 2-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
- Synthetic ropes give no warning before they break and recoil violently
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 48](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
  [BCH, A.10.b, p. 2-8](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
- Do not stand on slack warp: if a stopper chain slips it can snap tight and
  throw you up and over the side. Stand clear when the load is transferred
  to the towing chains ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 50](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- A splinter in a wire can catch an oilskin and drag a hand or foot into a
  sheave. Guard warp runs
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 50](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Winches, haulers and net drums

Unguarded drum winches are a known killer. On Gulf of Mexico shrimp boats,
35 winch injuries were reported between 2000 and 2011, 8 of them fatal;
entanglements happened mainly while levelling the wire on the drum,
retrieving wire and working the catheads
([NIOSH 2024-110, p. 1](../library/niosh-winch-guard-2024.html)).

- **Guards**: a rail or barrier in front of the winch stops a person falling
  onto the drum ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 50](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  US rules require guards on gearing, chain and belt drives and rotating
  shafts, while leaving access to winches, drums and gurdies
  ([46 CFR 28.215](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)); NIOSH publishes free
  guard designs for common shrimp winches.
- **Controls and stops**: hauling gear needs an emergency stop within reach,
  and a second one in the wheelhouse. The person at the controls must see the
  crew near the gear, give it full attention and do nothing else. If they
  cannot see, agree clear hand signals and keep a reliable link between deck
  and wheelhouse ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 47-49](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Never leave a hauler running unattended.** People have been struck by
  the anchor or weight coming up to the block while they were doing another
  job ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 56](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  A skipper working alone set his winch hauling, went back to box fish,
  tripped over a box, and was dragged into the winch by his oilskin sleeve.
  He lay trapped for nine hours
  ([EU guide, Module IV, 14.1, p. 116](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)).
- **Pot haulers**: on a V-wheel hauler the ejector knife must be in place, or
  the rope can wrap round and pull your hands in. A capstan hauler is more
  dangerous: a riding turn can drag your hand onto the drum. Keep cuffs and
  loose clothing away. Use a guard over the control so it cannot be knocked
  on ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 55](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Too much power capsizes boats**: a strong hauler can pull a small boat
  over if the pots are fast on the bottom. Set the hydraulic relief valve so
  it can haul the string but not endanger the boat (same page). See
  [Stability on a fishing boat](fishing-vessel-stability.html).
- **Net drums**: the operator must see the crew handling the net and be able
  to stop at once; otherwise fit a stop next to the drum
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 52](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Lifting, trawl doors and the bag

- Lifting gear must be strong enough, marked with its safe working load, and
  inspected every year by a competent person; take worn gear out of service
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 47 and 50](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- When chaining up trawl doors, **throw the chain through; never put a hand
  or arm through an opening.** The winch operator must see that the men at the
  doors are clear ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 49](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- Stop a lifted bag from swinging, and keep people out from under it. Wear a
  harness if you must lean far over the rail to hook on
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 51](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Pots and creels

Being caught in the rope and dragged overboard while shooting is the great
danger ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 52-54](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)):

- Separate the crew from the rope: a barrier, a shooting ramp or transom
  gate, crew standing forward and clear.
- Stack pots securely in shooting order and mark any pot left aside, so pots
  do not go out of sequence.
- Make a plan of who does what; everyone wears a PFD and carries a knife.
- Keep clear of back ropes and leg ropes as pots go over; shoot more slowly if
  the crew are rushed; avoid shooting in fairways.
- Carry pots close to the chest, back straight, and set up a davit or rail
  roller so pots come aboard at working height
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 56](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Nets, lines and hooks

Monofilament snags on watches, rings and clothing; hooks can catch flesh and
pull a person over ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 58-59](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)):

- Take off watches and jewellery; wear snag-free clothing and a close-fitting
  PFD; keep a sharp knife at hand.
- Shoot nets from bins, through a chute, so no one stands on the net; lay
  baited hooks on the coils and use a chute or stick rather than throwing
  lines by hand.
- Fairleads and hanging blocks must hold the gear even in a heavy roll, or it
  sweeps across the deck.

For an embedded hook see [Fish hooks and fish spines](../health/fish-hook-injuries.html).

## Slips, falls and fouled gear

- Keep the deck clear and clean; use non-slip coatings, duckboards or mats;
  close hatches; light the deck well; fit handrails; keep freeing ports and
  escape routes clear ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 42](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- Someone keeps a lookout and warns the crew of heavy seas coming aboard
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 48](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Fouled gear**: stop and think first, tell everyone the plan, make sure
  the gear cannot pull back out, and wear a harness if you lean outboard. You
  can replace gear but not a life
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 43](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Protective gear

Oilskins, steel-toe boots and suitable gloves for deck work; a hard hat where
something can hit your head; goggles when grinding; ear protection near
noisy machinery ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 37-39](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
