## Situation

You need to stop and hold position: to wait, to shelter, or because the
engine has failed and you are being set towards danger. Anchoring is also a safety tool: the US Coast Guard lists
engine failure, waiting outside a breaking bar, riding out a storm and holding
position near a disabled vessel as reasons to anchor
([BCH, J.3, p. 6-119](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).

**If the engine has failed near rocks or a lee shore, go to
[Engine failure near rocks or a lee shore](../emergency/engine-failure-near-danger.html)
first.**

## Do now

1. **Brief the crew** before you arrive. Agree simple hand signals: engine
   and wind noise make shouting useless. Everyone working the anchor wears a
   lifejacket ([BCH, J.10, p. 6-124](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
2. **Choose the spot** on the chart: sheltered, shallow enough for your rode,
   clear of cables and obstructions, and outside the swinging circle of other
   boats ([BCH, J.11, p. 6-125](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
   Prefer firm sand or dense clay; avoid weed, soft mud and rock
   ([BCH, J.12, p. 6-127](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
3. **Check the gear**: the anchor shackle is done up (and moused or wired for a
   long stay) and the inboard end of the rode is made fast to the boat. Many
   anchors have been lost through a loose shackle or an unsecured bitter end
   ([BCH, J.14, p. 6-128](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
4. **Flake the rode** on deck so it runs free. Never stand in the coils and do
   not throw the anchor (same page).
5. **Approach slowly, into the wind or current.** Line
   up two marks ashore, or use radar or GPS, so you can tell later whether you
   are moving ([BCH, J.11 and J.13, pp. 6-125 and 6-127](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
6. **Stop, and lower the anchor** hand over hand to the bottom. Note how much
   rode is out when it touches. Take a turn on the bitt or cleat so you
   control the rode; in strong wind your hands alone will not hold it
   ([BCH, J.14, p. 6-128](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
7. **Drop back** slowly (go astern gently or let the wind push you), paying out
   rode around the turn.
8. **Pay out enough scope**: 5 to 7 times the depth, measured from the bow
   fairlead to the seabed; 10 times in heavy weather. In 6 m (20 ft) of water
   at 5:1 that is 30 m (100 ft) of rode
   ([BCH, J.14.a, p. 6-128](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
   Count the depth plus the height of the bow above water, and allow for the
   tide rising.
9. **Set it**: snub the rode so the anchor bites, then go gently astern. If it
   comes up clogged with mud or weed, wash it off and try again
   ([BCH, J.15, p. 6-129](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
10. **Make fast**: on a post, one round turn then three figure-eights; on a
    cleat, a turn round the base then three figure-eights. Put chafe gear
    where the rode passes the fairlead
    ([BCH, J.16 and J.18, pp. 6-129 to 6-130](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
11. **Check it holds before you stop the engine** (next section), then show
    the anchor light or shape: see
    [Navigation lights and day shapes](navigation-lights-and-shapes.html).

**Never anchor by the stern**: waves can swamp the boat
([BCH, J.10, p. 6-124](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).

## Why

An anchor holds best when the pull on it is nearly horizontal. Enough scope
lets the lower end of the rode lie at 8° or less to the bottom, so the flukes
dig in instead of being lifted out. A short length of chain between anchor
and rope lowers the angle of pull further and protects the rope from chafe on
the bottom ([BCH, J.7 and J.14.a, pp. 6-122 and 6-128](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).

The US Coast Guard suggests carrying two anchors: a working anchor and a
storm anchor one and a half to two times as effective
([BCH, J.4, p. 6-120](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
Its suggested weights for lightweight fluke-type anchors:

| Boat length up to | Working anchor | Storm anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 m (20 ft) | 2.3 kg (5 lb) | 5.4 kg (12 lb) |
| 9 m (30 ft) | 5.4 kg (12 lb) | 8.2 kg (18 lb) |
| 12 m (40 ft) | 10 kg (22 lb) | 20 kg (44 lb) |

## Check that it worked

- Take bearings of two fixed marks at least 45° apart. Small changes as the
  boat swings are normal. **If the heading stays steady but the bearings
  change, you are dragging**
  ([BCH, J.17, p. 6-130](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
- A rode that jerks or vibrates usually means the anchor is not holding.
- A buoyed trip line float should stay in one place when you go astern.
- A GPS anchor alarm helps but does not replace bearings (same page).

Keep an anchor watch: check the strain on the rode, bearings, weather, other
boats and the fastening; sound the fog signal if fog comes in
([BCH, J.19, p. 6-130](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html);
[FM 55-501, para 2-36, p. 2-13](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
See [Keeping watch, especially at night](night-watchkeeping.html).

To weigh anchor, motor slowly towards it while taking in the rode, so the
rode cannot foul the propeller. When the rode is straight up and down the
anchor usually breaks out. Wash it clean at the surface before lifting it
aboard, then secure it so it cannot shift
([BCH, J.20 and J.22, pp. 6-131 to 6-132](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).

## If that fails

- **Dragging**: pay out more scope. If it still drags, recover the anchor and
  re-anchor on better ground, or use a second anchor. Watch the rode near the
  propeller when you manoeuvre.
- **Anchor will not break out** when you weigh: snub the rode on the bitt and
  motor ahead a few feet; if that fails, motor slowly in a wide circle to
  change the angle of pull, keeping the rode clear of the propeller. If you
  rigged a trip line from the crown, haul the anchor up by it, crown first
  ([BCH, J.20 and J.21, p. 6-131](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
- **Rode round the propeller**: stop the engine and see
  [Rope or net round the propeller](../fix-it/fouled-propeller.html).
- **Fishing gear or anchor snagged and the boat heeling**: release the load.
  Have a quick release, bolt cutters, grinder or axe ready
  ([MCA stability guidance, p. 16](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).
