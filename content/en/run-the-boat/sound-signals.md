Sound signals let vessels talk without a radio. A **short blast** lasts about
1 second; a **prolonged blast** lasts 4 to 6 seconds (Rule 32). Below, `·` is
a short blast and `—` a prolonged blast. Check the exact text in the
[library copy of the Rules](../library/uscg-navrules.html).

## What a boat must carry

A vessel of 12 m (39 ft) or more must have a whistle; 20 m (66 ft) or more, a
whistle and a bell; 100 m (328 ft) or more, also a gong. A vessel under 12 m
need not carry these, but must have some other means of making an efficient
sound signal, such as an air horn (Rule 33).

## Manoeuvring signals, vessels in sight of one another

These are sounded by a power-driven vessel under way, when vessels are in
sight of one another and she manoeuvres as the rules allow or require. They
announce what she **is doing**, not a request (Rule 34(a)).

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `·` one short | I am altering my course to starboard |
| `· ·` two short | I am altering my course to port |
| `· · ·` three short | I am operating astern propulsion (going astern) |
| `· · · · ·` five or more short and rapid | **I doubt** that you are taking enough action to avoid collision (Rule 34(d)) |

The whistle signals may be backed up by light flashes: one, two or three
flashes with the same meanings. Each flash lasts about 1 second, with about
1 second between flashes and at least 10 seconds between signals; the light,
if fitted, is an all-round white light visible at least 5 miles (Rule 34(b)). The doubt signal may be backed
up by at least five short and rapid flashes (Rule 34(d)).

If you hear five short blasts, **you** are the one being warned. Check your
situation at once and act clearly: see
[Rules of the road](rules-of-the-road.html).

### Overtaking in a narrow channel (Rule 34(c))

When the vessel ahead must move over to let you pass:

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `— — ·` | I intend to overtake you on your starboard side |
| `— — · ·` | I intend to overtake you on your port side |
| `— · — ·` | Reply from the vessel being overtaken: I agree |
| `· · · · ·` | Reply: I doubt it is safe (do not overtake) |

### Other signals

- **Bend or obstruction:** coming up to a bend or an area where other vessels
  may be hidden, sound one prolonged blast `—`. Any vessel round the bend
  that hears it answers with one prolonged blast (Rule 34(e)).
- **Whistles far apart:** a vessel with whistles more than 100 m apart uses
  only one of them for these signals (Rule 34(f)).

The US Inland Rules add more signals, for example one prolonged blast when a
power-driven vessel leaves a berth, and allow agreement by VHF instead of
whistles (Inland Rule 34(g) and (h)). They are not part of the International
Rules.

## Fog signals (restricted visibility)

In or near restricted visibility, by day or night, these signals are sounded
at intervals of **not more than 2 minutes** unless shown otherwise (Rule 35).

| Signal | Vessel | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `—` | Power-driven vessel making way through the water | 35(a) |
| `— —` (about 2 s apart) | Power-driven vessel under way but stopped, not making way | 35(b) |
| `— · ·` | Not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught, **sailing**, **fishing**, towing or pushing | 35(c) |
| `— · ·` | Fishing vessel at anchor, or restricted vessel working at anchor | 35(d) |
| `— · · ·` | Last vessel of a tow, if manned (right after the towing vessel's signal) | 35(e) |
| Bell rung rapidly for about 5 s, at least **every minute** | At anchor. Over 100 m: also a gong aft right after the bell. May add `· — ·` to warn an approaching vessel | 35(g) |
| 3 separate strokes on the bell, rapid ringing, 3 strokes | Aground (plus gong if over 100 m; may add a suitable whistle signal) | 35(h) |
| Any other efficient sound signal, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes | Vessel of 12 m to under 20 m (instead of the bell signals); vessel under 12 m (instead of any of the signals above) | 35(i), 35(j) |
| `· · · ·` | Pilot vessel on duty, in addition to her other signal | 35(k) |

Notice that a **sailing boat in fog sounds `— · ·`**, not one long blast. A
yacht motoring in fog is power-driven and sounds `—`.

Fixed fog signals on lighthouses and buoys (horn, siren, bell, whistle, gong)
are marked on the chart with their type ([Chart No. 1, section R](../library/noaa-chart-no-1.html)).
A wave-actuated bell or whistle on a buoy sounds irregularly; a vessel's
signals are regular.

What to do in fog is in [Rules of the road](rules-of-the-road.html) (Rule 19).
In short: slow down, show lights, sound your signal, post a lookout away from
the engine noise, and listen ([Boat Crew Handbook, p. 5-9](../library/uscg-boat-crew-handbook-seamanship.html)).
Sound travels strangely in fog. The direction a signal seems to come from is
not reliable.

## Attracting attention

Any vessel may make light or sound signals to attract attention, or point a
searchlight towards a danger, as long as these cannot be mistaken for any
signal in the rules and do not dazzle other vessels. Avoid strobe-like
flashing or revolving lights for this (Rule 36). A boat not normally used for
towing that tows a vessel in trouble may light up the towline (Rule 24(i)).

## Distress

A vessel in distress that needs help uses the signals in Annex IV (Rule 37).
They include a continuous sounding of any fog-signal apparatus, a gun or other
explosive signal fired about once a minute, SOS by any means, "Mayday" by
radio, red flares, orange smoke, flames on the vessel, and slowly raising and
lowering the outstretched arms. **Use them only for real distress.** The full
list and what to do is on [Distress signals and what they mean](../emergency/distress-signals.html)
and [Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
