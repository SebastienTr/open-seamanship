VHF radio is the first tool for safety at sea within roughly coastal range:
coastguards, ships and other boats listen on it, and a call reaches all of
them at once, unlike a phone. Keep it switched on and listening whenever you
are under way. Know your boat's name, call sign and MMSI (the nine-digit
number that identifies a DSC radio,
[Pub. 117, p. 3-3](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)), and keep them written
by the set.

## The channels that matter

| Channel | Use | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | International distress, safety and calling. Vessels and coast stations keep a listening watch. Use it to make contact, then move to a working channel. | [Pub. 117, p. 4-5](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html) |
| 70 | Digital selective calling (DSC) only. Never speak on it. | [Pub. 117, p. 4-10](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html) |
| 06 | Intership safety; also used at the scene of a search and rescue. | [Pub. 117, p. 4-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html); [FM 55-501, p. 9-7](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html) |
| 13 | Bridge-to-bridge navigation safety. In US waters ships over 20 m keep a watch on it. | [Pub. 117, p. 4-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html) |

Working channels for ship-to-ship and port calls vary by country: see
[VHF channels](../tables/vhf-channels.html). In the UK, the coastguard keeps a
loudspeaker watch on channel 16, and the primary way to raise a distress or
urgency alert is by VHF DSC ([UK small fishing vessel code, 9.2.3](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html)).
For DSC see [GMDSS and DSC](gmdss-and-dsc.html).

## Making a routine call

1. Listen first, so you do not talk over someone else, especially a
   distress call.
2. Speak slowly and clearly in a normal voice, with the microphone a few
   centimetres (1 to 3 inches) from your mouth. Keep messages short and in
   plain language.
3. Say the name of the station you are calling, then "this is", then your
   boat's name. Say names once in good conditions and twice in poor ones.
4. End each transmission with "over" when you expect a reply, and "out" when
   the exchange is finished. Never "over and out". "Wait" means a short
   pause; "wait out" a longer one.
5. Let go of the transmit button between phrases, so others can break in with
   something urgent.
6. If there is no answer after three calls at 2-minute intervals, wait (the
   rule is 15 minutes, or at least 3 if you will not interfere with others)
   before trying again.

([FM 55-501, paras 9-15 to 9-19, pp. 9-5 to 9-7](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html).)
Spell names with the [phonetic alphabet](../tables/phonetic-alphabet.html).

## The three priority calls

Messages go in this order: distress, then urgency, then safety, then
everything else ([FM 55-501, para 9-80, p. 9-25](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

**MAYDAY: grave and imminent danger, immediate help needed.** It has
absolute priority ([FM 55-501, para 9-69, p. 9-23](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
Send a DSC distress alert first if you have DSC, then the voice call on
channel 16. The format posted on US fishing boats is
([46 CFR 28.265(d)(4)](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)):

> MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY
> This is (boat name), (boat name), (boat name). Over

Let go of the button and listen briefly. If no one answers, repeat. Then,
whether answered or not:

> MAYDAY, this is (boat name)
> Position (latitude and longitude, or bearing and distance from a known point)
> Nature of the distress
> Number of people on board and any injuries
> How seaworthy the boat still is
> Description of the boat (length, colour, hull)
> I will be listening on channel 16
> This is (boat name, call sign)

Repeat until answered; if there is still no answer, try another channel
([FM 55-501, para 9-72, p. 9-23](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
The full step-by-step card is [Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).

**PAN PAN: urgent, but no immediate danger to life or boat.** For example
engine failure when you are safe from immediate danger, or a need for medical
advice. Format
([Pub. 117, p. 5-1](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html);
[Wikipedia, 'Pan-pan'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)):

> PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN
> All stations (three times), or the coastguard station's name
> This is (boat name) three times, call sign
> Position, the problem, the help you need
> Over

When the problem is over, tell all stations it is cancelled
([FM 55-501, para 9-87, p. 9-26](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
See also [Getting medical advice by radio](../health/getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).

**SECURITE (say-cure-ee-tay): a navigation or weather warning.** Usually
announced on channel 16 and then read on a working channel. Listen until you
know whether it concerns you ([FM 55-501, paras 9-88 to 9-90, p. 9-27](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## When you hear a call

- **MAYDAY**: stop any transmission that could interfere, keep listening on
  that channel, and write down everything: name, position, problem, number of
  people. If you are close, answer at once; if you are some way off, wait a
  short time for the coastguard or nearer vessels to answer. To acknowledge,
  say the casualty's name three times, "this is", your name three times,
  "received MAYDAY", then ask for what you need to help
  ([FM 55-501, paras 9-69 to 9-76, pp. 9-23 to 9-24](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
  If no one answers the casualty, try to raise the coastguard yourself
  ([FM 55-501, para 9-73, p. 9-24](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).
- **PAN PAN**: listen for at least 3 minutes; do not interfere
  ([FM 55-501, para 9-85, p. 9-26](../library/fm-55-501-marine-crewman.html)).

## Mistakes and false alerts

If you send a DSC distress alert by mistake, do not just switch off and hope.
Switch the set to channel 16 and tell all stations: "All stations, all
stations, all stations, this is (name, call sign, MMSI, position). Cancel my
distress alert of (date, time UTC)." Action is not normally taken against a
crew who report and cancel a false alert
([Pub. 117, 400H, p. 4-16](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).

## Language and licences

VHF calls between ships are usually in English. A French study of collisions
found that attempts by fishermen to agree manoeuvres with merchant ships by
VHF had failed, often for language reasons; it suggests asking the coastguard
(CROSS in France) to relay when you are unsure
([BEAmer 2001, section 19](../library/beamer-abordages-peche-commerce-2001.html)).
Talking is never a substitute for following the
[rules of the road](rules-of-the-road.html).

In the US, a commercial fishing vessel that uses a radio needs a Federal
Communications Commission ship station licence
([46 CFR 28.150](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)). In the UK and Europe, the
Short Range Certificate is the operator qualification for non-SOLAS boats
using VHF and DSC in Sea Area A1
([Wikipedia, 'Global Maritime Distress and Safety System'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
See also [UK: HM Coastguard and the RNLI](../regions/uk-search-and-rescue.html)
and [France: CROSS, SNSM and calling for help](../regions/france-search-and-rescue.html).
