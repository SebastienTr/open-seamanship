**If your boat is heeling dangerously right now:** stop hauling or lifting,
release or cut the load or the gear, and close weathertight doors and
hatches ([MGN 526, section 3, pp. 6-7](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html)). If she has capsized or is swamped, go to
[Capsized or swamped small boat](../emergency/capsized-or-swamped.html).

## Why it matters

Capsize from too little stability is a major cause of death on fishing
vessels under 24 m, and especially under 15 m
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 22](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
A capsize happens suddenly, often without warning, leaving little or no time
to call for help or get into a liferaft
([FAO 2025, 4.2, p. 29](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).
Boats have capsized in flat calm
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 24](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
Stability is not fixed: it changes during each trip and over the years, and
it depends on how the skipper loads and works the boat
([FAO 2025, 4.1, p. 29](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).

For the basic idea of weight, buoyancy and the centre of gravity, see
[Why boats float and stay upright](../know-your-boat/stability-basics.html).
In short: a boat is safest with a **low centre of gravity**, a **wide beam**
and **plenty of freeboard**, the height from the water to the deck edge
([MCA stability guidance, pp. 10-14](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

## What capsizes fishing boats

**Weight high up.** Gantries, masts, derricks, net drums, shelters, pots and
rope stacked on deck all raise the centre of gravity, and so does fitting a
lighter engine low down. A small weight high up does as much harm as a large
one low down ([MCA stability guidance, p. 10](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html);
[MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 22](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
Boats also gain weight over the years; check the draught or freeboard every
year (same page).

**Overloading.** Every boat will capsize if overloaded. Know your minimum safe
freeboard and keep to it: "when your freeboard is gone, so is your
survivability" ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 24](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
Carrying extra pots to a new ground is a common trap: a boat that looks safe
in calm water can capsize when she ships a little water or the gear shifts
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 53](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

**Lifting.** A lifted load acts as if its weight were at the top of the
derrick or crane, even when it is just clear of the water. When the boat
heels, the load swings out and pulls her further over
([MCA stability guidance, pp. 20-22](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).
Keep within the safe working load, keep the lifting point low, and stop
lifting well before any part of the deck goes under. A heavy cod end full of
mud or stones can be enough to capsize the boat; if in doubt, cut the net
open ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 23 and 51](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

**Water and fish that can move.** Water on deck, half-full tanks and loose
fish all slide to the low side when she heels (the "free surface effect"),
and water on deck also adds weight and lowers the freeboard
([MCA stability guidance, pp. 17-19](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).
Keep freeing ports and scuppers clear and never block them; use pound boards;
stow fish below as soon as you can; keep tanks full or empty; keep bilges low
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 23](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
Cover tubs and bins so they cannot fill with water
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 57](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

**Snagged gear and other outside forces.** Gear fast on the bottom, towing,
wind or a hard turn heel the boat; the higher the force acts, the worse. Tow
from a point as low and as far inboard as you can, and have a quick release
on the winch, or bolt cutters, a grinder or an axe at hand
([MCA stability guidance, p. 16](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html);
[MGN 526, section 3, p. 6](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html)).

**Weight on one side** gives a list that will not correct itself, and leaves
less stability on that side ([MCA stability guidance, pp. 16-17](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

**Loll.** A boat that is top-heavy may flop from one side to the other
instead of standing upright. This is far more dangerous than a list. To
correct it: lower weights, add weight low down (low side first), remove weight
high up (high side first), and get rid of free surfaces
([MCA stability guidance, p. 20](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

Most capsizes come from several of these at once. Break the chain: secure
gear, keep the catch in pounds or boxes, close doors and hatches, keep freeing
ports open, keep weight low
([MCA stability guidance, p. 23](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

## Simple checks

**Freeboard mark (UK, Wolfson method).** A mark painted on the side shows the
minimum freeboard for your boat
([MGN 526, 1.2, p. 1](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html);
[MCA stability guidance, pp. 30-31](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)):

| Waterline, upright or heeled while working | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Below the mark (green) | Safe operation (decked boats only) |
| Mark partly under (amber) | Acceptable with care, in good conditions |
| Mark completely under (red) | Danger of capsize: stop |

Open (undecked) boats have no green zone. It is the freeboard left when the
boat is heeled, not the angle of heel, that shows the risk. With catch on
deck, the MCA example boat could lift only 0.3 tonnes safely; with the catch
in the hold, 3 tonnes (same pages).

**Roll test.** Time five full rolls (side to side
and back) three times and take the average for one roll. If the roll period
in seconds is longer than the beam in metres, the boat is tender and its
centre of gravity is too high: get advice from the MCA, Seafish or a naval
architect. A roll period that grows over the months means stability is
getting worse ([MCA stability guidance, pp. 29 and 35](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html);
[MGN 526, 1.8, p. 2](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html)).

**Heel test.** Heel the boat with the same test weight in the same loading
condition and record the angle. If a repeat test differs by more than 10%,
seek professional advice ([MCA stability guidance, p. 36](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

## Modifications and the rules

Bigger winches or drums, higher gantries, shelter decks, cranes, hoppers or a
lighter engine can all reduce stability. Do not just add ballast: it lowers
freeboard ([MGN 526, section 3, pp. 3-4](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html);
[MCA stability guidance, p. 34](../library/mca-fishing-stability.html)).

- **UK**: tell the MCA and get approval before modifying the boat or changing
  the fishing method ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 25](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
  [MSN 1871, p. 1](../library/mca-msn-1871.html)). Owners and skippers have a
  legal duty to keep the boat and its working methods safe even where no
  stability standard applies ([MGN 526, 2.1, p. 3](../library/mca-mgn-526-wolfson.html)).
- **US**: formal stability rules apply to fishing vessels of 79 ft (24 m) and
  over built or substantially altered since 15 September 1991. They must carry
  stability instructions written so the master can use them, and meet extra
  criteria when lifting over the side
  ([46 CFR 28.500, 28.530, 28.545](../library/cfr-46-part-28.html)).
- **Everywhere**: the skipper or owner should check stability regularly; it is
  in the skipper's hands ([FAO 2025, 4.1, p. 29](../library/fao-small-fishing-vessel-inspection-2025.html)).
