Lights tell you **what** a vessel is, **which way** she is heading, and
sometimes **what she is doing**. Day shapes (black balls, cones, cylinders and
diamonds) do the same by day. The rules on lights and shapes apply in all
weathers. Lights are shown from sunset to sunrise, and also by day in
restricted visibility; shapes are shown by day (Rule 20). This page gives the
International Rules in plain words; check the exact text in the
[library copy of the Rules](../library/uscg-navrules.html). Who gives way to
whom is on [Rules of the road](rules-of-the-road.html).

## The basic lights

| Light | Colour | Arc (where you can see it) | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masthead light | White | 225°, from right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on each side | 21(a) |
| Sidelights | Green to starboard, red to port | 112.5° each, from right ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on its side | 21(b) |
| Sternlight | White | 135°, 67.5° each side of right astern | 21(c) |
| Towing light | Yellow | Same arc as the sternlight | 21(d) |
| All-round light | Any | 360° | 21(e) |
| Flashing light | Any | 120 or more flashes a minute | 21(f) |

**Reading a vessel's heading at night.** If you see red and green together,
she is pointing at you. Green only: you are on her starboard side. Red only:
you are on her port side; if she is power-driven and crossing, she is the
stand-on vessel and you must keep clear (Rule 15). A white light alone, low
down, is often a sternlight: you are overtaking her and must keep clear
(Rule 13). Two masthead lights, the after one higher, mean a power-driven
vessel usually 50 m (164 ft) or more; the way the two lines up shows her
heading (Rule 23(a)).

### Minimum range of visibility, in nautical miles (Rule 22)

| Vessel length | Masthead | Sidelights | Stern, towing, all-round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 m (164 ft) or more | 6 | 3 | 3 |
| 20 m to under 50 m | 5 | 2 | 2 |
| 12 m to under 20 m | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Under 12 m (39 ft) | 2 | 1 | 2 |

A small boat's sidelights may be visible for only 1 mile. At 6 knots that is
10 minutes; at 20 knots, 3 minutes.

## Vessels under way

| What you see at night | By day | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Masthead light(s), sidelights, sternlight | none | Power-driven vessel under way. Under 50 m, one masthead light is enough | 23(a) |
| As above plus an all-round **flashing yellow** light | none | Air-cushion vessel (hovercraft) not on her hull | 23(b) |
| All-round white light plus sidelights | none | Power-driven vessel under 12 m may show this instead | 23(d)(i) |
| All-round white light only (sidelights if practicable) | none | Power-driven vessel under 7 m whose top speed is 7 knots or less | 23(d)(ii) |
| Sidelights and sternlight, **no masthead light** | none | Sailing vessel under way | 25(a) |
| One tricolour lantern at the masthead | none | Sailing vessel under 20 m may combine her side and stern lights this way. Never use it with the engine running | 25(b) |
| Red over green all-round lights at the masthead, plus side and stern lights | none | Sailing vessel (optional extra; not with the tricolour) | 25(c) |
| White torch or lantern shown in time | none | Sailing vessel under 7 m, or a boat under oars, if it cannot carry proper lights | 25(d) |
| Lights of a power-driven vessel | **Cone, point down** | Sailing vessel motor-sailing (sail and engine together) | 25(e) |

## Towing and pushing

| Night | Day | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Two** masthead lights in a vertical line, sidelights, sternlight, and a **yellow towing light above the sternlight** | none | Power-driven vessel towing astern, tow 200 m or less | 24(a) |
| **Three** masthead lights in a vertical line, plus the rest as above | **Diamond** | Towing astern, tow longer than 200 m (656 ft), measured from her stern to the end of the tow | 24(a) |
| Two masthead lights, sidelights, sternlight (no towing light) | none | Pushing ahead or towing alongside | 24(c) |
| Sidelights and sternlight | Diamond (if tow over 200 m) | The vessel being towed | 24(e) |
| Towing lights plus red-white-red all-round | Ball, diamond, ball (plus the towing diamond if the tow is over 200 m) | Towing vessel that cannot easily turn away from her course | 27(c) |

A tow is a hazard you may not see. **Never pass between a tug and her tow.**
An almost submerged towed object shows a white all-round light, visible
3 miles (Rule 22(d) and 24(g)).

## Fishing vessels

These lights mean the vessel is actually fishing. When she is only passing
through, she shows the lights of an ordinary vessel of her length (Rule 26(e)).

| Night | Day | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Green over white** all-round, plus a masthead light abaft and higher (optional under 50 m); sidelights and sternlight when making way | **Two cones, points together** | Trawling | 26(b) |
| **Red over white** all-round; sidelights and sternlight when making way | Two cones, points together | Fishing other than trawling (nets, lines, pots) | 26(c) |
| As above plus a white all-round light (or a cone point up) on the side of the gear | Cone, point up, towards the gear | Gear extends more than 150 m (492 ft) from the vessel | 26(c)(ii) |

Memory aid: "green over white, trawling tonight; red over white, fishing
tonight". A fishing vessel's nets may be far from her. Give her plenty of
room, on the side away from the gear.

## Vessels that cannot keep out of the way

| Night | Day | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Two red** all-round in a vertical line (plus side and stern lights if making way) | **Two balls** | Not under command: broken down, cannot manoeuvre as required | 27(a) |
| **Red, white, red** all-round in a vertical line (plus normal lights if making way) | **Ball, diamond, ball** | Restricted in her ability to manoeuvre: dredging, laying cable, supplying, launching aircraft, towing that she cannot leave | 27(b) |
| Restricted lights plus **two red** on one side and **two green** on the other | Ball-diamond-ball plus **two balls** (danger side) and **two diamonds** (side you may pass) | Dredging or working underwater with an obstruction. **Pass on the green / diamond side** | 27(d) |
| Red-white-red, or a rigid copy of code flag **A** at least 1 m high | Flag A | Small vessel supporting divers. Keep well clear and slow down | 27(e) |
| Three green all-round lights (one at the masthead, one at each end of the fore yard) | Three balls in the same places | Mine clearance. Keep more than 1,000 m (0.54 miles) away | 27(f) |
| Power-driven lights plus **three red** all-round in a vertical line | **Cylinder** | Constrained by her draught (big ship that cannot leave the deep water) | 28 |
| **White over red** all-round at the masthead (plus side and stern lights under way) | none | Pilot vessel on duty | 29 |

Memory aids: "red over red, the captain is dead" (not under command); "white
over red, pilot ahead". Vessels under 12 m (39 ft), except diving boats, need
not show the lights and shapes of Rule 27 (Rule 27(g)).

## At anchor and aground

| Night | Day | What it is | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White all-round forward, and a lower white all-round aft | **One ball** forward | At anchor. Under 50 m, one white all-round light is enough. Ships of 100 m or more must also light their decks | 30(a) to (c) |
| Anchor light(s) plus **two red** all-round in a vertical line | **Three balls** in a vertical line | Aground | 30(d) |

A vessel under 7 m (23 ft) at anchor not in or near a narrow channel, fairway
or anchorage, or where other vessels normally navigate, need not show anchor lights or the ball (Rule 30(e)). A vessel
under 12 m aground need not show the two reds or three balls (Rule 30(f)).
Everyone else must, and in practice a small boat anchored where others pass
should always show an anchor light.

## What your own boat must show

- **Under power, under 12 m:** masthead light, sidelights and sternlight, or
  an all-round white light plus sidelights (Rule 23(a) and 23(d)(i)).
- **Under sail:** sidelights and sternlight, or a tricolour at the masthead if
  under 20 m (Rule 25(a) and (b)).
- **Motor-sailing:** you are power-driven. Show steaming lights (masthead,
  sides, stern), not the tricolour, and by day a cone point down (Rules 3(c)
  and 25(e)).
- **At anchor:** an all-round white light where it is best seen; by day a
  black ball forward (Rule 30).
- Carry spare bulbs and a powerful torch. A sailing boat under 7 m or a rowing
  boat that cannot carry lights must at least show a white light in time to
  prevent a collision (Rule 25(d)).

Check your lights at dusk and during the night. A dead sidelight makes your
boat impossible to read; see [Keeping watch](night-watchkeeping.html) and
[Finding a 12 V electrical fault](../fix-it/electrical-fault-finding.html).

## Lights that are not vessels

Shore lights, lighthouses and buoys have their own colours and rhythms, shown
on the chart (for example "Fl(2) R 10s" for a red light flashing twice every
10 seconds) ([Bowditch, section 538](../library/bowditch.html)). A light that
flashes in a regular rhythm and stays in the same place is almost certainly an
aid to navigation; see [Buoys and beacons](iala-buoyage.html) and
[Reading a nautical chart](reading-a-chart.html).
