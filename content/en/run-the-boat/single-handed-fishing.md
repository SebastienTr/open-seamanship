**Fishing alone is not recommended.** If you do it, everything on this page
is about one fact: if you are hurt, trapped or in the water, nobody is there
to stop the boat, pull you out or call for help
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 32](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
[EU guide, p. 43](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)).

Two real cases from the EU guide
([Module IV, 14, pp. 115-117](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)):

- An experienced skipper trawling alone set the winch hauling and went to box
  fish. He tripped over a fish box, his oilskin sleeve caught on the warp and
  he was dragged under the winch. He lay trapped for nine hours before he tore
  free and reached the radio.
- A potter with 17 years' experience left harbour at 07:30 and spoke to his
  wife by VHF at 09:30. His boat was found that evening with no one aboard,
  held by a pot jammed in the gunwale. He did not normally wear a PFD. His
  body was never found.

## Before you leave

- **Tell someone** (family, a friend or the coastguard) where you are going
  and when you will be back
  ([MCA single-handed leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)).
- **Check the weather** before you go and during the trip.
- **Test the radio**, tell the coast radio station or coastguard where you
  will fish and when you expect to return, and keep in touch with them and
  with nearby boats during the day
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 34](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Maintain the boat**: you cannot afford a breakdown (same page).
- **Rest**: alone, you are both lookout and crew. In a French study of
  collisions, the fishing boat's wheelhouse was manned by one person in about
  95% of cases, and drowsiness or fatigue was noted in more than a quarter
  ([BEAmer 2001, sections 14 and 15](../library/beamer-abordages-peche-commerce-2001.html)).
  Fit a watch alarm and keep it switched on
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 92](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  See [Keeping watch, especially at night](night-watchkeeping.html).
- **Limit the gear**: think about your safety before the number of pots you
  can work ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 53](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Stay on board

([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 33-34](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
[EU guide, p. 44](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html))

- **Remove the risk where you can**: non-slip deck, enough bulwark height, and
  a layout that keeps you separated from running ropes, wires and pots. If you
  cannot remove the risk, you must wear a PFD or a safety harness.
- **Clip on**: a safety line on a sliding ring along an overhead wire the
  length of the deck. If it can be arranged, connect the wire to an engine
  cut-out so that a heavy pull stops the engine.
- **Controls within reach**: fit extra boat controls where you can steer from
  the working deck; make sure you can reach the winch or hauler control, and
  add an emergency stop if needed. Think about how the engine will stop if
  you go over the side.
- **Never leave a hauler or winch running** while you do another job
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 56](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html);
  [EU guide, p. 117](../library/eu-guide-small-fishing-vessels.html)).
- **Keep the deck clear** of anything you could trip on.
- **Knives**: carry one you can reach easily, and place others at points
  round the deck, so you can cut yourself free of a rope
  ([MCA single-handed leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)).

More on deck hazards: [Working safely on deck](working-on-deck.html).

## If you go in the water

- **Wear a PFD all the time** that turns you face up with your mouth clear
  even if you are unconscious. The MCA recommends a 150 N automatic
  inflatable, separate or built into oilskins. Buoyant waistcoats and work
  vests are not enough. Without a PFD, cold water shock can kill in less than
  five minutes ([MCA single-handed leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)).
  See [Lifejackets and buoyancy aids](lifejackets.html).
- **Wear a PLB** (406 MHz with GPS) on the upper part of the PFD, so its
  aerial is clear of the water and can transmit to the coastguard
  ([MCA single-handed leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)).
- **Be able to climb back aboard**: a fixed ladder at the stern, or a rope
  ladder you can pull down from the water by a lanyard
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 33](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Fit an EPIRB** on the boat: it sends an alert with your position by
  itself if the boat capsizes or sinks
  ([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, p. 33](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  Register every beacon. See [GMDSS and DSC](gmdss-and-dsc.html).

What to do once you are in: [Person in cold water](../emergency/person-in-cold-water.html)
and [Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html).

## Health

A lone fisherman taken ill at sea may not be able to call for help. Medical
fitness matters more when you work alone
([MCA Fishermen's Safety Guide, pp. 92-93](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## UK rules for single-handed fishing boats

Under the current UK code for fishing vessels under 15 m
([UK small fishing vessel code](../library/mca-small-fishing-vessel-code-2024.html)):

- A single-handed boat must have a way for a person in the water to get back
  aboard, which that person can deploy from the water (7.11.1).
- Where an EPIRB or a PLB is required, a single-handed boat may carry either,
  but the MCA recommends both (Chapter 7 tables); skippers carrying an EPIRB
  are strongly advised to carry and wear a 406 MHz PLB as well (7.10.2).
- All crew, including a lone skipper, must wear a PFD or a harness and
  lifeline unless the risk of going overboard has been removed and recorded
  in a risk assessment (6.4.1).

The MCA also recommends checking stability with a roll or heel test
([MCA single-handed leaflet](../library/mca-fishing-leaflets.html)): see
[Stability on a fishing boat](fishing-vessel-stability.html).
