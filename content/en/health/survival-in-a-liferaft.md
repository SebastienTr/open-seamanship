## Situation

You have abandoned the boat and are in a liferaft, or about to board one. Help
may take hours or days. Your job now is to be found, to stay warm or cool,
and to keep the body's water.

Getting into the raft is the single best thing you can do: in Alaska's
fishing fleet, crew who reached a liferaft were 17 times more likely to
survive than those who stayed in the water
([NIOSH](../library/niosh-survive-sinking.html)). For abandoning the boat, see
[Abandon ship](../emergency/abandon-ship.html) and
[Liferafts](../run-the-boat/liferafts.html).

## Do now

### The first hour

1. **Get everyone in,** then cut or slip the painter with the raft knife and
    move clear of the sinking boat, but stay near the spot: searchers start there ([MCA, p. 89](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
2. **Stream the drogue (sea anchor).** It slows drift, keeps you near where
    searchers will look, and steadies the raft
    ([MCA, p. 89](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
3. **Close the entrance,** bail out water, inflate the floor, check for leaks,
    and leave only a small opening for air
    ([MCA, pp. 89 to 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
4. **Everyone takes seasickness tablets now,** even "hardened" fishermen:
    vomiting wastes water you cannot replace
    ([MCA, p. 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)). See
    [Seasickness](seasickness.html).
5. **Set up signals:** EPIRB on, SART or radar reflector up, handheld VHF and
    flares ready ([MCA, p. 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
    Post a lookout ([MCA, p. 89](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
6. **First aid:** treat injuries, then get everyone as dry and warm as
    possible: wring out wet clothes, put on thermal protective aids, huddle
    together ([FM 21-22, pp. 81 and 90](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
    See [Hypothermia](hypothermia.html).
7. **Take stock:** count the water, food, flares and kit. Put one reliable
    person in charge of rationing
    ([FM 21-22, pp. 83 to 84](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).

### Water: the rules

- **Never drink seawater.** It makes thirst worse, causes diarrhoea and
  delirium, and kills. In a study of 163 liferaft voyages, about 39 in 100
  people who drank seawater died, against 3 in 100 who did not
  ([Wikipedia, 'Seawater'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
  [FM 21-22, p. 81](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Never drink urine.** It contains waste products and increases thirst
  ([Navy 1944, p. 10](../library/navy-survival-land-sea-1944.html)).
- **Never drink alcohol** or the liquid in a compass
  ([FM 21-22, p. 84](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **No water, no food.** Digesting food uses up body water
  ([FM 21-22, p. 81](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Catch every drop of rain.** Rig the canopy and any cloth to collect it;
  let the first rain wash the salt off before you keep it
  ([FM 21-22, p. 82](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Ration from the start,** based on how long rescue may take and the chance
  of rain ([FM 21-22, p. 83](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
  Follow the instructions packed with your raft's water.
- **Drink your ration slowly,** holding it in the mouth before swallowing
  ([FM 21-22, p. 84](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
  The sick, the injured and anyone with fever need more
  ([FM 21-22, p. 84](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Save body water:** rest, keep in the shade in the heat, do not shout or
  struggle ([FM 21-22, p. 89](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).

### Day by day

- **Cold:** keep the entrance closed, stay dry, huddle, and keep the floor
  inflated ([MCA, p. 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- **Sun:** keep skin covered even when cloudy; shade the eyes from glare
  ([FM 21-22, p. 90](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Feet:** wet, cold, cramped feet swell and blister ("immersion foot").
  Bail out, keep the feet as dry as you can, loosen boots, move the toes, and
  raise the feet from time to time. If they swell or go numb, do not rub or
  heat them; keep them raised and dry
  ([Navy 1944, p. 8](../library/navy-survival-land-sea-1944.html);
  [FM 21-22, p. 100](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Bowels and urine:** with little food and water, constipation and dark,
  stinging urine are expected and not harmful in themselves. Do not take
  laxatives ([FM 21-22, p. 102](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html);
  [Navy 1944, p. 8](../library/navy-survival-land-sea-1944.html)).
- **Watch:** keep a lookout in turns, and a simple log of days, water and
  events ([FM 21-22, p. 91](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
- **Morale:** give everyone a job, talk, keep track of time, and expect
  tricks of the mind (seeing ships or land that are not there)
  ([FM 21-22, pp. 91 to 93](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
  Watch for anyone who becomes confused and may go over the side
  ([FM 21-22, pp. 95 and 103](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).

## Why

Many cold-water deaths come from drowning in the first minutes and hours
([PHS, p. 10-3](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)); getting out of the
water is far better than staying in it, even in cold wind
([PHS, p. 10-8](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). After that,
water is the limit: a person lasts only days without it, less in heat and
with effort ([FM 21-22, p. 81](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
Salt water cannot be used by the body: the kidneys must pass more water to get
rid of the salt than the seawater gave
([Wikipedia, 'Seawater'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The raft is dry inside, the drogue is out and the EPIRB light is flashing.
- Everyone has a ration plan and knows it.
- Nobody is shivering hard for long, and everyone's urine, though dark,
  still comes.

## If that fails

- **Someone becomes confused, drowsy, or stops passing urine:** give them
  extra water from the ration if you possibly can, keep them warm or shaded,
  and make sure they cannot fall overboard. See
  [Heat illness and dehydration](heat-illness-and-dehydration.html) and
  [Hypothermia](hypothermia.html).
- **A ship or aircraft is seen:** use flares and signals as packed; see
  [Distress signals and what they mean](../emergency/distress-signals.html).
- **Injuries:** see [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html),
  [Bleeding and wounds](bleeding-and-wounds.html) and
  [Burns](burns.html).
