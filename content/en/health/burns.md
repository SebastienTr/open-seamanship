## Situation

Someone is burned or scalded: hot water or oil in the galley, a hot exhaust
or engine part, a fire, a chemical such as battery acid, an electric shock,
or a bad sunburn. Act within seconds: cooling early limits the damage.

## Do now

1. **Stop the burning.** Get the person away from the heat. For
    electricity, switch off first or move them clear with a dry rope or stick
    that does not conduct; never touch them while they are live
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. **Check breathing.** Burns with breathing problems, or burns of the face
    from flames or hot gas, are among the most serious
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-57](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Get medical advice by radio at once: see
    [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
3. **Cool the burn with cool water now**
    ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). Use clean
    water at 10 to 25 °C (50 to 77 °F); not ice and not ice water
    ([Wikipedia, 'Burn'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Keep cooling for
    about 20 minutes, not just a few seconds (see "Uncertain"). Keep the rest of the
    body warm: a large burn cooled too long can cause hypothermia
    ([Wikipedia, 'Burn'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
4. **Take off rings, watches and tight clothing** near the burn before it
    swells. Do not pull off clothing that is stuck to the burn
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. **Leave blisters alone**
    ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
6. **Cover** loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing, cling film, petroleum
    gauze or a clean sheet
    ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
    [Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Do not put butter, oil or home remedies on a burn
    ([Wikipedia, 'Burn'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
7. **Treat for shock** if the burn is large: lay them down, keep them warm,
    give nothing by mouth until a doctor advises.
8. **Get medical advice** for any burn listed under "If that fails".

### Chemical burns (acid, alkali, battery, cleaning products)

1. Flush with lots of water at once and keep flushing while someone removes
    contaminated clothing, shoes and socks
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. Dry powder (for example lime or cement): brush it off first, unless you
    can flood it with a large amount of water
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. **Eyes:** hold the lids open and flush from the inner corner outwards with
    gentle running water for at least 20 minutes, whatever the chemical; you
    often cannot be sure whether it is an acid or an alkali. Do not put
    anything but water or saline in the eye
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-59](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Then get medical advice: chemical eye injuries can threaten sight.
4. Do not try to "neutralise" a chemical on the skin unless you know exactly
    what it is ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-59](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

### Electrical burns

The marks on the skin may be small while the damage inside is large. Watch
breathing and pulse, be ready for CPR, cover the entry and exit wounds, and
get medical advice
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-58](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

## Why

How bad a burn is depends more on how much skin it covers than on how deep it
is: a red burn over half the body can be worse than a deep burn over 3 per
cent ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-57](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Burned skin leaks fluid, so large burns cause shock; expect it when more than
10 per cent of the body is burned
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-22](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
To estimate the area, the casualty's own handprint (palm and fingers) is
about 1 per cent of the body; red skin without blisters is not counted
([Wikipedia, 'Burn', section 'Size'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). The
"Rule of Nines" divides the body into areas of about 9 per cent each
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-57](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
A burn with a headache or dizziness after a fire may also be
[carbon monoxide poisoning](carbon-monoxide.html)
([Wikipedia, 'Burn', section 'Diagnosis'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

| Depth | What you see | Usual course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial (first degree) | Red, painful, no blisters | Heals in about a week |
| Partial thickness (second degree) | Blisters, red and mottled, very painful | 2 to 3 weeks, may scar |
| Full thickness (third degree) | White, leathery or charred; may not hurt | Needs a hospital |

(After [Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-56](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html).)

## Check that it worked

- Pain eases after cooling.
- The dressing stays clean and dry. Change small burn dressings daily
  ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- Over the next days, watch for infection: spreading redness, pus, a bad
  smell, fever.

## If that fails

Get medical advice by radio, and expect evacuation, for:

- burns of the face, hands, feet, genitals or across a joint
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-57](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html));
- any burn with breathing problems or other injuries
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-57](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html));
- full-thickness burns, and any burn covering a large area (shock is
  expected above 10 per cent of the body);
- electrical and chemical burns;
- signs of shock (pale, cold, sweaty, fast weak pulse).

The PHS guide says burn patients with charred skin should be evacuated to a
doctor at once because they need hospital care for fluids and other
complications ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
If there is a fire on board, see [Fire on board](../emergency/fire-on-board.html).
For sunburn and heat, see [Heat illness and dehydration](heat-illness-and-dehydration.html).
