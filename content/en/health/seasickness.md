## Situation

You or a crew member feel queasy, yawn, go pale, sweat, get a headache, and
then vomit. Seasickness can strike anyone, including experienced sailors
([MCA, p. 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)). It is usually
just miserable, but it makes people careless on deck, and long vomiting can
dehydrate them.

## Do now

1. **Get the person on deck, in the fresh air, looking at the horizon,** if
    it is safe. Going below, where you cannot see the motion, makes it worse
    ([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
2. **Keep the head still.** If they must rest, lie on the back with eyes
    closed ([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
3. **Give them a job: steer.** Being the one in control helps; reading and
    close work make it worse
    ([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
4. **Clip them on.** A sick person leaning over the rail is a man-overboard
    waiting to happen. Make them vomit to leeward, clipped on, with someone
    watching. See [Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html).
5. **Keep them warm and dry,** and give small sips of water often.
6. **Medicines work best before symptoms start** or very early
    ([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Take them
    as the packet says, before sailing.

## Why

Seasickness comes from a mismatch: the inner ear feels the boat move but the
eyes, inside the cabin, see a still room
([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Seeing the
horizon lets the eyes agree with the ears. Most people get used to the motion
after some time at sea ("sea legs"); getting used to it is the most effective
cure but takes time ([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

| Medicine type | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antimuscarinic | scopolamine (hyoscine), often as a skin patch | The most effective; best taken before sailing; may blur vision |
| First-generation antihistamine | cinnarizine, cyclizine, meclizine, promethazine, dimenhydrinate | Prevent better than they cure; cause drowsiness |
| Ordinary anti-nausea drugs | ondansetron, metoclopramide | Do not work for motion sickness |

(After [Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness', 'Medication'](../library/wikipedia-en.html).)
The US Public Health Service medicine chest list includes meclizine tablets
and scopolamine patches
([PHS, pp. G-16 to G-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
Ginger and acupuncture help some people, but the evidence is mixed
([Wikipedia, 'Motion sickness'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The person stops vomiting and can keep water down.
- Their urine becomes light yellow again
  ([PHS, p. 1-16](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- They are alert enough to work safely on deck.

## If that fails

- **Vomiting for many hours, cannot keep fluids down, very little dark urine,
  dizziness when standing:** this is dehydration. Give small, frequent sips of
  an oral rehydration drink (water with sugar and salt; see
  [Heat illness and dehydration](heat-illness-and-dehydration.html)) and get
  [medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html)
  ([PHS, pp. 1-14 to 1-16](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Vomiting with severe belly pain, a high fever, a head injury, or blood in
  the vomit:** this is not seasickness. Get medical advice by radio.
- **In a liferaft:** everyone should take seasickness tablets as early as
  possible, because vomiting wastes body water you cannot replace
  ([MCA, p. 90](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)). See
  [Surviving in a liferaft](survival-in-a-liferaft.html).
- **A person who is sick every trip:** suggest they try medicine well before
  the next one, after checking with a doctor or pharmacist.
