## Situation

A hook has gone into someone's hand, arm or face while shooting or hauling
gear, baiting, or landing a fish; or someone has been stabbed by the spine of
a fish or ray. Hooks on moving lines can also catch a person and pull them
towards the hauler or over the side.

## Do now

### Caught on a moving line

1. **Stop the gear and cut the line at once.** Keep a sharp knife within reach
    whenever gear is running
    ([MCA, p. 59](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
2. Then deal with the hook as below.

### Hook in the skin

1. **Cut the line or lure free** so nothing pulls on the hook.
2. **Do not pull a barbed hook straight back out.** The barb tears the flesh
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-41](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. **Only the point is in, not the barb:** back it out gently along the curve
    ([PHS, p. 1-35](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
4. **Barb under the skin, near the surface:** clean the skin, then push the
    point on through the skin along its curve until the barb comes out. Cut the
    barb off with wire cutters or side-cutting pliers, and back the rest of
    the hook out the way it went in
    ([PHS, pp. 1-35 to 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
    [Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-40 to 4-41](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. **Clean** the wound with soap and clean water, let it bleed a little,
    and cover it with a dressing
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-40](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
6. **Ask about tetanus.** A hook or spine wound is a dirty puncture wound. If
    their last jab was 5 or more years ago, or they do not know, tell the
    doctor: they may need a booster
    ([PHS, pp. 1-36 and 1-40](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

**Leave the hook in, tape it so it cannot move, and get medical advice by
radio** if it is:

- in or near the eye: never try to remove anything from the eye
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-29](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html));
- deeply embedded: first aid does not include digging out deep objects
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-40](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html));
- in a joint, the face or the genitals, or near a large blood vessel;
- a large or treble hook you cannot cut.

The PHS guide says a hook is best removed by a doctor when one can be reached
([PHS, p. 1-35](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). See
[Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).

### Venomous fish spines (weever, stingray, scorpionfish, sea urchin)

1. **Rinse the wound** with sea water and remove any bits of spine you can see
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-14](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. **Soak the part in hot water**, as hot as the casualty can stand without
    scalding. **Test it on your own skin first.** Keep it hot for at least
    15 minutes and up to 30 to 60 minutes, adding hot water as it cools
    ([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
    [Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-15](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. **When the pain eases,** look again for pieces of spine and remove them
    ([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
4. **Do not use urine, vinegar or other folk remedies;** they do little or
    nothing ([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
    [Wikipedia, 'Stingray injury'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
5. **Raise the limb,** dress the wound and watch for infection
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-15](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

## Why

A barb is a backward-pointing spike, so pulling a barbed hook back tears a
second wound. Pushing it through and cutting the barb lets it come out along
the path it already made
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-41](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Hook and spine wounds are puncture wounds: dirty, deep and narrow, with a high
risk of infection ([PHS, p. 1-35](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
Fish venom causes severe pain, worst in the first 30 to 60 minutes. Hot water
usually eases it within minutes
([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
[Wikipedia, 'Stingray injury'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The hook is out in one piece, with the barb accounted for.
- Pain from a sting falls a lot within 10 to 20 minutes of hot water
  ([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
- Over the next days the wound gets less red and sore, not more.

## If that fails

- **Spreading redness, swelling, heat, pus or fever:**
  infection. See [Bleeding and wounds](bleeding-and-wounds.html) and ask a
  doctor by radio about antibiotics.
- **After a sting: faintness, trouble breathing, irregular heartbeat, a fit,
  or swelling spreading up the limb:** get medical advice by radio at once
  ([Wikipedia, 'Weever'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). A stingray barb in
  the chest or belly is an emergency: do not pull it out; pad around it
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-29](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html))
  and send a [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
- **Prevention:** wear gloves, keep clothing and lifejackets free of snag
  points, and keep hooks and lines in chutes or bins rather than shooting
  them by hand ([MCA, pp. 59 to 60](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
  See [Working safely on deck (fishing)](../run-the-boat/working-on-deck.html).
