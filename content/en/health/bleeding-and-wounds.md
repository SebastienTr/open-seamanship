## Situation

Someone is bleeding from a cut, a tear, a crush or a puncture. First stop the
bleeding; cleaning and dressing come after.

If the bleeding is heavy, call for help and get someone on the radio now:
see [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).

## Do now

1. **Protect yourself.** Wear gloves if you have them. Wash any blood off
    your own skin as soon as you can
    ([PHS, pp. 1-32 to 1-33](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
2. **Lay the casualty down.** People often faint from blood loss and pain
    ([PHS, p. 1-33](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
3. **Press hard on the wound** with a pad of clean cloth or gauze, using your
    hand. If there is nothing at hand, use your bare hand: stopping fast blood
    loss matters more than infection
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-31](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
4. **Hold the pressure for at least 4 to 6 minutes** without lifting to look
    ([PHS, p. 1-33](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
5. **If blood soaks through,** do not remove the first pad. Add more pads on
    top and press again
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-31](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
6. **Keep pressing on the wound itself.** Do not rely on raising the limb or
    on pressing "pressure points" instead; current first-aid guidelines no
    longer recommend them (see "Uncertain").
7. **Bandage firmly** over the pads, winding from the far end of the limb
    towards the heart. Check the pulse or the colour of fingers or toes
    beyond the bandage. Do not cut off the blood flow
    ([PHS, p. 1-33](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
8. **Life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that pressure does not
    stop** (spurting, pooling, or a limb torn off): apply a tourniquet.
    - Put it on the limb between the wound and the heart, as close to the
      wound as practical. Use it only on arms and legs, never on the head,
      neck or body
      ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-34](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    - Use a manufactured tourniquet if you carry one. Otherwise use a wide,
      flat band (belt, folded scarf), never rope, wire or string
      ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-34](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    - Tie it, put a stick across the knot, tie again, and twist until the
      bleeding stops. Secure the stick
      ([PHS, p. 1-33](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
    - Write "T" and the time on the casualty's forehead or a label. Do not
      cover it. Do not loosen it
      ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-34 to 4-35](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    - This person needs evacuation. Send a
      [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html) or PAN PAN at once.
9. **Treat for shock:** keep them lying down, warm, and calm. See
    [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html).

### Once bleeding has stopped: small wounds

1. Wash your hands. Rinse the wound well with clean (boiled and cooled, or
    bottled) water or saline and remove visible dirt
    ([PHS, p. 1-34](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
2. Cover with a non-stick pad and a bandage. Change it every day
    ([PHS, p. 1-34](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
3. Clean, straight cuts can be held closed with adhesive strips placed
    across the cut, with gaps between them. Never close a dirty or infected
    wound, a bite, or a deep wound that still bleeds inside
    ([PHS, p. 1-37](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
4. Large or dirty wounds: do not try to clean them deeply. Cover with a large
    dressing and get medical advice
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-35](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. Puncture wounds: let them bleed a little to wash them out; they carry a
    high risk of infection
    ([PHS, p. 1-34](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
6. Ask when the casualty last had a tetanus jab and tell the doctor
    ([PHS, p. 1-40](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

### Special cases

- **Object stuck in the wound:** do not pull it out if it is deep. Pad around
  it and bandage over the padding
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-29](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
- **Chest wound that sucks air or froths:** do not seal it airtight. Leave
  it open to the air, or cover it loosely with a clean, dry dressing that
  lets air out (or a vented chest seal if you carry one), and press beside
  it to control bleeding. If breathing gets worse after anything is put on
  the wound, take it off at once
  ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-38 to 4-39](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
  Sit the casualty half up and get medical advice by radio; prepare for
  evacuation. (The older airtight-seal method is explained under
  "Uncertain".)
- **Neck wound:** never wind a bandage round the neck
  ([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Severed finger or limb:** wrap it in a clean dressing and keep it in a
  cool container, not frozen, not in water and not in direct contact with
  ice ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-30](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
- **Fish hooks and fish spines:** see
  [Fish hooks and fish spines](fish-hook-injuries.html).

## Why

An adult has about 5 litres (10.5 US pints) of blood. Losing about 1 litre
usually causes shock, and fast loss of smaller amounts can too
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-31](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Direct pressure stops most bleeding. A tourniquet stops all blood to the
limb, so it is kept for bleeding that would otherwise kill. Recent first-aid
guidelines favour a manufactured tourniquet for such bleeding
([Wikipedia, 'Tourniquet'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

Wounds at sea often get infected because help is days away. Normal healing
shows less pain, swelling, heat and redness by day 2 or 3
([PHS, p. 1-36](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

## Check that it worked

- No fresh blood comes through the dressing.
- Fingers or toes beyond the bandage stay pink and warm.
- In the next days: pain and redness get less, not more.

## If that fails

- **Bleeding will not stop, or shock signs** (pale, cold, sweaty, fast weak
  pulse, thirst, restless): get medical advice now and prepare for
  evacuation. Shock can also come from bleeding inside the body, for example
  after a crush or a fall
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-34](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
- **Signs of infection** (more pain, swelling, heat, redness, pus, a bad
  smell, fever of 37.8 °C (100 °F) or more one or two days after the injury):
  open the dressing, rinse, keep the part raised, and ask a doctor by radio
  about antibiotics ([PHS, pp. 1-36 to 1-37](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- Do not try to stitch a wound unless a doctor tells you to and talks you
  through it; most wounds heal well without stitches
  ([PHS, p. 1-37](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
