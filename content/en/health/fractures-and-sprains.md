## Situation

After a fall, a blow from the boom, a crush between boat and quay, or a
wrench on a line, someone has pain, swelling or a misshapen limb and cannot
use it normally. You cannot X-ray it, so treat it as broken
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-46](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

**First:** if there is heavy bleeding or the person is not breathing normally,
deal with that first: see [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html).

## Do now

1. **Keep the casualty still** where they are, unless the place is dangerous.
    Pad and secure them so the boat's motion does not roll them about
    ([FM 21-22, p. 95](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
2. **Neck or back pain, numbness or tingling, or weak arms or legs after a
    fall or blow:** assume a broken spine. Keep the head in line with the
    body. Do not let them sit up, bend or twist. Move them only if their life
    is in danger, and then as one piece with several helpers
    ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-52 to 4-53](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Get [medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html) now.
3. **Open wound over the break:** cover it with a clean dressing and stop the
    bleeding with direct pressure on the wound. Do not push bone back in ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-47](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
    [FM 21-22, p. 98](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
4. **Check the hand or foot beyond the injury:** colour, warmth, feeling,
    pulse. Write it down.
5. **Splint it as you find it.** Do not try to straighten a bent arm or leg
    unless the doctor on the radio tells you to (see "Uncertain"). The navy
    manual allows a gentle pull along its length with the hands to line it up
    for a splint; if you do this, stop if it hurts much more or resists. Never force it.
    If the injury is at the elbow, do not straighten it
    ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-47 to 4-48](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
6. **Use what is aboard:** an oar or paddle, a board or batten, stiff
    cardboard, a folded newspaper or chart, padded with clothing or a
    blanket
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-45](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    The splint must be padded and long enough to reach past the joint above
    and the joint below. A broken leg can be tied to the good leg with padding
    between them.
7. **Tie the splint firmly but not tight.** Leave fingers and toes showing.
    Check them every half hour: if they turn blue, cold or numb, loosen the
    ties ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-45](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Wet bandages shrink and limbs swell
    ([FM 21-22, pp. 98 to 99](../library/fm-21-22-survival-at-sea-1950.html)).
8. **Arm:** after splinting, support it in a sling across the chest, hand a
    little higher than the elbow
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-48](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
9. **Treat for shock and keep warm.** A broken thigh or pelvis can bleed a
    lot inside ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-48 and 4-53](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
10. **Call a doctor by radio** for any suspected break; every fracture needs
    medical treatment ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-46](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

### Sprains and strains

If you are sure nothing is broken (the person can bear some weight and there
is no deformity), use **rest, cold, a firm bandage and raising the limb**:

- cold packs for 10 to 15 minutes at a time, several times a day, wrapped in
  cloth ([Wikipedia, 'Sprain'](../library/wikipedia-en.html));
- a firm figure-of-eight bandage, checking it does not cut off the blood
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-55](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html));
- after 24 to 48 hours, when swelling stops, gentle warmth and gentle
  movement help healing
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-55](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
  [Wikipedia, 'Sprain'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

Treat a sprain as a possible fracture until a doctor has ruled it out
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-55](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

## Why

Signs of a fracture are pain, tenderness, swelling, bruising, a limb that
looks wrong or moves where it should not, or a grating feeling. A cracked bone
can still be moved, so being able to move it does not rule out a break
([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-46 to 4-47](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
A splint stops the sharp bone ends moving, which reduces pain, bleeding and
damage to nerves and vessels
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-45](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Rough handling can turn a closed fracture into an open one
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-46](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
A dislocation looks deformed **at** the joint and the joint is locked; a
fracture is deformed **between** joints
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-54](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

## Check that it worked

- Pain eases once the limb is splinted and still.
- Fingers or toes stay pink, warm and can feel touch.
- The casualty is secured so the motion of the boat does not jolt the limb.

## If that fails

- **No pulse, cold or numb hand or foot beyond the injury:** the blood supply
  may be cut off. Tell the doctor at once; this is urgent.
- **Broken thigh, pelvis, spine or neck, open fracture, or signs of shock:**
  ask for evacuation. Do not move the casualty until the limb is splinted,
  unless life is at risk
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-48](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
- **Long wait for help:** if days pass, keep the joints that are not injured
  moving gently twice a day to prevent stiffness, but do not move fractured
  areas ([PHS, p. 1-39](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
