## Situation

Someone on board is ill or injured and you are not sure what to do, or you
think they may need a doctor or an evacuation. At sea, a doctor can be
reached by radio, usually free of charge, through the coastguard.

Call **early**. A ship's medicine guide written for crews says that for any
serious illness or injury the person caring for the patient should contact a
doctor ashore and should not hesitate to use the radio for advice
([PHS, p. xiv and p. 1-2](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

**If life is in immediate danger and you need rescue now, send a Mayday
instead: see [Mayday: calling for help](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).**

## Do now

1. Give first aid for anything that threatens life first: breathing, severe
    bleeding. See [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html).
2. Note the time. Take and write down pulse, breathing rate and, if you have
    a thermometer, temperature.
3. Call the coastguard. On VHF use channel 16 (156.8 MHz), the international
    distress, safety and calling channel; a DSC call is made on channel 70
    ([Pub. 117, pp. 4-4 to 4-5](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).
4. Use the urgency call
    ([Pub. 117, p. 5-3](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)):

    > PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN
    >
    > All stations, all stations, all stations (or the coastguard station's name)
    >
    > This is (boat name) three times, call sign (call sign), MMSI (your MMSI, if you have one)
    >
    > In position (position)
    >
    > I require medical advice
    >
    > Over

5. When the coastguard answers, they will move you to a working channel or
    set up a call with a doctor. Answer their questions about the boat and the
    patient.
6. Pass the patient details in a fixed order (see the list below). Speak
    slowly. Spell names.
7. Write down the advice, the time, and any medicine the doctor tells you to
    give, with the dose. Read it back.
8. Agree when you will call again, and call back at once if the patient gets
    worse.

### What the doctor will ask

The Italian radio medical centre (CIRM) lists the information it needs
([Pub. 117, p. 5-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)):

| About the boat | About the patient |
| --- | --- |
| Boat name and call sign | Date of birth (age), sex |
| Position, port of departure, destination, ETA | Temperature, blood pressure if you can, pulse, breathing rate |
| What medicines and first-aid kit you carry | When it started; what the symptoms are; where the pain is |
| | Other illnesses, allergies, regular medicines |
| | For an injury: where and how it happened |
| | What you have already given or done |

Have your medicine chest list next to the radio. The doctor can only
prescribe what you carry; see [The medicine chest](medicine-chest.html).
CIRM asks crews to call **before** giving medicines
([Pub. 117, p. 5-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).

## Why

A doctor on the radio can judge how serious it is, tell you what to give from
your own kit, and decide with the skipper whether an evacuation is worth the
risk. The decision to evacuate is the skipper's, based on the doctor's
advice; weather, sea state and helicopter range all count
([Pub. 117, p. 5-4](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)). Medical calls are
treated as safety or urgency traffic and normally cost nothing
([Pub. 117, p. 5-3](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).

## Check that it worked

- You have a written note of the advice, timed, and you have read it back.
- You know when to call back and on which channel or number.
- You are recording pulse, breathing and temperature at fixed times. A navy
  manual advises every 15 minutes for a seriously ill patient
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-24](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
  Keep a simple log (time, findings, treatment), as the PHS guide advises
  ([PHS, pp. 1-40 to 1-41](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

## If that fails

- **No answer on VHF:** you may be out of range. Try a coast radio station,
  MF/HF radio, satellite phone or a mobile phone to the national coastguard.
  In the UK, call "UK Coastguard" and ask for medical advice; in the US, any
  Coast Guard rescue centre; in France the national medical service is
  CCMM in Toulouse
  ([Pub. 117, section 500B](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)). When in
  doubt, call any rescue centre (MRCC); the list itself says so.
- **Patient gets much worse, stops breathing, or there is a danger to the
  boat:** send a [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
- **Language problems:** the International Code of Signals has a medical
  section for this ([Pub. 117, p. 5-3](../library/nga-pub-117-2014.html)).
- Meanwhile keep giving first aid: see
  [Medical emergency at sea](../emergency/medical-emergency-at-sea.html).
