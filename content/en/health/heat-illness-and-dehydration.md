## Situation

Someone has been working in a hot engine room or galley, or in strong sun on
deck, or has been vomiting or had diarrhoea, and now feels weak, dizzy, sick,
or has cramps. Or worse: they are hot, confused, or have collapsed. That is
heat stroke, and it kills.

## Do now

### Hot, confused, very ill, or collapsed: heat stroke

1. **This is an emergency.** Get medical advice by radio now and plan for
    evacuation ([PHS, p. 1-22](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)): see
    [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
2. **Cool them fast.** Move them to the coolest shaded place, take off as much
    clothing as possible, soak them with cool or cold water and fan them hard
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
    [Wikipedia, 'Heat stroke'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
3. **Cold packs or cold wet cloths** on the neck, armpits and groin
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Immersing the body in cold water is the fastest method if you can do it
    safely; never immerse an unconscious person unless the head is held
    clear of the water ([Wikipedia, 'Heat stroke'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
4. **Airway:** if they are unconscious, keep the airway open and lay them on
    their side. Give nothing by mouth.
5. **If they can drink,** give cool water. No alcohol, no hot drinks
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
6. **Stop cooling at about 39 °C (102 °F)** if you can measure it, and check
    every 10 minutes; start again if it rises
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
    [Wikipedia, 'Heat stroke'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
    Keep cooling during transfer.

### Weak, dizzy, sweaty, pale: heat exhaustion

1. Lay them down in a cool place, feet slightly up. Loosen clothing
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-60](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. Cool wet cloths and a fan, but do not let them get chilled
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-60](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. If fully awake and not vomiting, give an oral rehydration drink in sips.
4. No more heat exposure for several days
    ([PHS, p. 1-22](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

### Cramps after hard work in heat

Rest in a cool place and drink fluids such as juice or an electrolyte drink
([PHS, pp. 1-21 to 1-22](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

### Oral rehydration drink

Use ready-made rehydration sachets if you carry them. Otherwise the PHS guide
gives this approximation of the WHO formula, per 1 litre (1 US quart) of
clean water ([PHS, p. 1-15](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)):

- 1½ tablespoons of sugar;
- ½ teaspoon of table salt;
- ¾ teaspoon of baking soda;
- ¼ teaspoon of salt substitute (potassium chloride).

Give it in frequent small sips. If the person vomits, wait 5 to 10 minutes
and start again slowly
([Wikipedia, 'Oral rehydration therapy'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Why

Sweating in a hot working space can lose up to 1.5 litres (3 US pints) of
water an hour, and people working in heat are often already 3 per cent
dehydrated without knowing it
([PHS, p. 1-14](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). Sugar helps the gut
take up salt and water, which is why the drink contains both
([PHS, p. 1-15](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

| | Heat exhaustion | Heat stroke |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skin | Cool, moist, clammy, grey | Hot; often dry, but may still sweat after hard work |
| Mind | Tired, irritable, may faint | Confused, delirious, fitting or unconscious |
| Temperature | Near normal | Above 40 to 41 °C (104 to 105 °F) |
| Danger | Can turn into heat stroke | Life-threatening; high death rate even with care |

(After [PHS, pp. 1-21 to 1-22](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
[Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-60 to 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html).)

## Check that it worked

- The person becomes clear-headed and their temperature falls.
- Pulse slows; dizziness on standing goes.
- They pass light-yellow urine again
  ([PHS, p. 1-16](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

## If that fails

- **Still confused, temperature rising again, fits, or unconscious:** treat as
  heat stroke, keep cooling, and ask for evacuation. Even if the temperature
  returns to normal, a person who had heat stroke still needs a doctor:
  liver, kidney and heart damage can follow
  ([PHS, p. 1-22](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Cannot keep fluids down, or dizzy and faint on standing:** they may need
  fluids by drip. Ask a doctor by radio; have pulse and blood pressure lying,
  sitting and standing ready to report
  ([PHS, pp. 1-15 to 1-16](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Prevention:** drink water regularly in heat, cover up and use sunscreen,
  cut alcohol, and do not use salt tablets
  unless a doctor says so
  ([PHS, p. 1-21](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
  [Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-61](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
