## Situation

A person has been underwater, or face-down in the water, and is now aboard.
They may be unconscious, not breathing, coughing, or seem fine. Anyone who
has breathed in water needs watching, even if they recover quickly.

## Do now

1. **Lift them out carefully**, horizontally if possible. Protect the neck if
    they may have hit something, for example after a fall or a dive
    ([PHS, pp. 10-4 and 10-6](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
2. **Shout for help.** One person to the radio: send a
    [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html) if they are not breathing,
    or get [medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
3. **Check breathing.** In a cold casualty, check for breathing and pulse for
    up to a full minute: they can be very slow
    ([PHS, p. 10-6](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). If they are
    plainly not breathing, do not wait out the minute: go to rescue breaths
    (step 5).
4. **Clear the mouth** of vomit, weed or other objects. Do **not** press on
    the stomach or try to drain water out of the lungs
    ([PHS, p. 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
5. **Not breathing: start with 5 rescue breaths.** Tilt the head back, lift the
    chin, pinch the nose and blow into the mouth until the chest rises
    ([Wikipedia, 'Drowning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Then
    do CPR: 30 chest compressions, 5 to 6 cm (2 to 2.4 in) deep at 100 to 120 a
    minute, then 2 breaths, and repeat
    ([Wikipedia, 'Cardiopulmonary resuscitation'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
    Breaths matter most in drowning because the heart stops from lack of
    oxygen. If you are alone, do about 2 minutes of CPR before you leave to
    use the radio ([Wikipedia, 'Cardiopulmonary resuscitation'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
6. **Give oxygen** if you carry it and know how to use it
    ([PHS, p. 10-6](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
7. **Use a defibrillator (AED)** if you carry one; follow its voice
    instructions ([Wikipedia, 'Cardiopulmonary resuscitation'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
8. **Breathing but unconscious:** put them on their side, head low, so
    vomit can drain, and watch them all the time
    ([Wikipedia, 'Recovery position'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
9. **Keep them warm.** Remove wet clothes, wrap them, and rewarm while CPR
    goes on ([PHS, p. 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). See
    [Hypothermia](hypothermia.html).
10. **Do not give up early.** Keep CPR going until help takes over, you
    cannot go on, or a doctor tells you to stop.

## Why

The main problem in drowning is lack of oxygen. When water reaches the throat
the vocal cords close; the person cannot breathe and loses consciousness.
Water may then enter the lungs
([PHS, p. 10-6](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). Getting oxygen
back in is the most important part of the rescue
([PHS, p. 10-6](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)), which is why
rescue breaths come first.

The brain normally survives 4 to 6 minutes without oxygen. In cold water,
survival after much longer under water has been recorded, up to 60 minutes,
because the cold protects the brain. So people pulled from cold water should
be resuscitated hard and not declared dead just because they were under for
more than a few minutes. No one should be declared dead until they have been
rewarmed and still do not respond
([PHS, pp. 10-6 to 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
[PHS, p. 1-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

Water in the lungs cannot be pumped out by pressing on the stomach; doing so
wastes time and makes vomiting and choking more likely. Old methods you may
read about in this library, such as abdominal thrusts, prone pressure or
rocking the casualty on a stretcher, are obsolete
([PHS, p. 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

## Check that it worked

- The chest rises with each rescue breath.
- The person starts to breathe on their own, coughs, moves or opens their
  eyes. Put them on their side and keep watching.
- Breathing rate, pulse and level of response are written down every
  15 minutes.

## If that fails

- **Chest does not rise:** check the head tilt and the mouth again. If a solid object blocks the airway, treat it as choking; that
  is the only case for abdominal thrusts
  ([PHS, p. 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **The person recovers:** they still need medical advice. The PHS guide
  recommends transfer to medical care after near drowning, especially if the
  boat cannot give advanced care
  ([PHS, p. 10-7](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Several people in the water:** see
  [Person in cold water](../emergency/person-in-cold-water.html) and
  [Man overboard](../emergency/man-overboard.html).
