## Situation

People below decks have a headache, feel dizzy, sick, weak or confused, or
someone is found drowsy or unconscious in a cabin, engine room or under a
cockpit tent. Sources include a cabin heater, gas or paraffin cooker,
charcoal barbecue, generator, outboard or engine exhaust, or a neighbouring
boat's exhaust
([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 5-7 to 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
[Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
A carbon monoxide alarm may be sounding.

Carbon monoxide (CO) has no smell, no taste and no colour. Poisoning can
happen quickly and be fatal
([MCA, p. 27](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Do now

1. **Do not become the next victim.** Never go into a space with fumes unless
    you have breathing apparatus and are trained to use it. A wet cloth over
    the face gives no protection
    ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 3-16 and 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
2. **Shut off the source** if you can do so from outside: stop the engine or
    generator, turn off the heater or cooker at the supply.
3. **Ventilate.** Open hatches and ports from outside and run fans that blow
    fresh air in. If you also smell gas (LPG), do not touch any switch or fan:
    go to [Smell of gas (LPG)](../emergency/gas-leak.html) first.
4. **Get people out into fresh air** as soon as it is safe, and keep them
    quiet and at rest
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. **Check breathing.** If they are not breathing, start CPR: see
    [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html). If unconscious
    but breathing, put them on their side.
6. **Give oxygen** if you carry it and know how to use it
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html);
    [Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
7. **Keep them warm and treat for shock**
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
8. **Get medical advice by radio** for anyone who was unconscious, confused,
    short of breath, has chest pain, or is pregnant; and ideally for everyone
    exposed. See [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
    Ask about evacuation.
9. **Check everyone else on board,** including anyone asleep.
10. **Do not use the appliance or engine again** until the cause is found and
    fixed.

## Why

CO is made when fuel burns without enough air. It sticks to the blood far
more strongly than oxygen does, so the blood can no longer carry oxygen
([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
The early signs look like flu: headache, dizziness, weakness, vomiting,
chest pain and confusion; larger doses cause collapse, fits and death
([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
People often feel only slight dizziness and headache before they pass out
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 5-8](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Boat engines and generators, notably on houseboats, have caused deaths
([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

An ordinary finger pulse oximeter does not help: it cannot tell CO-loaded
blood from oxygen-loaded blood and may read normal
([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
Some people develop memory, mood or movement problems days to weeks later,
which is another reason to see a doctor after serious exposure
([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Check that it worked

- Headache and dizziness ease within hours in fresh air.
- The CO alarm stops and stays quiet with the space ventilated.
- Breathing and level of response are normal and stay normal; note them every
  15 minutes for anyone who was badly affected.

## If that fails

- **Still drowsy, confused, or getting worse:** this is serious. Keep giving
  oxygen if you have it, and arrange evacuation. If they stop breathing, send
  a [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
- **Fire on board:** smoke contains CO. See
  [Fire on board](../emergency/fire-on-board.html).
- **Smell of gas (LPG):** that is a different danger (explosion). See
  [Smell of gas (LPG)](../emergency/gas-leak.html).

### Prevent it

- Fit a CO alarm in every space with a cooker or heater or where exhaust runs
  through living spaces, and test it regularly. An alarm does not replace
  proper fitting and servicing of appliances
  ([MCA, p. 27](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- Warning signs: sooty stains around an appliance or flue; a flame that is
  yellow or orange and "floppy"; an appliance that is hard to light or keep
  lit; smelling exhaust inside the boat
  ([MCA, p. 27](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
- Never run a generator, barbecue or portable heater in a closed cabin or
  under a cockpit cover. Keep exhaust systems in good repair
  ([Wikipedia, 'Carbon monoxide poisoning'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
