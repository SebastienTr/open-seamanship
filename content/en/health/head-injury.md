## Situation

Someone has been hit on the head (by the boom, a block, a hatch, a swinging
load) or has fallen and struck their head. They may have been knocked out,
or seem fine. The danger is bleeding or swelling inside the skull, which can
show up hours later.

## Do now

1. **Check airway and breathing first.** If unconscious and breathing, turn
    them on their side, keeping head, neck and body in line. See
    [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html).
2. **Assume a neck injury** with any significant head or face injury: keep
    the head still and in line with the body
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-52](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
3. **Control scalp bleeding** with direct pressure, gently, taking care not
    to press a broken skull inwards
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-52](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
4. **Leave anything stuck in the skull in place.** Pad around it with a ring
    bandage and get the casualty evacuated
    ([PHS, p. 1-35](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
    [Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-52](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
5. **Keep them lying down,** warm but not hot. Watch for vomiting and turn
    them on their side if they vomit
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-52](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
6. **Give nothing to drink and no medicines** unless a doctor says so
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-52](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Painkillers that cause drowsiness hide the warning signs and can slow
    breathing ([PHS, p. 1-24](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
7. **Get medical advice by radio** for any head injury where the person was
    knocked out, even briefly, or has any danger sign below: see
    [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
8. **Watch and record** every 15 minutes at first: are they awake, do they
    know who and where they are, can they move both arms and both legs, are
    the pupils the same size
    ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-4, 4-24 and 4-51](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Wake them at intervals, even at night, to check
    ([PHS, p. 1-24](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

### Danger signs: call for evacuation

- Getting drowsier or harder to wake; confusion that gets worse.
- A headache that keeps getting worse; vomiting again and again.
- A fit (seizure).
- Pupils of different sizes.
- Weakness, numbness or clumsiness of an arm or leg; slurred speech; double
  vision; unsteady walking.
- Bleeding from the ears, nose or mouth.

([Wikipedia, 'Concussion', 'Diagnosis' and 'Treatment'](../library/wikipedia-en.html);
[Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-51](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html).)

## Why

With a head injury, the main danger is to the brain, not the skull. A blow
that does not break the skull can still damage the brain, and the signs may
be greatly delayed
([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-51](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Some people are knocked out, wake up and seem normal (a "lucid interval"),
then get worse as blood collects inside the skull
([Wikipedia, 'Head injury'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). On a boat there
is no scan, so the only tool you have is careful watching, and the doctor on
the radio needs your notes.

## Check that it worked

- The person stays awake or wakes easily, and knows the day, place and what
  happened.
- Headache steadily eases; no new vomiting.
- Your notes show no change for the worse over several hours of watching
  ([Wikipedia, 'Concussion', 'Treatment'](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## If that fails

- **Any danger sign:** call the coastguard and ask for medical advice and
  evacuation. If they become unconscious, send a
  [Mayday](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html).
- **Neck pain, or tingling or weakness in the arms or legs:** treat as a
  spinal injury: see [Broken bones and sprains](fractures-and-sprains.html).
- **Fit (seizure):** protect them from injuring themselves, then put them on
  their side when it stops, because there is a real risk of breathing in
  vomit or saliva. Report it to the doctor; a seizure after a head injury
  needs evacuation
  ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-69 to 4-70](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
