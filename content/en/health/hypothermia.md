## Situation

A person is cold, wet and shivering, or has been pulled out of the water, or
has spent hours on deck or in a liferaft in wind and spray. Hypothermia means
the body core is below normal. It can kill even after rescue.

If the person was in the water, also read
[Person in cold water](../emergency/person-in-cold-water.html) and
[Near drowning](near-drowning.html).

## Do now

1. **Lift them out horizontally** if you can, or lay them flat as soon as
    they are aboard. Do not make them climb, stand or walk
    ([PHS, p. 10-4](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
2. **Handle gently.** Rough handling can stop a cold heart
    ([PHS, pp. 10-5 and 1-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
    Do not lift them by the arms or legs
    ([MCA, p. 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
3. **Check breathing and pulse for up to a full minute.** A cold person can have a
    very slow, weak pulse and slow, shallow breathing
    ([PHS, pp. 1-18 and 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
    - Plainly not breathing: do not wait out the minute. If they were under
      water, start with 5 rescue breaths: see [Near drowning](near-drowning.html).
    - No breathing and no pulse: start CPR (see
      [First aid: what to do first](first-aid-priorities.html)).
    - Any pulse at all, even very slow: **do not** start chest compressions
      ([PHS, p. 1-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). If they
      have a pulse but are not breathing, give rescue breaths only.
4. **Get them out of the wind** into the warmest sheltered place.
5. **Decide how cold they are:**
    - **Alert and shivering hard (mild):** remove wet clothing, dry them,
      wrap in blankets or a sleeping bag with a windproof layer (plastic sheet,
      survival bag) and cover the head and neck
      ([PHS, p. 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html);
      [MCA, p. 75](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
      Give sugar, glucose or a warm sweet drink if they can swallow safely.
      No alcohol ([MCA, pp. 75 to 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
    - **Not shivering, drowsy, confused or unconscious (moderate or severe):**
      keep them lying down and level; if they are drowsy or unconscious,
      lay them level on their side (recovery position)
      ([MCA, p. 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)). Do not let
      them sit, stand or move. Nothing by mouth. Do not put them in a shower or bath
      ([PHS, p. 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). If they are
      barely conscious, do not struggle to undress them: wrap them, wet clothes
      and all, in blankets and a plastic sheet, and put them on their side
      ([MCA, pp. 75 to 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).
6. **Add gentle heat to the trunk:** one or two people lying close to them,
    or hot-water bottles and heat packs **wrapped in cloth**, on the body
    rather than the arms and legs
    ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-62](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
    Never put a hot object on bare cold skin: it burns easily
    ([PHS, p. 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
7. **Call a doctor by radio** ([PHS, p. 1-19](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)):
    see [Getting medical advice by radio](getting-medical-advice-by-radio.html).
    For anyone not shivering or not fully alert, ask about evacuation.
8. **Never leave them alone.** Watch breathing all the time
    ([MCA, p. 76](../library/mca-fishermens-safety-guide.html)).

## Why

| Stage | Core temperature | Signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild | 32 to 35 °C (90 to 95 °F) | Strong shivering, clumsy hands, stumbling, slurred speech, poor judgement |
| Moderate | 28 to 32 °C (82 to 90 °F) | Shivering stops, slow and irregular pulse, may lose consciousness |
| Severe | below 28 °C (82 °F) | No reflexes, breathing and heartbeat may stop |

(After [PHS, pp. 1-18 and 10-1 to 10-2](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html).)

Ordinary thermometers often read only down to about 34 °C (94 °F). If you
have no low-reading thermometer, treat as severe a person who is very cold
but not shivering, drowsy or confused, or who cannot walk or talk properly
([PHS, p. 1-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

The water pressed on the body while in the sea supports blood pressure.
Lifting someone out upright can make it fall suddenly; this is thought to
cause deaths just after rescue. The core may also keep cooling after rescue
("afterdrop"), and exercise makes this worse: in one test, walking made it
three times larger than lying still
([PHS, pp. 10-4 to 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). Warm the
body core first; warming the limbs first can cause a dangerous fall in blood
pressure ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-62](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
Shivering itself is a strong heat source, so
help a shivering person keep it up with sugar and insulation
([PHS, p. 10-5](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).

## Check that it worked

- A mildly cold person shivers strongly, then less, becomes clear-headed and
  talks normally.
- Pulse and breathing stay steady. Note them every 15 minutes
  ([Hospital Corpsman, p. 4-24](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).
- Skin of hands and feet: look for frostbite (white, hard, numb). Do not rub
  it; rewarm it gently against warm skin
  ([Hospital Corpsman, pp. 4-63 to 4-64](../library/navedtra-14295-hospital-corpsman.html)).

## If that fails

- **Getting drowsier, shivering stops, or the pulse becomes irregular:** this
  is moderate or severe hypothermia. Keep them lying level (on their side
  if not fully conscious), insulate, warm the trunk
  only, and arrange evacuation through the coastguard.
- **No pulse:** CPR may be needed for a long time. A cold person must not be
  declared dead until they have been rewarmed
  ([PHS, p. 1-18](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)).
- **Prevention next time:** dress for the weather, stay dry, eat, drink, avoid
  alcohol, and treat shivering as a warning
  ([PHS, p. 1-19](../library/phs-ships-medicine-chest.html)). Carry a
  low-reading thermometer, blankets and hot-water bottles.

See also [Surviving in a liferaft](survival-in-a-liferaft.html) and
[Lifejackets and buoyancy aids](../run-the-boat/lifejackets.html).
