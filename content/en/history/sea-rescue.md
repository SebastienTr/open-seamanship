For most of history a ship in trouble near the coast was on its own. People
ashore could see a wreck in the surf and do nothing. Organised sea rescue is
barely 250 years old.

## The first lifeboats

The first lifeboat station in Britain opened at Formby, near Liverpool, in
1776. On 2 November 1785 Lionel Lukin, a London coachbuilder, patented an
"unimmergible" boat: sealed air spaces along the sides and in the ends kept it
afloat when swamped, and a heavy iron keel kept it upright. Those principles
were used in most lifeboats until the 1960s
([Wikipedia: Lifeboat (rescue); Lionel Lukin](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

On 29 January 1790 Henry Greathead's *Original* was tried on the River Tyne.
It was 8.5 m (28 ft) long, rowed by up to 12 men in cork jackets, and 31 more
were built to the same design. William Wouldhave entered a self-righting
design in the same competition, but self-righting boats were not used until
the 1840s. In 1851 the Beeching-Peake self-righter became the standard boat
of the British national service
([Wikipedia: Lifeboat (rescue); Henry Greathead](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## National services

**Britain.** After wrecks on the Isle of Man in 1822, William Hillary
called for a national institution. It was founded on 4 March 1824 and took
the name Royal National Lifeboat Institution in 1854. Its crews were, and
mostly still are, unpaid volunteers
([Wikipedia: Royal National Lifeboat Institution](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
On 7 September 1838 the steamer *Forfarshire* broke up on the Farne Islands.
Grace Darling, the keeper's daughter, and her father William rowed a coble
nearly a mile (1.5 km) through rough water to the wreck; nine people were saved
([Wikipedia: Grace Darling](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

**United States.** The Massachusetts Humane Society built the first American
lifeboat station, at Cohasset. Federal money followed on 14 August 1848.
The Life-Saving Service launched heavy boats from open beaches, and its
1899 regulations gave crews their unofficial motto: "You have to go out, but
you don't have to come back." On 28 January 1915 it merged with the Revenue
Cutter Service to form the US Coast Guard, with more than 270 stations
([Wikipedia: United States Life-Saving Service](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

**France.** The first French society was formed at Boulogne-sur-Mer in 1825,
followed by Dunkirk in 1834 and other ports
([Wikipedia (fr): SNSM, Histoire](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). After the
loss of the *Amphitrite* at Boulogne in 1833 and the frigate *Sémillante* in
1855, the Société centrale de sauvetage des naufragés was founded on
12 February 1865. By 1883 it ran 70 lifeboat stations and 150 rocket
line-throwing posts. In 1873 the Hospitaliers sauveteurs bretons were founded
in Rennes by Henri Nadault de Buffon
([Wikipedia (fr): SCSN; Hospitaliers-Sauveteurs bretons](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).
In 1967 the two merged into the Société nationale de sauvetage en mer (SNSM),
a volunteer association recognised as being of public utility in 1970
([Wikipedia (fr): SNSM](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

## Engines change everything

| Year | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 1890 | First steam lifeboat in Britain, *Duke of Northumberland* |
| 1899 | First American "motor life boat", 12 hp, on Lake Superior |
| 1908 | First purpose-built British motor lifeboats |
| 1918 | RNLI: 233 pulling and sailing boats, 23 powered |
| 1939 | RNLI: only 15 unpowered boats left |

Sources: [Wikipedia: Lifeboat (rescue); Royal National Lifeboat Institution](../library/wikipedia-en.html).

Range grew so much that fewer stations were needed: 145 motor lifeboats did
the work. The first engines ran on petrol; diesel took over because it used
less fuel and went further
([Wikipedia: Royal National Lifeboat Institution](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
See [The marine diesel engine](the-marine-diesel.html).

Medicine changed too. Luce's *Seamanship* of 1866 prints the RNLI's
instructions for "restoring the apparently drowned", with hot flannels and
warm brandy ([Luce, pp. 361-362](../library/luce-seamanship-1866.html)).
They show how seriously the problem was taken, but they are long out of date.
For today's method see [Near drowning](../health/near-drowning.html).

## Coordination: from shore watch to CROSS

Lifeboats are only half the system; someone must raise the alarm and direct
the search. In 1924 the RNLI's centenary conference founded the International
Lifeboat Federation, now the International Maritime Rescue Federation
([Wikipedia: Royal National Lifeboat Institution](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
The International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue, adopted on
27 April 1979 and in force from 22 June 1985, divided the oceans into search
and rescue regions, each with a rescue coordination centre
([Wikipedia: International Convention on Maritime Search and Rescue](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
Morse distress watches, whose need was recognised after the *Titanic* sank in
1912, gave way
to the satellite and DSC-based GMDSS, fully in force for ships on
1 February 1999 ([Wikipedia: GMDSS](../library/wikipedia-en.html)); see
[GMDSS and DSC](../run-the-boat/gmdss-and-dsc.html).

France built its coordination centres in the same years. A regional centre at
Lorient, set up in 1966, became the CROSS Atlantique at Étel in 1968, and the
CROSS name was made official in 1967
([Wikipedia (fr): CROSS, Historique](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). Today
SNSM boats go out at the request of the CROSS
([Wikipedia (fr): SNSM](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)); see
[France: CROSS, SNSM and calling for help](../regions/france-search-and-rescue.html)
and [UK: HM Coastguard and the RNLI](../regions/uk-search-and-rescue.html).

## The cost

Rescue crews still die at sea. On 7 August 1986 five SNSM volunteers from
L'Aber-Wrac'h drowned after going to the aid of a yacht. On 8 June 2019 the
Sables-d'Olonne lifeboat capsized in storm Miguel while going to a fishing
boat, and three of her crew died
([Wikipedia (fr): SNSM, Pertes humaines](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

The best way to honour them is to need them less: carry and wear a
[lifejacket](../run-the-boat/lifejackets.html), know how to make a
[Mayday call](../emergency/mayday-distress-call.html), and call early, while
help can still arrive in time.
