A light on a headland is one of the oldest aids to navigation. For most of
history it marked a harbour entrance. Only in the last three centuries did
lights go out onto the rocks themselves, to warn ships off.

## Ancient towers

The Pharos of Alexandria was built under Ptolemy II (280-247 BC) and may have
been at least 100 m (330 ft) tall. It was ruined by earthquakes between 956 and
1323 ([Wikipedia: Lighthouse of Alexandria](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
The Tower of Hercules at A Coruña in Spain, a Roman tower of the late 1st
century, is the only fully preserved Roman lighthouse still in use
([Wikipedia: Tower of Hercules](../library/wikipedia-en.html)). Ancient lights
burned wood or coal on a platform, and served mainly as harbour markers
([Wikipedia: Lighthouse](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

France's oldest lighthouse, Cordouan, stands at sea 7 km (4.3 miles) off the
mouth of the Gironde. It was built between 1584 and 1611 to a design by Louis
de Foix ([Wikipedia: Cordouan Lighthouse](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
In England, Henry VIII granted a charter to Trinity House on 20 May 1514
([Wikipedia: Trinity House](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Eddystone: building on a rock

The Eddystone rocks, a major hazard in the western Channel, show how hard it was to build in
the open sea ([Wikipedia: Eddystone Lighthouse](../library/wikipedia-en.html)):

| Tower | Built | Fate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winstanley (wood) | 1696-1698 | Swept away in the Great Storm of 1703 |
| Rudyerd | completed 1709 | Burned, 2 December 1755 |
| Smeaton (granite) | 1756-1759 | Rock below eroded; top moved ashore to Plymouth Hoe |
| Douglass (granite) | lit 18 May 1882 | Still in use |

Winstanley's was the first tower fully exposed to the open sea. John Smeaton
dovetailed granite blocks together, used a lime mortar that sets under water,
and curved the tower like an oak trunk; his design became the model for
later rock lighthouses. Robert Stevenson built on it for the Bell Rock in
1810 ([Wikipedia: Lighthouse, Modern construction](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## Better light: lamps and lenses

Until 1782 lighthouses burned wood or coal. The Argand oil lamp of 1782 gave a
steady, smokeless flame. Reflectors came in 1777; focusing the light made
revolving beams, and so flashing characters, possible
([Wikipedia: Lighthouse](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

The great step was French. Augustin Fresnel designed a lens built of
concentric rings and prisms: thin, light, and able to catch far more of the
lamp's light. The first was installed at Cordouan in 1823 and could be seen
more than 20 miles (32 km) away; it made the light about four times brighter
([Wikipedia: Lighthouse, Optical systems](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
Electric arc lamps followed at South Foreland in 1875, and in the early 1900s
Gustaf Dalén's gas light with a "sun valve" that switched off by day made
unwatched lights practical
([Wikipedia: Lighthouse, Lighting improvements](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

## The Breton rock lights

The waters off western Brittany, where the Channel meets the Atlantic, are
full of reefs and strong tides. After the paddle corvette *Sané* was wrecked on
the Chaussée de Sein in 1859, the French lighthouse commission decided to
build at its seaward end. A lightship was impossible there. Work on the rock
of Ar-Men began in 1867; in the first season, crews landed nine times and
worked 8 hours in total. The light was first lit in 1881, and the tower was
strengthened again from 1897 to 1902. Keepers called it "the hell of hells"
([Wikipedia (fr): Phare d'Ar-Men](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)).

Others followed on the approaches to Brest and the Raz de Sein
([Wikipedia (fr)](../library/wikipedia-fr.html), articles on each light):

| Light | Built | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Créac'h, Ouessant | 1863 | Landfall light at the entrance to the Channel |
| Ar-Men, Chaussée de Sein | 1867-1881 | Keepers removed 1990 |
| La Vieille, Raz de Sein | 1882-1887 | Automated 1995 |
| Eckmühl, Penmarc'h | lit 1897 | Over 60 m tall, paid for largely by a donation |
| La Jument, Ouessant | 1904-1911 | Built from a private legacy, marks the Fromveur passage |
| Kéréon, Fromveur | 1907-1916 | Paid for largely by a gift; luxurious interior |

For the coast these lights guard, see
[Brittany: the coast, tides and races](../regions/brittany-coast.html) and
[Breton seafarers](breton-seafarers.html).

## The end of the keepers

Electric light and automatic lamp changers made keepers unnecessary
([Wikipedia: Lighthouse, Modern lighthouses](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).
Ar-Men's last two keepers were lifted off by helicopter on 10 April 1990
([Wikipedia (fr): Phare d'Ar-Men](../library/wikipedia-fr.html)). Trinity House
completed its automation programme on 26 November 1998
([Wikipedia: Trinity House](../library/wikipedia-en.html)).

Today most major lights run unattended, and LED optics are replacing many
Fresnel lenses. Bowditch adds a practical warning: give offshore lights a
wide berth when you have sea room
([Bowditch, section 812](../library/bowditch.html)). To use them, see
[Reading a nautical chart](../run-the-boat/reading-a-chart.html) and
[Buoys and beacons (IALA A and B)](../run-the-boat/iala-buoyage.html).
